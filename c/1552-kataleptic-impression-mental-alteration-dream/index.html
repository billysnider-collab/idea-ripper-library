Perception, again, is an impression produced on the mind, its name being appropriately borrowed from impressions on wax made by a seal; and perception they divide into comprehensible and incomprehensible: Comprehensible, which they call the criterion of facts, and which is produced by a real object, and is, therefore, at the same time conformable to that object; Incomprehensible, which has no relation to any real object, or else, if it has any such relation, does not correspond to it, being but a vague and indistinct representation... But there is a difference between φαντασία and φάντασμα. For φάντασμα is a conception of the intellect, such as takes place in sleep; but φαντασία is an impression, τύπωσις, produced on the mind, that is to say, an alteration, ἀλλοίωσις, as Chrysippus states in the twelfth book of his treatise on the Soul... But φαντασία is understood to be that which is impressed, and formed, and imprinted by a real object, according to a real object, in such a way as it could not be by any other than a real object... — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives*, VII.35–36 (tr. [Why it matters]

The Stoics distinguish between a *phantasma* (an ungrounded mental figment, as in dreams) and a *phantasia* (a real physical alteration of the soul), making the "comprehensible" (*kataleptic*) impression—stamped unmistakably by a real object—the sole criterion of truth. Stoic epistemology is grounded in physical contact. Because the soul is a corporeal breath (*pneuma*), perceiving an external object is a literal physical impact upon the soul's ruling center (*hegemonikon*). Zeno originally described this impact as a seal-impression (*typosis*) in wax, which Cleanthes took literally as physical indentations; Chrysippus refined it into "alteration" (*alloiosis*) so the same corporeal mind could hold multiple simultaneous modifications without overwriting itself like soft wax. Against Academic Skeptics who argued that no perception can guarantee its own truth, the Stoics posit the "comprehensible" (*kataleptike*) impression: a presentation stamped by an existing object, in strict accordance with that object, carrying a clarity so specific it could not come from anything non-existent. By contrast, a *phantasma* is an internal, objectless projection of the mind in sleep or madness. Wisdom begins in policing the border between physical alteration by reality and self-generated shadow. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the Neoplatonic and Renaissance imagination—where *phantasia* (*imaginatio*) becomes the crucial astral interface between body and intellect—this Stoic distinction between *phantasia* and *phantasma* was read as a science of spiritual discernment. The corporeal soul is a living mirror or pneumatic medium continually modified (*alloiosis*) by the rays and signatures of real cosmic beings. When the mirror is clean and tense, reality stamps it with a self-verifying seal; when the mirror is slack or fevered by melancholy, it breeds *phantasmata*—autonomous spectral larvae and dream-idols that mimic real objects and trap the soul's assent. Chrysippus's correction of "seal-in-wax" to "alteration of the pneuma" anticipates the Renaissance pneumatic theories in which the *spiritus* does not merely take static dents, but vibrates and transforms into the exact qualitative frequency of the object it contemplates.
