Card #c945IR // #c945Synthesis

Arseny has the gift of healing, and the elder tells him bluntly: 'I won't spare you: you are guilty of her bodily death.' His gift saved everyone around him — the plague-stricken, the wounded, the dying — and it could not save the one person it was for. The mechanism: you become great at the thing precisely because it is useless for your one real wound. Competence as penance. The gift strengthens the more he sacrifices, but it is aimed at everyone except the one loss that matters. Ripped from the novel (translated from the Russian by the cutter): «Не буду тебя жалеть: ты виноват в ее смерти телесной.» — "I won't spare you: you are guilty of her bodily death."

— In our words · Source: Laurus (Eugene Vodolazkin)

[Use when]

Use when you are becoming extremely good at something while the actual thing you wanted stays broken. Ask: is this excellence, or is this the wound learning to work around itself?

[Why it matters]

This is the tragic motor of the whole book. The healing gift is not a consolation for Ustina's death; it is the wound's byproduct. It grows precisely where it cannot help her. What looks like virtue — endless service, endless sacrifice — is grief doing the only work it can find.
