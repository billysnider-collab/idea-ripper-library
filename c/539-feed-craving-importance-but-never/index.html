Card #c539IR // #c539Synthesis

There is one burning desire shared by every person you'll ever meet: "the desire to be important," which Carnegie calls "a gnawing and unfaltering human hunger." The winning move is "honest and sincere appreciation," and the losing move is flattery: "One comes from the heart out; the other comes from the teeth out. One is unselfish; the other selfish. One is universally admired; the other universally condemned." He sharpens the difference — "Flattery is telling the other person precisely what he thinks about himself" — and gives the rule behind it: "talk to people about themselves and they will listen for hours."

— In our words · Source: How to Win Friends and Influence People (Dale Carnegie)

[Use when]

You need to win someone over — find something genuinely admirable about them and say it, instead of praising yourself.

[Why it matters]

Most people try to impress others when they should be attending to others. This rip flips the polarity: the person who satisfies the importance-craving without faking it gets liked, trusted, and listened to — the person who flatters gets detected and discounted.
