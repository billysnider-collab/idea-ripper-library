Card #c419IR // #c419Synthesis

Your goal isn't to "understand the story." Your goal is to figure out what Severian is accidentally telling you. And you don't get to assume he's telling the truth. Keep three columns as you read. WHAT HAPPENED: what Severian explicitly says happened. WHAT I THINK HAPPENED: your interpretation. WHAT'S WEIRD: anything that doesn't quite fit. Don't solve it immediately. That's the fun. Wolfe hid the instructions inside the book itself: the brown book says "everything, whatever happens, has three meanings. The first is its practical meaning, what the book calls, 'the thing the plowman sees.'"

— In our words · Source: The Book of the New Sun (Gene Wolfe)

[Use when]

Use when reading any narrator who insists on his own honesty.

[Why it matters]

The narrator warns you in sentence one that certainty is off the table, then the brown book hands you the method: three meanings per thing, three columns per scene. The game isn't fan theory — it's the intended interface. Severian never lies about what he saw; he just never does the arithmetic. That's your job.
