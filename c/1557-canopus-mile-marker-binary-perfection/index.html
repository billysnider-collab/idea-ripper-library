And they lay down the position that all offences are equal, as Chrysippus argues in the fourth book of his Ethic Questions, and so say Persæus and Zeno. For if one thing that is true is not more true than another thing that is true, neither is one thing that is false more false than another thing that is false; so too, one deceit is not greater than another, nor one sin than another. For the man who is a hundred furlongs from Canopus, and the man who is only one, are both equally not in Canopus; and so too, he who commits a greater sin, and he who commits a less, are both equally not in the right path... And they say, that not only are the wise free, but that they are also kings, since kingly power is an irresponsible dominion, which can only exist in the case of the wise man... Another doctrine of the Stoics is, that there is nothing intermediate between virtue and vice... For the Stoics say, that as a stick must be either straight or crooked, so a man must be either just or unjust, and cannot be more just than just, or more unjust than unjust; and that the same rule applies to all cases. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives*, VII.64–65 (tr. Yonge, 1853) **Surface Gloss:** Because virtue is an all-or-nothing structural perfection like a straight stick or being inside the city of Canopus, all sins are equally outside the mark, and only the wise man is truly free, sane, and a king. Know when a problem is governed by **analog progress** (getting closer to Canopus matters) versus **binary threshold** (being *inside* Canopus, or a stick being straight). In engineering, security, and personal integrity, a bridge that is 99% structurally sound or a cryptographic key that is 99% secure is 100% compromised. Use the Canopus test to kill moral complacency: *"Closer to the gate is good for the march, but until we are inside the wall, we are still outside in the desert."*

[Use when]

Enforcing zero-defect standards in safety, security, or personal ethics where "mostly straight" or "almost inside the city" still produces total system failure. ---

[Why it matters]

Because virtue is an all-or-nothing structural perfection like a straight stick or being inside the city of Canopus, all sins are equally outside the mark, and only the wise man is truly free, sane, and a king. This cluster contains the famous *Paradoxa Stoicorum*—the counter-intuitive conclusions that follow from Stoic definitions. Against the Peripatetics, who viewed virtue as a gradual spectrum of habituation, the Stoics define virtue as a structural *disposition* (*diathesis*) of the soul's tension—like the straightness of a stick, the tuning of a lyre, or the geometry of an arch (VII.54). A stick with a one-degree bend and a stick with a ninety-degree bend are both crooked; a traveler one furlong outside the gates of Canopus and a traveler a hundred furlongs away are both equally "not in Canopus." Moreover, the virtues are reciprocally entailed (*antakolouthia*, VII.65): whoever has one has all, because prudence, courage, justice, and temperance are simply the same perfected *logos* applied to four domains (choice, endurance, distribution, and restraint). Because only the sage possesses this unshakeable cognitive sovereignty, he alone holds "unaccountable rule" (kingship) and true freedom, while all fools are technically madmen and slaves. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the history of esotericism, the Stoic Sage—who is simultaneously the only true Priest, Prophet, Poet, Rich Man, and King (VII.64)—is the philosophical ancestor of the Hermetic *Magus* and the Rosicrucian adept. The Canopus analogy was read alongside another famous Stoic image (preserved in Plutarch and Cicero): a man drowning one inch below the surface of the water drowns just as surely as a man at the bottom of the Aegean, yet if he rises that final inch into the air, he breathes all at once. Spiritual awakening is not a quantitative accumulation of bourgeois niceness; it is a sudden, total phase-shift from the sublunary water of *doxa* into the upper air of *Logos*. Once that threshold is crossed, the adept possesses all virtues simultaneously as a single diamond-like state of consciousness, exercising invisible kingship over a world of sleepwalking kings.
