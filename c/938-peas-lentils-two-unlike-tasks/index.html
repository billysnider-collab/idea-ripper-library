Card #c938IR // #c938Synthesis

The engine is a sorting rule, not a passion. Employment, rank, gold, and craft are collapsed into one class: motion performed for the opinion of others. Shelling peas and counting lentils are declared identical, so no second channel can outrank the licensed one on the evidence. Books are refused on the same rule — guidance would be a rival heat. The sort is what makes the monopoly look like a discovery. Ripped from the novel: "Am I not sufficiently employed? And is it not in reality the same, whether I shell peas or count lentils? The world runs on from one folly to another; and the man who, solely from regard to the opinion of others, and without any wish or necessity of his own, toils after gold, honour, or any other phantom, is no better than a fool."

— In our words · Source: The Sorrows of Young Werther (Johann Wolfgang von Goethe)

[Use when]

Two unlike tasks — a chore and a career, a walk and a craft — have started to feel like the same worthless gesture, and only one name still makes either of them matter.

[Why it matters]

A living reader uses this whenever a job, a boring repetition, or someone else's competence is felt as an insult to the one thing that counts. The dark edge: the sort also deletes the channels that could have paid out in a currency the licensed object never will. You do not lose the alternative. You un-price it.
