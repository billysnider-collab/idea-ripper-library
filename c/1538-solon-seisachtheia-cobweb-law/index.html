[Why it matters]

Solon abolishes debt-slavery by forgiving his own seven-talent inheritance first (*Seisachtheia*), warns that written laws are cobwebs that catch the weak while the powerful tear through them, and grounds justice in third-party civic indignation. Solon's political philosophy in Diogenes is a masterclass in structural statecraft and its limits. First, the *Seisachtheia* ("shaking off of burdens") permanently removes the human body from the collateral market: citizens may no longer pledge their own flesh for loans, preventing capital accumulation from cannibalizing the citizen body into chattel slavery. Crucially, Solon establishes skin-in-the-game legitimacy by burning his own seven-talent receivable before demanding creditor compliance. Second, in Section VII, he re-engineers civic prestige by slashing state payouts to Olympic athletes (who are expensive ornaments) to fund the public education of war-orphans (who took lethal civic risk). Yet Solon harbors zero illusions about legal formalism: written laws on wooden tablets (*axones*) are "cobwebs" (*arachnia*)—mechanically strong enough to trap petty offenders, but structurally incapable of restraining oligarchs or aspiring tyrants like Pisistratus unless uninjured bystanders treat an attack on any citizen as an attack on themselves. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the esoteric and Platonic reception of Solon (who serves in the Platonic tradition as the receiver of the Egyptian priests' Atlantis lore, echoed in Thales's letter in Book I, Thales XV), the lawgiver is an earthly demiurge attempting to stamp harmonic proportion (*dikē*) onto the unruly flux of the *polis*. The *Seisachtheia* is read allegorically as the liberation of the rational soul from its "debt-bondage" to the corporeal passions—untying the inner citizen from the mortgage of the body. The "cobweb" apophthegm exposes the tragedy of all exterior, written law (*nomos*) when divorced from interior virtue: without a shared pneumatic bond of sympathy among the citizens (where the uninjured feel the wound of the injured, just as the organs of a single cosmic body share pain), the wooden tablets of the law are dead letter, easily shattered by the titanic force of ambition.
