Still it is given thee to fight and overcome Thy foolish passions: learn thou to subdue them (7). Be sober, diligent, and chaste; avoid all wrath. In public or in secret ne'er permit thou Any evil; and above all else respect thyself (8). — *The Golden Verses of Pythagoras*, Purification, trans. Nayan Louise Redfield (1917), p. 115. **Surface Gloss:** Train yourself to conquer appetite, laziness, lust, and anger. Never commit a shameful act whether watched by others or completely alone; make your own internal sense of shame and self-respect your highest judge. **The Internal Witness Standard:** External accountability (laws, bosses, public shame) fails the moment the lights go out or you gain enough power to hide your tracks. Replace external policing with **Ontological Self-Respect**: treat your own consciousness as the highest-ranking observer in the room, someone before whom you refuse to embarrass yourself even in total solitude.

[Why it matters]

Train yourself to conquer appetite, laziness, lust, and anger. Never commit a shameful act whether watched by others or completely alone; make your own internal sense of shame and self-respect your highest judge. The Greek text on p. 114 specifies four somatic-emotional drives the initiate must habituate himself to rule (*kratein*): the belly (*gastros*, rendered "sober"), sleep (*hypnou*, rendered "diligent"), lechery (*lagneiēs*, rendered "chaste"), and boiling rage (*thymou*, "wrath"). In Pythagorean psychology, the soul is a tripartite or bipartite harmony where the rational principle must govern the irrational impulses of the body. If these four leaks are left unsealed, the soul's energy is dissipated into matter. The climax of this section—"above all else respect thyself" (*pantōn de malista aischyneo sauton*, literally "feel shame before yourself most of all")—shifts moral enforcement from external Greek *timē* (public reputation) to an internal panopticon. Because the initiate's own rational soul is a fragment of divine proportion, acting basely in secret violates the sacred witness inside one's own breast. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In the Hermetic-Cabalistic synthesis of the Renaissance, the four passions listed here were mapped onto the four elemental corruptions of the lower astral body: gluttony (Earth), slothful sleep (Water), lust (Air/humid heat), and wrath (Fire). Until the magus rectifies these four elemental imbalances, his *ochēma* (the subtle star-vehicle of the soul) remains heavy and earthbound. The command to "respect thyself" was read through the lens of the indwelling *daimon* or divine spark (*scintilla*). For Ficino and Agrippa, to sin in secret is to pollute the temple in which the tutelary spirit resides; true self-reverence is not egoic pride, but awe before the immortal divine image stamped upon the human *mens*.
