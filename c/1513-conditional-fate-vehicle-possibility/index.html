All things he says are in Fate, but all things are not however fated. For Fate, while holding the rank of a law, does not, as it were, say that one person shall do this, and another suffer that; for it would proceed to infinity; since the things produced are infinite, and infinite too the accidents around them; moreover that, which is in our power, would depart, and praise too and blame, and every thing (else) that borders on them; but (it says) that if a soul selects a life of this kind, and does some such acts, some such things will follow it. The soul then is without a master, and it rests with itself to do or not an act; nor is it forced to do this (or that). But that, which follows upon the doing, will be accomplished according to Fate. ... Now the nature of a possibility falls somehow in the middle between the true and the false; but that, which rests on ourselves, is borne, as it were, on a vehicle, upon that (possibility) which is naturally indefinite. ... But possibility is neither of these; while that, which rests on ourselves, being indefinite, receives, according to the balance either way, the truth or not. — Alcinous, *Handbook of Platonism*, Ch. 26, trans. George Burges (1854), pp. 291–293. *Surface Gloss:* Everything is inside Fate's jurisdiction, yet individual actions are not predetermined. Fate acts like a conditional statute ("If you choose X, consequence Y is fated"). Free will rides like a charioteer atop indefinite possibility, tipping the scale to make an outcome actually true or false. **Jurisdictional Determinism (The If-Then Universe):** Stop arguing over whether the world is 100% rigged or 100% free. The environment is a statute book, not a script: "all things are in Fate, but not all things are fated." You have total sovereignty over which door you walk through (the antecedent), and zero sovereignty over the physics of the room on the other side (the consequent).

[Why it matters]

To defend Platonic moral responsibility ("virtue is without a master," *Republic* 617e) against Stoic causal determinism, Alcinous constructs the classic Middle Platonic doctrine of **Conditional Fate** (*ex hypotheseos heimarmene*). If Fate predetermined every particular act of every infinite individual, cosmic law would be an unmanageable infinity and moral praise or blame would be abolished. Instead, Fate has the status of civil law (*nomou taxin*): a law does not force a citizen to commit murder or perform bravery, but it rigidly decrees the penalty or reward *once the citizen acts*. The antecedent choice is unmastered (*adespoton*) and "up to us" (*eph' hemin*); the consequent chain (Paris abducts Helen $\rightarrow$ Greeks wage war; Laius begets a son $\rightarrow$ the son kills him) is locked in by Fate. To ground this logically, Alcinous distinguishes three Aristotelian levels—bare **Possibility** (*dynamis*, a boy's unformed fitness to learn carpentry), acquired **Habit** (*hexis*, knowing the trade), and **Active Operation** (*energeia*, building). Bare possibility hangs in an indeterminate middle zone between true and false, and human free will rides upon this indeterminacy "as on a vehicle" (*hosper epocheitai*), tipping the balance. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Burges (1854) notes that Ficino rendered the vehicle image as *eique suapte natura indefinito libera animae rationalis potestas undique superfertur* ("and over that, indefinite by its own nature, the free power of the rational soul is borne on all sides")—a phrasing that intentionally echoes the Spirit of God moving over the face of the unformed waters in *Genesis* 1:2. In the Renaissance astrological and magical debates (Ficino's *Disputatio contra iudicium astrologorum* and Pico della Mirandola's *Oration*), Alcinous's chapter was a primary weapon against fatalistic judicial astrology. The stars (the instruments of Fate) do not compel the human soul's choices; rather, the heavens are a gigantic matrix of *if-then* algorithms. Because the rational soul is "without a master," it hovers over the indefinite field of sublunary possibility like a sovereign rider on a chariot. Only when the soul voluntarily steps into a specific frequency of action does the planetary machinery of Fate lock onto it and execute the karmic consequence.
