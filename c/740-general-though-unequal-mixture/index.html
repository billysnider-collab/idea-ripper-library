in England it was not so; among the English, she believed, in their hearts and habits, there was a general though unequal mixture of good and bad.

— In our words · Source: Northanger Abbey (Jane Austen)

[Use when]

Use when tempted to sort people into villains and saints. Look for the mixture.

[Why it matters]

Catherine's mature moral epistemology, earned the hard way: no angels, no fiends, only mixtures in unequal proportions. The Gothic novel flattens people into spotless or fiendish; reality mixes.
