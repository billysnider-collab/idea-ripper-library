[Use when]

[Why it matters]

Father Zeus could free all humans from their misery simply by showing them what kind of *daimon* they are using; yet humans must take courage themselves, because their lineage is divine and sacred Nature reveals everything to them. Here d'Olivet's French (*Mais non: c'est aux humains...*) slightly reshapes the facing Greek (p. 120) while preserving its core tension. In the Greek, the poet cries out: *Zeu pater* ("Father Zeus, either you would free all men from many evils, or show to all what sort of *daimon* they employ!"). Immediately, the poet answers his own prayer: *Alla sy tharsei, epei theion genos esti brotoisin* ("But take courage yourself! For mortals are of a divine race, to whom sacred Nature brings forth and reveals each thing"). In the Pythagorean system, the *daimon* is both the indwelling rational soul-principle and the guardian allotment of a human life. Zeus does not magically override human blindness because humans already possess **ontological kinship with the divine** (*theion genos*), and *hierē physis* (sacred Nature) is an open book continuously displaying her ratios to anyone willing to do the purifying work. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] For Marsilio Ficino and Pico della Mirandola, this passage was the jewel of pagan theology: the affirmation of human *theosis* through knowledge and will. The Greek phrase "what sort of *daimon* they employ (*hoiōi tōi daimoni chrōntai*)" was read through Plato's *Timaeus* (90a), where God gives each person the sovereign part of the soul as their *daimon*. Mortals suffer only because they do not realize they are operating a divine vehicle. Furthermore, "Sacred Nature reveals each thing" grounded the Renaissance doctrine of the **Book of Nature**: the physical cosmos is a living theophany of divine signatures waiting to be read by the awakened child of God.
