Card #c509IR // #c509Synthesis

Humans and computers are categorically different — people have intentionality and judgment; computers do efficient data processing. The premise that computers will replace humans is wrong: "computers are complements for humans, not substitutes," and "computers are tools, not rivals." The valuable businesses ask "how can computers help humans solve hard problems?" instead of "what problems can be solved with computers alone?" And the big-data mystique gets deflated in one line: "big data is usually dumb data" — patterns without human interpretation are just patterns.

— In our words · Source: Zero to One (Peter Thiel with Blake Masters)

[Use when]

Someone frames AI as replacing workers instead of empowering them; designing human-in-the-loop systems.

[Why it matters]

The PayPal fraud story is the proof: pure automation failed against adaptive fraudsters, pure human review couldn't scale — the hybrid ("Igor") turned a $29.3M quarterly loss into profit. Gains from pairing humans and computers dwarf gains from pitting them against each other. It's the strongest counter-argument to the replacement narrative in the whole book.
