Card #c1004IR // #c1004Synthesis

Angleton’s doctrine: “It was the paper record—the ‘legend’—that mattered most.” Under him “a person’s file sometimes took on a life of its own, full of actions and dialogue that bore no relation to the subject’s real life.” The file becomes the person; reality is whatever the paperwork says.

— In our words · Source: The Devil's Chessboard (David Talbot)

[Use when]

You need someone to be something. Don’t change him; change his file.

[Why it matters]

Whoever controls the file controls the person. The subject cannot argue with paperwork he cannot see.
