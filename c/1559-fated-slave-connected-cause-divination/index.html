They say that he was once scourging a slave whom he had detected in theft; and when he said to him, 'It was fated that I should steal;' he rejoined, 'Yes, and that you should be beaten.' ... But Chrysippus, in his treatise on Fate, and Posidonius, in the second book of his work on Fate, and Zeno, and Boethus, in the eleventh book of his treatise on Fate, say, that all things are produced by fate. And fate, (εἱμαρμένη), is a connected (εἰρομένη) cause of existing things, or the reason according to which the world is regulated. They also say that divination has a universal existence, since Providence has; and they define it as an act on account of certain results, as Zeno and Chrysippus, in the second book of his treatise on Divination, and Athenodorus and Posidonius... all agree in saying... They also say that there are some Dæmones, who have a sympathy with mankind, being surveyors of all human affairs; and that there are heroes, which are the souls of virtuous men, which have left their bodies. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives*, VII.19, 74–75, 79 (tr. [Why it matters]

Zeno's famous retort to the thieving slave ("fated to steal, and fated to be beaten") illustrates Stoic determinism: Fate is an unbroken chain of causes (*heimarmene*) that grounds both moral response and cosmic divination across a sympathetic universe overseen by *daimones*. Because the Stoic cosmos is a single compact organism with no internal void ("there is no such thing as a vacuum in the world, but that it is all closely united and compact; for that this condition is necessarily brought about by the concord and harmony [*sympatheia*] which exist between the heavenly bodies and those of the earth," VII.70), every event is locked into **Fate** (*heimarmene*), etymologized as a "strung-together chain of causes" (*eiromene aitia*). Critics attacked this with the "Lazy Argument" (*argos logos*): if everything is fated, why act or punish? Zeno's whip-crack in VII.19 is the vernacular version of Chrysippus's doctrine of "co-fated" (*confatalia*) events: the theft and the beating are linked links on the same causal chain, mediated through the agents' own internal dispositions. This same cosmic compactness validates **divination** (*mantike*) and the existence of **Dæmones**—not evil spirits, but ontological intermediaries and departed heroic souls who survey human affairs through the physical medium of cosmic sympathy. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** This cluster is the primary classical engine for the Renaissance science of natural magic, astrology, and divination in Ficino's *De Vita* and Agrippa's *De Occulta Philosophia*. If the cosmos has no internal vacuum and is strung together by a single pneumatic tension (*sympatheia*) from the fixed stars down to terrestrial stones and human livers, then divination is not supernatural miracle-mongering; it is reading the vibrations of a single taut spiderweb. Within this web, the pagan *daimones* (strictly ontological intermediaries in the air and æther, along with the surviving pneumatic bodies of virtuous *heroes*) act as cosmic relay-stations, transmitting providential signs and dreams to the human soul. Even Zeno's joke to the slave carries an esoteric edge: you cannot invoke cosmic determinism to excuse your lower nature while exempting yourself from the karmic counter-stroke built into the same causal string.
