“33. “Natural justice is a covenant of what is suitable, leading men to avoid injuring one another, and being injured. [...] 35. “Justice has no independent existence; it results from mutual contracts, and establishes itself wherever there is a mutual engagement to guard against doing or sustaining mutual injury. 36. “Injustice is not intrinsically bad; it has this character only because there is joined with it a fear of not escaping those who are appointed to punish actions marked with that character. 37. “It is not possible for a man who secretly does anything in contravention of the agreement which men have made with one another, to guard against doing, or sustaining mutual injury, to believe that he shall always escape notice, even if he have escaped notice already ten thousand times; for, till his death, it is uncertain whether he will not be detected. XXXI, Maxims 33, 35–37, 41, trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Justice is not an eternal entity; it is a local non-aggression treaty. Breaking it in secret is foolish only because lifelong fear of exposure ruins peace of mind, and any law stops being just the exact moment it stops being useful. Two linked mechanisms: 1. **The Sunset Clause of Rules (Maxim 41):** Never defend a legacy policy, process, or norm by claiming it was "always wrong" or "always right." Acknowledge that it *was* valid when it served mutual utility, and declare it dead the exact second environmental shifts erase that utility. 2. **The Gyges Paranoia Tax (Maxim 37):** Never take a covert shortcut whose value depends on permanent non-detection. Even a 99.9% chance of staying hidden charges an ongoing cognitive tax ("will today be the day?") that costs more than the illicit gain.

[Why it matters]

Justice is not an eternal entity; it is a local non-aggression treaty. Breaking it in secret is foolish only because lifelong fear of exposure ruins peace of mind, and any law stops being just the exact moment it stops being useful. Here Epicurus demolishes both the Platonic Form of Justice and the Stoic doctrine of eternal Natural Law (*nomos physeos*). In an atomic universe without providential teleology, "justice has no independent existence" (*ouk en ti kath' heauto dikaiosyne*). It is strictly a *syntheke* — a pragmatic non-aggression compact negotiated between rational agents to prevent mutual harm. Epicurus then answers Plato's famous Ring of Gyges challenge (why obey the compact if you can break it invisibly?) without appealing to afterlife punishment or mystical soul-corruption (Maxim 36–37): even if a secret treaty-breaker escapes detection ten thousand times, he cannot achieve *ataraxia* because up to his dying breath he carries the epistemic uncertainty of future exposure. Thus, the penalty of injustice is immediate internal paranoia. Conversely, because law derives its validity solely from mutual utility (*to sympheron*), statutes have a built-in expiration date: when historical circumstances shift and an old law loses utility, its moral authority evaporates instantly (Maxim 41). [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] To the Renaissance Platonic-Hermetic tradition, Maxims 33–41 were the most scandalously anti-metaphysical propositions in antiquity, directly denying the cosmic hierarchy in which human law mirrors divine Archetypes. Yet Maxim 37's internal punishment mechanism — that the secret criminal is tortured by his own inescapable dread of exposure — was eagerly assimilated by Renaissance emblematists and humanist commentators as a naturalistic confirmation of the *conscientia* (conscience) doctrine: even if you strip away the Furies and subterranean judges of myth, the human mind acts as its own inescapable inquisitor. Meanwhile, the radical temporality of Maxim 41 — that a law was genuinely just yesterday when useful, and becomes genuinely unjust today when circumstances change — went underground into Renaissance civic realism (Machiavelli, Guicciardini, and later Gassendi and Hobbes), providing the hidden blueprint for modern secular social-contract theory.
