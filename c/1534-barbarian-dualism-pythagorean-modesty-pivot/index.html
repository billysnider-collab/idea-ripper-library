[Why it matters]

Diogenes records the Persian Magian doctrine of two opposing cosmic principles (Oromasdes and Arimanius) and bodily resurrection, then contrasts priestly *sophia* with Pythagoras's invention of *philosophia*—naming humans lovers of wisdom because wisdom itself belongs only to God. As a third-century compiler organizing the history of Greek thought, Diogenes uses the Proem to draw a hard jurisdictional line between barbarian priestly caste-knowledge and Greek philosophy. Still, his Peripatetic sources (Aristotle, Theopompus, Eudoxus) preserve a precise structural model of Persian Magianism: a cosmic dualism governed by two primary *daimones*—ontological principles or governing powers, not later devils—identified by Greek equivalents as Zeus/Oromasdes (generative light/order) and Hades/Arimanius (chthonic darkness/dissolution), paired with a cyclical eschatology in which mortal beings return under their existing identities. Against both barbarian priestly omniscience and the older Greek title of *sophos* (master-sage), Diogenes places Pythagoras's linguistic pivot: renaming the practitioner a *philosophos* ("lover of wisdom"). Structurally, this shifts wisdom from a static possession held by an elite caste into a directional desire—an asymptotic pursuit defined by the ontological gap between mortal mind and divine perfection. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In Renaissance reception, this very passage becomes the cornerstone for Marsilio Ficino and Giovanni Pico della Mirandola's *prisca theologia*—the exactly opposite conclusion to Diogenes's Greek chauvinism. Where Diogenes cites Zoroaster and the Magi to dismiss barbarian claims, Ficino reads the succession from Zoroaster, Ostanes, and the Egyptian Hermes (Proem II, VI–VII) as proof of a single, primordial revelation preceding Moses and Plato. Oromasdes and Arimanius are read by Renaissance Platonists not as crude polytheism, but as the metaphysical tension between the intelligible Form (light) and privative Matter (shadow), while the Magian "phantoms" gliding into clear sight (Proem VI) are harmonized with the neoplatonic doctrine of aerial *daimones* and astral images. Pythagoras's refusal of the title *sophos* becomes, in this tradition, the initiatory humility required before the soul can ascend back to the One.
