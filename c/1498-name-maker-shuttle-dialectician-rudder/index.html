Plato inquires there whether names are from nature or imposition ; and he is satisfied that the correctness of names is referable to imposition; not however simply so, nor accidentally, but so that the imposition follows upon the nature of the thing; for the correctness of the name is nothing else than the imposition, which agrees with the nature of the thing... Hence he would be the best name-imposer, who should mark out by the name the nature of the thing. For the name is an instrument of a thing, not such as occurs accidentally, but has a mutual relation by nature; and through it we teach each other the things, and we judge of them ; so that the name is something with a teaching, and the instrument, that judges of the existence of each thing, as the shuttle is of weaving... For it is the part of an artificer to make a rudder, but of the steersman to make use of it properly. So too the name-imposer himself would make a proper use of the imposition, if he were to make the imposition in the presence of the Dialectician, who knows the nature of the things that are the subject (of the names.) — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Ch. 6, trans. [Why it matters]

Words are neither purely arbitrary labels nor automatic natural cries; they are deliberate human conventions forged to match the real nature of things. Like a shuttle or a rudder, a word is a separating tool best forged under the supervision of the Dialectician who actually uses it. Alcinous resolves the ancient Greek debate over language—whether words signify by *nature* (*phusei*) or by *convention/imposition* (*thesei*)—by synthesizing both poles inside the craft analogy of the *Cratylus*. Speaking is a species of action, and no action succeeds if done at random; it must conform to the structure of the materials it acts upon. Therefore, a name is a deliberate human imposition (*thesis*), yet its *correctness* depends entirely on whether the lawgiver stamps the vocal sound according to the actual nature (*phusis*) of the object. Functionally, a name is a tool (*organon*) that does two jobs: it teaches, and it separates/judges essences, just as a weaver’s shuttle separates the warp and woof threads. Because the user of a tool understands its required performance better than the mere carpenter, the Name-Imposer can only coin accurate terminology if supervised by the Dialectician, who knows the real divisions of being. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the Renaissance reception, this passage sits at the heart of the doctrine of Adamic and Orphic names. Burges (1854) notes that Ficino supplies *"quae sub nominibus latent"* ("the things that lie hidden beneath the names"). For Ficino and the Christian Cabalists who read him, the "Name-Imposer" (*nomothetes*) working in the presence of the Supreme Dialectician is Moses, Orpheus, or Adam naming the creatures according to their essential celestial signatures. Because a true name is not an arbitrary token ("not such as occurs accidentally," or as Ficino writes, *"non temere"*) but possesses a natural affinity and power (*vis*) linked to the essence of the thing, words used properly by the wise can directly move and separate realities. Yet Ficino preserves Alcinous’s crucial containment rule: the power lies not in the raw vocal sound (*prima vocis emissio*) by itself, but in the wedding of rational imposition to essential nature.
