[Why it matters]

The soul is a warm, eight-part corporeal breath (*pneuma*) centered in the heart; in vision, it projects a tense cone of luminous air that touches the object "like a staff," and in the wise, it grows strong enough to survive bodily death until the cosmic conflagration. Stoic psychology is a physics of pneumatic tension (*tonos*). The soul is a warm, connate breath (*pneuma symphyton*) divided into eight physical streams: the central ruling faculty (*hegemonikon*) housed in the heart (VII.86), plus seven pneumatic tentacles extending from the heart to the five sense organs, the vocal tract, and the reproductive organs (resembling an octopus). Because sensation is physical contact, Chrysippus explains vision not as passive reception of atomic films (against Epicurus), but as an active **tensional cone**: the visual *pneuma* shoots from the pupil into the surrounding illuminated air, tensing a cone of air whose apex is at the eye and whose base presses against the visible object, reporting its shape back to the heart exactly as a blind man feels an object with the tip of a walking-staff (*bakulon*). This same doctrine of pneumatic tension explains Chrysippus's Conditional Immortality: while Cleanthes held that all souls survive until the cosmic conflagration (*ekpyrosis*), Chrysippus argued that only the souls of the wise have cultivated enough internal *tonos* (structural cohesion) to hold together after leaving the body's frame; foolish souls, slack and鬆-strung by passions, dissipate swiftly in the upper air. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** Two profound esoteric doctrines converge here. First, Chrysippus's **Visual Staff**—seeing as active tactile projection of luminous *pneuma*—became the physical foundation for Renaissance theories of fascination, the evil eye, and the mage's gaze (as in Ficino and Bruno): to look at something with concentrated will is literally to touch and strike it with a cone of vital spirit projected from the heart. Second, Chrysippus's split with Cleanthes over posthumous survival is the exact classical formula of **esoteric conditional immortality**: the soul is not automatically immortal by a participation in static Platonic Being; it is a subtle pneumatic body (*ochema*) whose post-mortem coherence depends on the *tonos* (alchemical density and self-consistency) forged through philosophical practice during life. Virtue literally tempers the subtle body so it does not dissolve upon death, rising to the sub-lunar hero-sphere (VII.79) until the Great Year ends.
