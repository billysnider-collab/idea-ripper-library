Even as Truth, does Error have its lovers: With prudence the Philosopher approves or blames; If Error triumph, he departs and waits (13). — *The Golden Verses of Pythagoras*, Purification, trans. Nayan Louise Redfield (1917), p. 116. **Surface Gloss:** Falsehood attracts just as many passionate partisans as truth. The wise person judges discourses cautiously, and when collective delusion wins the day, steps back silently and waits it out. **Tactical Epistemic Retreat:** Falsehood has a massive, passionate fanbase. When a room, organization, or market is gripped by a triumphant delusion, do not waste your capital fighting a frontal war of words against a manic crowd. State your assessment prudently, then step off the tracks, protect your reserves, and wait for reality to bankrupt the error.

— In our words · Source: The Golden Verses of Pythagoras

[Use when]

Navigating corporate groupthink, ideological mob panics, speculative market bubbles, or futile public arguments where data no longer matters. ---

[Why it matters]

Falsehood attracts just as many passionate partisans as truth. The wise person judges discourses cautiously, and when collective delusion wins the day, steps back silently and waits it out. In the Greek original (p. 116), the poet observes that many *logoi* (discourses, arguments, public opinions)—both base (*deiloi*) and noble (*esthloi*)—strike against human ears like projectiles. The initiate must neither be stunned/intimidated by them nor allow himself to be bullied out of his position. When *pseudos* (falsehood/Error) is spoken and captures the crowd, the Greek instructs: *praōs eik'* ("yield gently" or step aside with mildness), which d'Olivet and Redfield expand into the striking tactical image: "If Error triumph, he departs and waits." Historically, the early Pythagorean communities in Magna Graecia faced violent populist uprisings (such as the Cylonian revolt in Croton). The system recognizes that truth cannot be forced into an unready, impassioned mob; when collective mania peaks, frontal debate is suicide. Tactical retreat preserves the seed of *logos* until the fever breaks. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] This verse became the foundational justification for the **Discipline of the Arcane** (*disciplina arcani*) across the esoteric tradition. From the ancient Pythagorean *akousmatikoi* silence through the Renaissance academies of Florence, initiates held that sacred truth has a dual nature: unveiled to the wise, it illuminates; thrown before the profane "lovers of Error," it provokes frenzy and desecration. When the outer world falls into a cycle of iron-age delusion, the adept does not martyr himself in public polemics; he withdraws into the sanctuary, encodes the teaching in symbol and cipher, and waits for the cosmic season to turn.
