Now since Mind is superior to the living principle, and as the Mind, which is, according to its working, thinking upon all things simultaneously and for ever, is superior to a mind in posse ; and as the cause of that is better than this, and what exists is still above these, this would be the primary god, as being the cause of perpetually operating for the mind of the whole heaven. Now he is operating, although unmoved himself, for that (mind), as the sun does for vision, when it looks for him, and as that, which has the faculty of desire, excites desire, itself being unmoved. At least in this way will this Mind likewise excite the mind of the whole heaven. Now, since the primary Mind is the most beautiful, there must needs be the most beautiful thing placed under it. But nothing is more beautiful than itself. It would therefore be thinking for ever upon itself and its own cogitations; and this its mental energy is Idea. — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Ch. 10, trans. Like a sun or a north-star specification, hold your position with such clarity, completeness, and self-consistency that peripheral actors must orient themselves toward you simply to see and function.

[Why it matters]

Ascending up the chain of value—from soul to potential mind, to active mind thinking all things at once, to its ultimate cause—we reach the Primary God. Unmoved himself, he activates the cosmic mind just as the sun activates sight or a beloved object stirs desire, eternally thinking himself and his own Ideas. To articulate the Third Principle—the "almost untellable" Primary God—Alcinous constructs an ontological hierarchy that fuses Plato’s Good (the Sun of the *Republic*) and Demiurge (*Timaeus*) with Aristotle’s Unmoved Mover (*Metaphysics* Lambda). He climbs by degrees of perfection: 1. *Living Principle (Soul)* is surpassed by *Mind*. 2. *Mind in posse* (potential intellect, which moves from ignorance to thought) is surpassed by *Mind in working* (actual intellect thinking all things simultaneously and forever). 3. Above the active Mind of the Whole Heaven stands its ultimate Cause: the **Primary God / Primary Mind**. How does this supreme God govern the cosmos without suffering the change, fatigue, or division of physical labor? By *unmoved attraction and illumination*. Just as the sun activates the eye when the eye turns toward it, and just as an object of desire sets the lover in motion while remaining utterly still itself, the Primary God awakens and orders the Mind of Heaven. Because the supreme Mind must contemplate the most beautiful object, and nothing is more beautiful than itself, it eternally thinks *itself and its own cogitations*—which are the Ideas. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** As Burges (1854) notes, Ficino clarifies the desire-formula as *"quod appetitur, immobile ipsum, appetentem agitat"* ("that which is desired, itself unmoved, agitates the one who desires"). In the esoteric reception of the Renaissance, this synthesis of Plato and Aristotle reveals how divine providence and cosmic magic operate without mechanical push-and-pull. The First Father does not reach down with hands to shove the planetary spheres; he radiates an erotic and intellectual gravitational field. By contemplating his own interior beauty, his self-reflexive thought (*noesis noeseos*) generates the world of Ideas. The World-Soul and the planetary intellects, struck with longing (*appetitus*) for that supreme beauty, revolve in eternal circular motion to imitate his stillness. For the Renaissance magus, this is the secret of attraction: the highest power in any hierarchy moves everything below it not by chasing after subordinates, but by becoming an unmoved, self-sufficient center of radiance toward which lower things voluntarily turn.
