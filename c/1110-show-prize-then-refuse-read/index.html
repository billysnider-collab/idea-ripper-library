"Let but the commons hear this testament-- Which, pardon me, I do not mean to read--" "You will compel me then to read the will?"

— In our words · Source: The Art of Public Speaking (J. Berg Esenwein & Dale Carnegie)

[Use when]

You have a strong document or number and want the room to ask for it.

[Why it matters]

Antony holds up Caesar's will and declines to read it, so the crowd demands it. By the time he gives in, they are asking for the argument he came to make.
