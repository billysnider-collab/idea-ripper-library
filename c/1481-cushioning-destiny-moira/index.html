[Use when]

Handling black-swan events, chronic illness, sudden regulatory or market shifts, or any crisis caused by forces outside your control. ---

[Why it matters]

Accept your allotted share of fated misfortunes without rage, work pragmatically to heal or soften them as much as possible, and trust that the wise are spared the worst ruin. The facing Greek (p. 116) speaks of sufferings that mortals incur through *daimoniēsi tychais* ("daimonic chances" or cosmic lotteries). The Pythagorean response is neither fatalistic passivity nor raging rebellion (*mēd' aganaktei*—"do not be indignant"). Instead, it prescribes a three-step operational mechanic: 1. **Diagnostic Realism:** "Judge them what they are" (recognize your specific *moira*, your allotted portion of the cosmic distribution). 2. **Equanimous Endurance:** Bear the initial impact gently (*praōs phere*) without adding psychological tantrums to physical pain. 3. **Active Remediation:** "Strive, as much as thou art able, to modify the traits" (Greek *iasthai*—to heal or remedy like a physician). Finally, the text offers a structural guarantee: fate does not deal a massive share of true evil to the *agathoi* (the good/wise), because a harmonized soul does not experience external blows as internal ruin. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Ficino and Pico della Mirandola devoted massive energy to reconciling astral fate (*fatum* / *daimoniē tychē*) with human providence. In their reading of this Pythagorean doctrine, the lower *daimones* administer the mechanical blows of planetary fate upon the physical body and fortune. The uninitiated person, living entirely in the passions, is crushed by these astral transits. The Sage, however, has elevated his centre of gravity into the intellectual sphere (*Mens*), above the reach of sub-lunary *Moira*. He uses natural magic, medicine, and philosophical music to "modify the traits" (*iasthai*—remedy the planetary influxes), while knowing that no astral configuration can penetrate the citadel of a purified soul.
