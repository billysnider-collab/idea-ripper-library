"Public bluff and bluster intended to induce caution often induced risk-taking." "Khrushchev had no way of knowing whether this statement was intended for a domestic audience or for him."

— In our words · Source: The Oxford Handbook of Modern Diplomacy (Andrew F. Cooper, Jorge Heine & Ramesh Thakur)

[Use when]

You're sending a warning through a public channel and assuming the target will read it the way you meant it.

[Why it matters]

David Welch finds that broadcast diplomacy before the Cuban missile crisis largely failed. Kennedy's September warning could be read as campaign talk for the midterms, which made Khrushchev's secret deployment look survivable. A message meant for one audience gets decoded by another.
