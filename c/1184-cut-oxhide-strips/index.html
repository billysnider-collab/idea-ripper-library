"‘as much land as could be covered by a single hide of an ox’" "Then, she secretly has the oxhide cut into the thinnest possible strips."

— In our words · Source: Carthage: A New History (Eve MacDonald)

[Use when]

Someone asks you for 'just one' of something and nobody has said how it is measured.

[Why it matters]

In Justin's telling, Dido asked a local king for one oxhide of land, then cut the hide into a thread long enough to circle the hill that became the Byrsa citadel. The price was agreed. She changed the unit. MacDonald notes the story fed the Roman picture of the tricky Carthaginian.
