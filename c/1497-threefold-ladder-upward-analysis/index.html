Card #c1497IR // #c1497Synthesis

But of Analysis there are three kinds; one is an ascent from things perceptible by a sense to the primary perceptible by mind; another is an ascent through things (fully) shown and obscurely shown to propositions not to be demonstrated and without a middle ; and another is that, which ascends from an hypothesis to principles not hypothetical. Now the first is something of this kind; as if we should proceed from the beauty relating to the body to the beauty relating to the soul; and from this to that in pursuits; and from this to that in laws; and then to the wide sea of beauty; and then, after having proceeded thus, we should discover what remains, namely, beauty itself. The second kind of Analysis is something like this. It is requisite to suppose what is to be sought, and to see what things are before it, and to show these from what come after, by ascending up to those before, until we arrive at the first and what is acknowledged ; and beginning (again), from this we shall descend to what is sought by the Synthetical manner. — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Ch. 5, trans. George Burges (1854) **Surface Gloss:** Dialectic climbs upward through three types of Analysis: (1) rising from sensory instances to the pure Idea, (2) working backward from a target theorem to self-evident axioms before deducing downward, and (3) testing hypotheses against higher non-hypothetical principles. When stuck on a complex problem, deploy the **Second Analytical Engine (Backward-Then-Forward Proof)**: 1. Assume your target conclusion is true. 2. Ask: "What single condition must be true immediately prior to this for my conclusion to hold?" 3. Repeat that backward step until you hit a bedrock fact that everyone in the room already agrees is undeniable. 4. Flip the chain around and walk your audience forward from their own accepted premise to your conclusion.

[Why it matters]

Dialectic climbs upward through three types of Analysis: (1) rising from sensory instances to the pure Idea, (2) working backward from a target theorem to self-evident axioms before deducing downward, and (3) testing hypotheses against higher non-hypothetical principles. In Alcinous’s dialectical architecture, Division and Definition move *downward* (slicing a broad genus like "animal" into species via differences), while Analysis moves *upward* from the complex and derivative to the simple and primary. Alcinous codifies three distinct upward engines drawn from across the Platonic corpus and geometrical method: 1. **Ontological Ascent:** Moving from physical particulars to psychical habits, civic institutions, vast fields of knowledge, and finally the isolated Form itself. 2. **Demonstrative Regress & Synthesis:** Taking a disputed proposition (e.g., "the soul is immortal"), tracing backward through its necessary conditions (always-moved $\rightarrow$ self-moved $\rightarrow$ principle of motion $\rightarrow$ unbegotten), hitting an undisputed axiom ("whatever is unbegotten is indestructible"), and then reversing direction to lock the proof down synthetically. 3. **Hypothetical Ascent:** Positing a working hypothesis, testing its consequences, and backing it up into higher hypotheses until reaching an unconditioned first principle. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the Renaissance Platonic revival, this chapter is celebrated as the union of logical rigor and erotic mysticism. Notably, Burges (1854) observes in his footnotes that where the Greek speaks of the "wide sea of beauty" (*pelagos*), Ficino substitutes *"ad ipsum—fontem"* ("to the very fountain of beauty"), and adds *"gradatim"* ("step by step"). For Ficino and Pico della Mirandola, Alcinous’s first mode of Analysis is the operational staircase of Platonic love (*eros*), by which the soul strips away material dimension at each rung until it drinks from the super-essential Fountain of Beauty. By placing this erotic ascent alongside geometrical regress and hypothetical dialectic, the *prisca theologia* tradition argued that mystical illumination is not an irrational leap, but the highest, most rigorous form of analytical science—resolving composite effects back into their simple, divine Monad.
