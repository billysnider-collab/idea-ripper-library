Card #c542IR // #c542Synthesis

"Many people calling for an interview, seeking an audience with a person in authority, do not want anything except a sympathetic ear — someone who will listen to them while they unburden themselves. It seems that no one wants sympathy so much as do these hard-boiled businessmen." The listener's job during the vent is to do almost nothing: "Listen patiently and with an open mind. Be sincere about it. Encourage the expression of the other person's ideas." Carnegie's case: an insurance company defused an indignant policyholder not by arguing but by letting him talk for hours — "When the storm blew over, the adjuster offered to do what was obviously right."

— In our words · Source: How to Win Friends and Influence People (Dale Carnegie)

[Use when]

Someone comes at you hot with a complaint — don't solve, don't defend, don't interrupt; let the talk finish the job it started.

[Why it matters]

The reflex is to fix, argue, or interrupt the vent — all three add pressure to a full vessel. The mechanism says the talking IS the fix: attention drains the charge, and only then is the person capable of hearing anything.
