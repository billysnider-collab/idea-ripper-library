But when a long train of abuses and usurpations, pursuing invariably the same object, evinces a design to reduce them under absolute despotism, it is their right, it is their duty, to throw off such government, and to provide new guards for their future security.

— In our words · Source: Declaration of Independence (Thomas Jefferson)

[Use when]

Someone needs the line between a grievance and a mandate — between being wronged and being obligated to act.

[Why it matters]

The escalation from "right" to "duty" is the whole moral engine of the document. A right lets you act; a duty condemns you if you don't. And the trigger is carefully set: not a single abuse, not a bad law, but a "long train" of them "pursuing invariably the same object" — a pattern that proves design. One injury is an incident; a pattern is a plan. That distinction is what separates revolution from riot. The sentence also smuggles in the forward obligation: you don't just tear down, you "provide new guards," which is why the document ends by constituting new states rather than merely dissolving old ties.
