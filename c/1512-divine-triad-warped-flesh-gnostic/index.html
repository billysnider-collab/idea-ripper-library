Humans have these same three divine powers, but when clothed in a mortal body, the appropriating power mutates into desire and the impelling into anger. **Refracted Faculties (Vice as Warped Infrastructure):** Destructive human drives are not alien bugs; they are high-level executive functions warped by a high-friction environment. Greed and lust are just the "Appropriating Faculty" (the power to claim, integrate, and steward) running through a scarcity-driven body; rage is just the "Impelling Faculty" (pure executive momentum) firing under threat. Don't amputate the drive—upgrade the medium it's running through.

[Why it matters]

Alcinous resolves a notorious contradiction in Plato's dialogues: in the *Timaeus* (69c), the two lower parts of the soul (anger and appetite) are "mortal" additions bolted onto the immortal rational soul upon incarnation; yet in the *Phaedrus* (246a), *even the unembodied souls of the gods* are depicted as a charioteer driving two horses. How can disembodied gods have a tripartite soul if the lower two parts are mortal and bodily? Alcinous solves this via **modal mutation under embodiment**. Disembodied divine and human souls alike possess a pre-corporeal triad of pure faculties: 1. **The Gnostic** (*kritikon / gnostikon*): the cognitive, judging power. 2. **The Parastatic** (*hormetikon / parastatikon*): the assistive, impelling, executive drive. 3. **The Appropriating** (*oikeiotikon*): the power of claiming, harmonizing, and making things one's own (a brilliant Middle Platonic capture of Stoic *oikeiosis*). When a human soul is invested with flesh, these latter two divine powers undergo a phase-change (*metabole*): pure executive drive warps into defensive *anger* (*thymos*), and pure appropriation warps into bodily *desire/appetite* (*epithymia*). [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Burges (1854) notes that Ficino struggled with these rare Greek technical terms, rendering *parastatikon* as *aggrediendi naturam, quem assistendi vigorem possumus nuncupare* ("the nature of undertaking, which we may call the vigor of assisting") and *oikeiotikon* as *vim qua conciliant et gubernant* ("the power by which they reconcile and govern"). In the esoteric Platonic and Kabbalistic reception, this passage is a revelation of **archetypal psychology**: our basest sins are not foreign additions from an evil demiurge, but *refracted divine attributes*. Lust and greed (*epithymia*) are the somatic distortion of the divine *oikeiotikon*—the cosmic power by which the gods draw, unite, and assimilate the universe to themselves. Rage (*thymos*) is the somatic distortion of the divine *parastatikon*—the pure executive force (the Martial/Geburah current) of the gods. Salvation or theurgy, therefore, does not consist in amputating our drives, but in *de-corporalizing* them—transmuting bodily appetite back into cosmic appropriation, and defensive anger back into unyielding executive will.
