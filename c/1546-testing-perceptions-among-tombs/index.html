[Why it matters]

Democritus walled himself into a tiny garden hut so deeply absorbed in thought that he missed an ox tied outside, and deliberately retreated to lonely places and graveyards to test and discipline his sensory impressions. Diogenes draws on Antisthenes' *Successions* to show how Democritus' physical asceticism served his atomist epistemology. In Democritus' system, sensation occurs by "the falling of visions across our sight" (*eidōla*—thin atomic films continuously shed by all objects, including decaying bodies and atmospheric compounds). Ordinary people, bombarded by these films in places of dread like graveyards, mistake disturbing *eidōla* for ghosts or wrathful gods, destroying their *euthymia* (cheerfulness/calm). When Democritus retreats to a walled garden cell or sits alone "among tombs," he is running a controlled perceptual laboratory: "testing perceptions in various manners" (literally, exercising himself in *phantasiai*). By isolating his senses from social noise and exposing them to high-fear environments like cemeteries, he trains his mind (which is itself a stable cluster of spherical fire-atoms) to receive incoming sensory films without superstitious panic or cognitive distortion. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In Hellenistic, late antique, and Renaissance esoteric traditions, Democritus' vigils "among tombs" transformed the hard-nosed atomist into the archetype of the melancholic magus and necromancer (celebrated in the pseudo-Democritean alchemical corpus, *Physica et Mystica*, and analyzed in the Renaissance tradition of *melancholia generosa* by Ficino and Agrippa). Ficino associated such extreme solitary withdrawal—so total that an ox tied at the door goes unnoticed—with Saturnian contemplation: the detachment of the rational soul (*mens*) from the bodily senses, which opens the mind to higher secrets and prophetic foresight (which Diogenes himself notes in Section VII, where Democritus predicts the future and wins divine honours). What the doxographer records as sense-testing among graves was read by the Renaissance occult tradition as Saturnian incubation at the threshold between the visible and invisible worlds.
