Card #c515IR // #c515Synthesis

"We should lead a decent human life simply because we are decent human beings" — the line that cut Lin's last tie to Christianity. Morality built on heaven-and-hell bookkeeping cheapens morality itself: it implies you wouldn't be decent without the reward, and it drags goodness through a gauntlet of hypothetical postulates — sin, redemption, celestial treasure — none capable of direct proof. Paganism's simplicity: doing good is its own justification. Love among men should be a final, absolute fact, without being reminded of a third party in heaven.

— In our words · Source: The Importance of Living (Lin Yutang)

[Use when]

Someone frames ethics as a cosmic loyalty program — rewards for the good, punishments for the bad — and you need the case that decency stands on its own feet.

[Why it matters]

Transaction-based morality is fragile morality. If your decency depends on cosmic wages, it collapses the moment the wages look uncertain — and it insults the dignity of the act itself. Lin's pagan standard is sturdier: reverence for the beauty, grandeur, and mystery of life is sufficient, and the orphan who has learned to be independent is more mature than the child waiting for a father's presents.
