For they placed that, which feels anger, in the heart; but that, which is affected by desire, in the intermediate place between the boundary on the side of the navel and that of the diaphragm, after binding it down, as if it were a mad and wild beast. But the lungs they planned, for the sake of the heart, to be soft and without blood, and with cavities, and sponge-like, in order that the heart, while leaping, according as anger was boiling, might have a softening down; but the liver for exciting the feeling of desire in the soul, and for rendering it gentle, by having a sweetness and bitterness; and moreover for making manifest the prophetic power in dreams; for there is shown in it the power carried on from the mind through what is smooth and thick and brilliant; but the spleen for the sake of the liver, in order that the former may cleanse the latter, and render it brilliant, and at least receive to the same the differences, generated by certain diseases around the liver. — Alcinous, *Handbook of Platonism*, Ch. 23, trans. George Burges (1854), pp. 285–286. *Surface Gloss:* The lesser gods quarantined mortal passions below the neck, giving the boiling heart a spongy lung-cushion and the wild-beast belly a smooth, shiny liver. The liver translates the mind's thoughts into bitter threats or sweet prophetic dreams, while the spleen acts as a sponge to wipe the liver's mirror clean. **The Sub-Rational Screen (Governing by Image, Not Argument):** You cannot govern an appetite or a mob with a syllabus, because appetites do not process abstract logic. To steer the non-rational tier of a system, you need a translator organ that converts high-level policy into visceral images—projected terror and sweet reward—and a maintenance mechanism (a "spleen") dedicated exclusively to keeping that communication channel free of noise.

[Why it matters]

Alcinous faces a classic Platonic engineering problem (drawn from *Timaeus* 70a–72d): if the appetitive soul (*epithymetikon*) is a "mad and wild beast" chained in the belly, completely deaf to rational argument (*logos*), how can the rational king in the head govern it? The lesser gods solve this through **visceral optics and chemical feedback**. They place the liver next to the seat of appetite and construct it to be dense, smooth, and bright (*leion kai pyknon kai lampron*)—the exact physical properties of a mirror. The rational mind in the head cannot argue with the belly, so it projects its thoughts downward as visual images onto the shiny surface of the liver. To terrify the beast into obedience, the mind uses the liver's native bitterness to wrinkle it and project frightening phantoms; to soothe it during sleep, it uses the liver's native sweetness to smooth the surface, allowing clear prophetic dreams to reflect there. The spleen exists solely as an anatomical wiper-rag (*ekmageion*), absorbing murky humors so the liver's prophetic screen stays polished. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Burges (1854) records that Ficino rendered the spleen's function as absorbing *putredines corrodentes* ("corroding putrefactions") that flow around the liver. In the esoteric reception of the *Timaeus*—from late antique theurgy through Ficino and Cornelius Agrippa—this passage explains why rational waking intellect is often inferior to dream-divination and why ancient haruspicy inspected the livers of sacrificial animals. The rational intellect (*nous*) speaks in abstract universals, which the lower somatic consciousness cannot read. The liver is the alchemical transducer that steps down high-voltage noetic truth into symbolic, imaginal cinema (*phantasmata*). However, the validity of a vision depends entirely on the physical purity of the receiver: if the spleen fails to draw off melancholic and bilious dregs, the mirror of the liver becomes tarnished, and the sleeper receives demonic nightmares rather than true divination.
