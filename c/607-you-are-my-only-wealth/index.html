Card #c607IR // #c607Synthesis

The beggar-master, robbed of everything and with nothing but a loincloth, tells his dog: 'you alone are my kin, my son, my household, my friend' - and calls his broken potsherd and his dog 'my everything.' The poem's quiet thesis is that wealth is relationship, not possession: the poorest man in the story is also the richest, because he has one being who is loyal to death, while the rich man with his hoard has nothing and gives nothing. "My loincloth alone is my fortune; you alone are my kin, my son, my household, my friend... the dog alone is my kin." (vv. 23-25)

— In our words · Source: Śvānastavakāvyam (The Dog-Praise Poem) (Jñāneśvara of Davuldeṇa)

[Use when]

Use when recalibrating what 'rich' or 'successful' means in your own life. Use when weighing a choice between more money and a relationship that actually holds. Use when someone with everything still feels empty - ask what they actually own.

[Why it matters]

It exposes a false measure. People who optimize possessions while letting relationships rot are, on this accounting, the actually poor - and the poem shows it by making the 'rich' man stingy and miserable and the 'poor' man rich in the only asset that held. It is a concrete reframe for any decision about where to spend your finite attention and care.
