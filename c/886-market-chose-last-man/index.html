Card #c886IR // #c886Synthesis

Zarathustra descends from ten years on the mountain with the Superman — and the crowd laughs him off the stage. He pivots to warning them about the Last Man, and the multitude shouts back: “‘Give us this last man, O Zarathustra,’—they called out—‘make us into these last men! Then will we make thee a present of the Superman!’” He turns away sad: “They understand me not: I am not the mouth for these ears.”

— In our words · Source: Thus Spoke Zarathustra (Friedrich Nietzsche)

[Use when]

When strong work lands flat — before rewriting the work, check whether you misjudged the room.

[Why it matters]

The best message fails at the wrong altitude. Zarathustra had the truth and the wrong audience calibration — he spoke mountain to marketplace. The lesson is not to dilute the message but to find ears it fits, or build the ears first. Launching is a translation problem, not just a truth problem.
