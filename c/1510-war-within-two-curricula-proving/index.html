Secondly, since the part, that has the property of suffering, and that, which has the property of reasoning, are different by nature, it is meet for them to be in separate places. For they are found to be at war with each other. Now nothing is able to be at war with itself; nor can the things opposed to each other stand together at the same time about the same object. At least in the case of Medea, anger is seen to be at war with reason; for she says— I know how great the ills I'm about to do; But rage has a pow'r greater than my counsels. And in the case of Laius, when carrying off Chrysippus, desire is at war with reason; for he says— Alas! this thing from god to man's an ill, When, what is good, one knows, but uses not. Still further is it presented to the mind, that the property of suffering is different from that of reasoning, from the care of the property of reasoning being one thing, but that of suffering another; for the former is effected by the discipline of teaching; the latter by the practice of morality. — Alcinous, *Handbook of Platonism*, Ch. 24, trans. George Burges (1854), pp. 286–287. *Surface Gloss:* Reason and passion must be distinct parts of the soul in separate bodily locations because they fight each other simultaneously (as in Medea and Laius), and because they require completely different training: reason is cared for by teaching, passion by habitual practice. **The Cure-Reveals-the-Organ Diagnostic:** When two impulses inside a person or organization are fighting each other at the exact same moment, stop treating it as a single confused mind that needs better information. You can prove two subsystems are structurally separate by how they are fixed: if a lecture fixes it, it's a software/reason problem; if only repeated behavioral drill fixes it, it's a hardware/drive problem.

[Why it matters]

This chapter is a direct Middle Platonic polemic against Stoic psychological monism. Orthodox Stoics (specifically Chrysippus) argued that the soul's ruling center (*hegemonikon*) is a single, unified rational faculty: what we call "passion" is merely a corrupt rational judgment, and Medea's inner conflict is just a single mind oscillating rapidly between two opinions. Alcinous refutes this using Plato's Principle of Opposites (*Republic* 436b): the same thing cannot do or suffer opposites in the same respect, toward the same object, *at the exact same time*. By turning Chrysippus's own favorite tragic proof-texts (Euripides' *Medea* and *Chrysippus*) against the Stoics, Alcinous demonstrates that cognitive awareness of the good ("I know") coexists simultaneously with the affective compulsion toward evil ("rage has greater power"). He seals the structural separation with an Aristotelian pedagogical test (*Nicomachean Ethics* II.1): if passion were merely a mistaken belief, a lecture would cure it. Instead, the rational part (*logistikon*) responds to instruction (*didaskalia*), while the passionate part (*pathetikon*) can only be shaped by non-rational habituation (*askesis ethous*). [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Burges (1854) notes that Ficino expanded the Principle of Opposites with full scholastic precision (*circa idem secundum idem eodem tempore*) and rendered the cure of the passionate part as *consuetudine et exercitatione* ("by custom and exercise"). In Renaissance Platonic-Christian synthesis, Laius's lament ("When, what is good, one knows, but uses not") was immediately aligned with St. Paul's *Romans* 7 ("For the good that I would I do not: but the evil which I would not, that I do") and Ovid's *video meliora proboque, deteriora sequor*. For the esoteric tradition, this duality proved that intellectual illumination (gnosis/doctrine) is impotent without preliminary ritual and ascetic purgation (*catharsis*). Because man is a compound creature, you cannot initiate the mind until you have broken and re-habituated the astral/passionate vehicle through austere discipline; otherwise, the initiate becomes a Medea—possessing high theoretical knowledge while being dragged into ruin by unpurged lower forces.
