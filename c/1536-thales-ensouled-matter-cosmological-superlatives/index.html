[Why it matters]

Thales posits water as the material first principle, argues from magnets and amber that seemingly inanimate matter possesses soul, declares the cosmos alive and filled with *daimones*, and defines six ultimate bounds: God, World, Place, Intellect, Necessity, and Time. Within the Milesian framework preserved by Diogenes, Thales dissolves the common-sense boundary between dead matter and living agency. First, he selects **water** (*hydor*) as the single *arche*—the underlying substrate capable of phase-shifting into solid, liquid, and vapor while sustaining biological life. Second, he uses empirical anomalies—the lodestone (magnet) pulling iron and rubbed amber attracting chaff—as diagnostic proof that *psyche* (understood functionally as the internal capacity to initiate motion) permeates what appears lifeless. When Thales says the cosmos is "full of dæmons" (*daimonon plere*), the term designates immanent, self-moving divine forces distributed throughout nature, not supernatural intruders. His six superlatives lock this living cosmos into a structural grid: unbegotten Divinity as origin, the ordered *kosmos* as craft-work, *topos* (Place) as the ultimate container, *nous* (Intellect) as the fastest penetrative medium, *ananke* (Necessity) as unyielding constraint, and *chronos* (Time) as the ultimate epistemic revealer. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** For Ficino (especially in *De Vita Coelitus Comparanda*) and Agrippa, Thales's observation of the magnet and amber is the foundational proof-text for **natural magic** and cosmic sympathy. If even stones and resins possess a vegetative or motive soul (*psyche*), then no part of the material world is dead; every mineral, herb, and talisman is a node in a living, ensouled animal (*mundus animatus*) teeming with mediating *daimones* that step down celestial influences into terrestrial matter. Thales's six superlatives were read in the Hermetic-Platonic revival as a catechism of the *Corpus Hermeticum*: Place as the vessel, Intellect (*Nous*) as the divine ray that traverses the spheres instantaneously without spatial delay, and Necessity as astral fate, which only the highest principle (the unbegotten God) transcends.
