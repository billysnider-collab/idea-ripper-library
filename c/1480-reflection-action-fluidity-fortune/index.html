[Why it matters]

Deliberate before speaking or acting, practice justice in word and deed, and keep mortality and the rapid rise-and-fall of material wealth constantly in view. The Greek text (p. 116) commands the student to practice justice (*dikaiosynēn*) in both deed and word, and never to habituate oneself to acting *alogistōs*—without *logos* (rational calculation or proportion). Why do humans act unjustly or impulsively? Because they suffer from temporal myopia, treating material acquisition as permanent and their biological lifespan as infinite. The Pythagorean corrective pairs two structural reminders: first, death is decreed (*peprōtai*) for all by an invincible law; second, *chrēmata* (wealth/property) moves in a tidal wave—now easily gained, now easily lost. By meditating on the guaranteed termination of the body and the high volatility of external goods, the rational mind breaks its panicked attachment to status and resources, making calm, just deliberation possible. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In the *prisca theologia* tradition, *dikaiosynē* (Justice) is the ethical manifestation of the number Four (the first square number, $2 \times 2$, returning equal for equal). To act only after reflection is to imitate the Demiurge, who contemplates the archetypal Ideas before speaking the cosmos into existence. Renaissance emblems and Neoplatonic treatises contrasted the Wheel of Fortune (*Rota Fortunae*)—which governs sub-lunary riches and honours—with the immovable center of the philosophical soul. By keeping mortality (*memento mori*) before the mind's eye, the initiate loosens the soul's glue to the corporeal shell while still incarnate, practicing the "philosophical death" praised by Plato and Ficino long before physical death arrives.
