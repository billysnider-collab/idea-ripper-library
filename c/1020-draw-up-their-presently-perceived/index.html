"In constructing the other side's presently perceived choice the first question to ask is 'Whose decision do I want to affect?' The second question is what decision people on the other side now see you asking them to make."

— In our words · Source: Getting to Yes (Roger Fisher, William Ury & Bruce Patton)

[Use when]

The other side keeps refusing something you think is obviously in their interest.

[Why it matters]

The authors map the Iranian students' yes/no ledger as the students saw it in 1980, and keeping the hostages looks rational from inside it. Your arguments bounce off because you are answering a question they are not asking.
