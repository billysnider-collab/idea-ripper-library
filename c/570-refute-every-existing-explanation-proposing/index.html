Card #c570IR // #c570Synthesis

To introduce a genuinely new claim, first enumerate the received options a listener already carries and knock each one down explicitly. Eugnostos lists the three ways philosophers explain the world — it runs itself, it is run by providence, it is run by fate — then dismisses all three: 'it is none of these.' Naming the alternatives out loud clears the space so the audience stops cycling through stock answers and becomes willing to hear yours.

— In our words · Source: The Nag Hammadi Library

[Use when]

Introducing an idea that contradicts popular or common-sense explanations. Use when an audience keeps defaulting to familiar answers you haven't ruled out. Use when framing a paper or pitch and you need to clear the board before your thesis.

[Why it matters]

Most arguments lose because the audience silently fills in a stock explanation you never addressed. By voicing and eliminating the menu first, you prevent 'what about providence?' objections from living rent-free. Before asserting what something is, assert what it is not — from among what people already believe.
