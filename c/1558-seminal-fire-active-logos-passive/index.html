They think that there are two general principles in the universe, the active and the passive. That the passive is matter, an existence without any distinctive quality. That the active is the reason which exists in the passive, that is to say, God. For that he, being eternal, and existing throughout all matter, makes everything... They also teach that God is unity, and that he is called Mind, and Fate, and Jupiter, and by many other names besides. And that, as he was in the beginning by himself, he turned into water the whole substance which pervaded the air; and as the seed is contained in the produce, so too, he being the seminal principle of the world, remained behind in moisture, making matter fit to be employed by himself in the production of those things which were to come after; and then, first of all, he made the four elements, fire, water, air, and earth... They also speak of the world in a threefold sense; at one time meaning God himself, whom they call a being of a certain quality, having for his peculiar manifestation universal substance, a being imperishable, and who never had any generation, being the maker of the arrangement and order that we see; and who, after certain periods of time, absorbs all substance in himself, and then re-produces it from himself. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives*, VII.68, 70 (tr. [Why it matters]

The universe consists of two co-eternal principles: qualityless passive matter and the active immanent Reason (God/Zeus), who operates as a "seminal principle" (*logos spermatikos*) inside cosmic moisture and periodically reabsorbs and regenerates the entire cosmos. Stoic physics synthesizes Heraclitean fire-cosmology with biological embryology. Unlike Plato's transcendent Demiurge who shapes matter from the outside looking at eternal Forms, the Stoic God is radically **immanent**: an active corporeal principle (*to poioun*) completely blended ("concretions take place over the whole of it... like wine thrown into the sea," VII.78) throughout qualityless passive matter (*apoiostes ousia*). At the start of each cosmic cycle, God—who in his pure state is "artificial fire" (*pyr technikon*, VII.84) or pure æther—condenses the cosmos through air into primordial moisture. Within that moisture, God remains present as the *logos spermatikos* ("seminal principle"), unfolding the four elements and all individual creatures according to programmed rational ratios, just as biological seed contains the blueprint of the adult animal (VII.85). Because every finite composite is perishable, at the end of the Great Year God reabsorbs all matter back into pure fire (*ekpyrosis*), only to seed the identical cosmos anew. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** Few passages in ancient philosophy had a deeper impact on Western esotericism, alchemy, and Christian Logos-theology (via Philo and Justin Martyr, then Ficino and Paracelsus) than VII.68–70. Here is the explicit doctrine of the *Logos Spermatikos*—the divine Word as a fiery seed hidden inside the primordial chaotic waters—and of *natura* as an "artificial fire tending by a regular road to production" (VII.84). To Renaissance alchemists, Zeno's cosmogony was the exact formula of the Great Work: the *Artifex* (Active Sulphur / Secret Fire) enters the qualityless *Prima Materia* through the medium of radical moisture (Philosophical Mercury), gestates the four elements, and at the end of the regime calcines the whole work back into the Red King, who absorbs all substance into himself to multiply it anew.
