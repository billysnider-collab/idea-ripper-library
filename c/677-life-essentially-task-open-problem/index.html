Card #c677IR // #c677Synthesis

Life is not something you have; it's something you must do, here and now, as an open problem. 'Life is, then, essentially a task and an open problem—a tangle of problems that must be resolved.' Presence is inhabiting the here-and-now where the task is still undone, rather than treating life as a solved thing or a finished possession. The person who is present is the one who is still doing life, not reviewing it.

— In our words · Source: Meditations on Hunting (José Ortega y Gasset)

[Use when]

Use when you catch yourself living in memory or anticipation as if life were settled. Use to return to the here-and-now as the only place the 'task' of living actually occurs.

[Why it matters]

Ortega's definition of life is radically active: life is 'that which we must do here and now,' never done, always yet to be done. It collapses the distance between 'having a life' and 'being alive' — you are alive only in the doing, in the open problem. This is the philosophical root of presence: the now is where the unfinished task lives.
