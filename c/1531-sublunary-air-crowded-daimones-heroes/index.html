Moreover, that Mercury is the steward of the souls, and that on this account he has the name of Conductor, and Commercial, and Infernal, since it is he who conducts the souls from their bodies, and from earth, and sea; and that he conducts the pure souls to the highest region, and that he does not allow the impure ones to approach them, nor to come near one another; but commits them to be bound in indissoluble fetters by the Furies. The Pythagoreans also assert, that the whole air is full of souls, and that these are those which are accounted dæmones, and heroes. [Why it matters]

Hermes sorts disembodied souls by purity, raising the pure to the upper ether while the lower atmosphere remains crowded with souls called *daimones* and heroes, who transmit dreams, omens of health or sickness, and divination to mortals and flocks. Within the Pythagorean physical system recorded by Alexander Polyhistor, there is no empty void in the sublunary atmosphere. Earlier in the same section, the text establishes a two-zone atmospheric physics: the upper *aither* is in perpetual motion, pure, and divine, whereas the lower air (*aer*, "cold aither") around the earth is sluggish and mortal. When souls detach from bodies under the stewardship of Hermes (*Tamias ton psychon*), pure souls ascend into the upper moving *aither*. The lower air remains densely populated with disembodied souls—categorized in Greek ontological hierarchy (cf. VIII.19 earlier: Gods -> *daimones* -> heroes -> men) as *daimones* and heroes. In this strictly pagan framework, *daimones* are **ontological intermediaries**, not evil fiends: because the immortal celestial Gods in the upper *aither* do not mix directly with terrestrial corruption, the aerial souls act as the conductive medium for dreams, prognostic signs (*semeia*) of disease or health in men and livestock, lustral purifications (*katharmoi*), and technical divination. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In Renaissance Neoplatonism (Ficino’s *De Vita*, Agrippa’s *De Occulta Philosophia*), this passage—paired with Pythagoras's rule to avoid flatulent beans so that "the visions which appear in one's sleep [remain] gentle and free from agitation" (VIII.19) and to wear clean white wool—explains the mechanics of pneumatic magic and dream-incubation. Because the surrounding air is literally a crowded sea of disembodied *daimones* and heroes constantly broadcasting impressions, the human soul (which is itself a fragment torn from the *aither*) acts like aresonating string or mirror. If the body's internal vapors are turbulent from heavy flesh, beans, or anger, the aerial *daimones* cast distorted, terrifying phantasms upon the sleeper; if the body is purified by lustration, fasting, and lyre-hymns, the soul tunes into higher aerial intermediaries and receives accurate diagnostic dreams and prophetic warnings.
