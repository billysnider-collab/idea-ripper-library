Card #c483IR // #c483Synthesis

Prudence, indeed, will dictate that governments long established should not be changed for light and transient causes; and accordingly all experience hath shown that mankind are more disposed to suffer, while evils are sufferable than to right themselves by abolishing the forms to which they are accustomed.

— In our words · Source: Declaration of Independence (Thomas Jefferson)

[Use when]

You need to explain why obviously broken systems persist, or why your own drastic move is the reluctant last resort rather than the first impulse.

[Why it matters]

This is the document's conservative valve, and it is there to make the revolution credible. By admitting that people endure far too much for far too long, the authors inoculate themselves against the charge of hotheadedness: if even these patient sufferers are rising, the cause must be grave. It is also a cold piece of political psychology that still holds — the status quo has a gravity of its own, and "the forms to which they are accustomed" will be defended by the very people they crush. Anyone trying to move a system should expect this drag and plan for it, not be surprised by it.
