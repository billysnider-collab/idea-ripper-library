XL. These now were his chief opinions. He affirmed that the soul was immortal and clothed in many bodies successively, and that its first principle was number, and that the first principle of the body was geometry. And he defined it as an abstract idea of spirit diffused in every direction. He said also, that it was self-moving and threefold. [...] And that it is placed in the middle of the body, and embraces it at the same time in all its parts, and that it consists of elements; and that when it is divided according to harmonic intervals it forms two connected circles; of which the inner circle is divided into six portions, and makes in all seven circles; and that this is placed on the left hand of the diameter, and situated in the interior. But the other is on the right hand of the same line; on which account, and because it is one only, it is the superior of the two. For the other is divided Internally; and this too, is the circle of that which is always the same; the other, the circle of that which is changeable and different. [...] The circle of what is changeable, engenders opinion by its regular movements; but the circle of that which is always the same produces knowledge. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book III, Section XL, trans. [Why it matters]

Summarizing the *Timaeus*, Diogenes describes the soul as rooted in number (while body is rooted in geometry) and structured as two intersecting harmonic rings: an undivided outer Circle of the Same generating knowledge, and a sevenfold inner Circle of the Different generating opinion. This doxographical digest compresses the psychogony of Plato's *Timaeus* alongside early Academic definitions (such as Xenocrates' definition of soul as self-moving number and Speusippus/Posidonius's mathematical definitions). The architecture rests on a strict dimensional hierarchy: the soul's first principle (*arche*) is **Number** (arithmetic/harmonic intervals), whereas the body's first principle is **Geometry** (the scalene and isosceles triangles of III.41 that build the four elemental polyhedra). The World-Soul (and by isomorphism, the human rational soul) is constructed like an armillary sphere: an undivided outer ring revolving to the right along the celestial equator—the Circle of the Same (*tauton*)—and an inner ring tilted along the ecliptic and sliced into seven unequal harmonic tracks (Moon, Sun, and five planets)—the Circle of the Different (*thateron*). Epistemology is directly wired to this celestial mechanics: when the soul's revolution aligns with the undivided outer ring, it produces unshakeable Knowledge (*episteme*); when it tracks the sevenfold planetary ring of becoming, it produces true Opinion (*doxa*). [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In Renaissance Platonic-Hermetic reception, the intersection of the Circle of the Same and the Circle of the Different at two opposite points forms the cosmic **Chi (Χ)**—the decussation of the World-Soul stamped into the heavens at the equinoctial points. Ficino and Pico della Mirandola read this passage as the blueprint for astral psychology and theurgy: man's microcosmic soul contains the exact sevenfold planetary octaves inside its "Circle of the Different," which is why sublunary passions and opinions fluctuate with planetary transits. To escape fate and opinion, the magus-philosopher must use harmonic music, number, and contemplation to shift consciousness out of the seven divided planetary tracks into the single, undivided right-hand revolution of the Circle of the Same—ascending from geometric body through numerical soul to the intelligible One.
