[Why it matters]

Citing Timaeus of Tauromenium, Diogenes describes the Pythagorean initiation: pooling all private property into a common store, enduring five years of strict silence while listening to unseen lectures, nocturnal assemblies of six hundred, and nightly three-question self-audits. Diogenes draws on the Sicilian historian Timaeus to reconstruct the institutional machinery of the Crotoniate brotherhood (*synedrion*). Unlike the open, fee-for-service lectures of the fifth-century Sophists or the public gymnasium debates of Athens, Pythagoras builds a high-barrier initiatory order (*hetaireia*) engineered around three structural compressors: 1. **Economic De-individuation:** *Koina ta ton philon* ("the property of friends is common"). Pooling assets into a common store strips away the status-hierarchy of private wealth at the door. 2. **The Five-Year *Echemythia* (Acoustic Silence):** Novices (*akoustikoi*, hearers) are barred from disputatious talk and even from visual contact with the master for five years until their character is tested (*dokimastheien*). By severing sight from sound and forbidding immediate rebuttal, the school starves the eristic ego (which Cratinus mocks at VIII.15bis) and trains the memory to retain complex oral architecture. 3. **The Nightly Retrospect:** The three-part evening catechism (later embedded in the *Golden Verses*) forces the disciple to replay the day's actions in reverse order before sleep, turning memory into a moral gyroscope. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] [Paratext Note: Yonge (1853), footnote 109, connects VIII.15 to the Latin tag: "Hence the proverb _Ipse dixit_; αὐτὸς ἔφα."] In Neoplatonic and Renaissance esotericism (Iamblichus, Reuchlin, Pico), the five-year silence behind the linen curtain (*velum*) separates the exoteric *akousmatikoi* (who hear only the disembodied voice of the master, like worshippers hearing an oracle from an inner adytum) from the esoteric *mathematikoi* (who cross the veil into the house—called by the Metapontines "the temple of Ceres," VIII.15—to behold the master face-to-face). Silence is the purgative alchemical bath of the tongue: until a student can hold a teaching without leaking it into vain chatter, their vessel is cracked. The nightly three-question examen trains backward-running memory, the exact faculty required to recover past-life recollection (*anamnesis*).
