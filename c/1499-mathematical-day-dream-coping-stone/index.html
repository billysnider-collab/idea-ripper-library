And as, by turning our thoughts to Astronomy, we are led on the road from things seen to an existence unseen and perceptible by mind, so by listening to the voice of Harmony, we pass, in like manner, from things heard to those that are beheld by the mind itself ; so that, unless we pursue in this way these subjects of learning, our contemplation on these matters will be incomplete, and unprofitable, and nothing worth... For the looking into Mathematical learning is a kind of prelude to the contemplation of things existing. For Geometry, and Arithmetic, and the sciences that follow upon them, although desirous to lay hold of the Being, yet are they in a dream respecting the Being, and unable to see it, as a day-dream, through being ignorant both of the principles (of things) and of what are formed from those principles. They happen, nevertheless, to be very useful, according to what has been stated. From whence Plato said that such subjects of learning were not sciences at all. The Dialectic art is then a progression, that naturally ascends from Geometrical Hypotheses to the first principles of things and non-hypo-thetical... and is, as it were, the coping-stone and guard. — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Ch. 7, trans. [Why it matters]

Arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, and music train the mind to leave the senses behind, but they remain "in a dream" about true reality because they cannot justify their own starting assumptions. Only Dialectic wakes up, questioning hypotheses to reach first principles as the coping-stone of knowledge. Alcinous maps the Contemplative sciences—Arithmetic, Geometry, Stereometry (solid dimension), Astronomy, and Harmonics—as a strictly propaedeutic training ground. Their job is cognitive purification: they scrub the rust of sensory error from the intellect and redirect the soul’s gaze from visible motions (the stars) and audible sounds (musical strings) to invisible ratios and intelligible order. If pursued merely for practical trade or sensory pleasure, they are worthless. Yet even at their highest pitch, the mathematical disciplines suffer from a structural blindness: they take their starting points (points, lines, odd, even) as unexamined hypotheses and reason downward from them. Because they cannot give an account of their own foundations, mathematicians are *dreaming* about reality rather than seeing it awake. They occupy a middle epistemic tier ("mental notion" / *dianoia*) between sense-opinion and true science. Only Dialectic steps onto geometrical hypotheses as springboards to ascend to unhypothetical first principles, serving as the protective coping-stone over all learning. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In his extensive footnote on this passage, Burges (1854) conjectures that the underlying Greek balanced the "eye of mind" with the "ear of mind" (*oti tou nou*), alluding to the inaudible Pythagorean harmony of the spheres. In the Renaissance reception of Ficino (who explicitly restores *Stereometria* as the third mathematical increase in his Latin text), the quadrivium is the ritual purification of the soul's astral vehicle prior to theological initiation. Numbers and harmonic proportions are the structural echoes of the Divine Mind stamped upon the cosmos. However, Ficino emphasizes Alcinous’s warning against getting trapped in mathematical formalism: the mathematician sees the divine archetypes only in a dream (*per somnium*) because he treats numbers as abstract quantities rather than living, substantial Ideas in the Mind of God. Dialectic is the *coping-stone* (*fastigium et custodia*) because it awakens the soul from mathematical symbols to the living realities they shadow forth.
