Card #c603IR // #c603Synthesis

The poem draws a hard line: a serpent raised daily on milk with love 'is never satisfied and never becomes grateful.' Its forked tongue, crooked motion, and habit of killing its benefactor are its nature, not a phase. The takeaway is a triage rule: before you invest repeated kindness into a person or situation, ask whether the ingratitude is circumstantial (fixable) or constitutional (not). Kindness does not cure a snake. "A serpent, though raised on milk daily with love, is never satisfied and never becomes grateful... what difference is there between a wicked man and a serpent?" (vv. 27-28)

— In our words · Source: Śvānastavakāvyam (The Dog-Praise Poem) (Jñāneśvara of Davuldeṇa)

[Use when]

Use when you keep helping someone who keeps not appreciating it. Use when a teammate, partner, or institution repays every good turn with disregard. Use when deciding whether a pattern is a rough patch or a character.

[Why it matters]

It protects you from the sunk-cost of generosity. The most expensive mistake in relationships, hiring, and politics is treating a constitutional ingrate as if more patience or more kindness would fix them. Naming the 'serpent' type early - someone who consumes care and still bites the hand - lets you withdraw the investment before it compounds.
