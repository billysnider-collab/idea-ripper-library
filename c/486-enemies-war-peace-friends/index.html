Card #c486IR // #c486Synthesis

We must, therefore, acquiesce in the necessity which denounces our separation, and hold them as we hold the rest of mankind, enemies in war, in peace friends.

— In our words · Source: Declaration of Independence (Thomas Jefferson)

[Use when]

A break has to be total on the issue but not total on the relationship — separating the regime from the people, the decision from the person.

[Why it matters]

The most disciplined sentence in the document. After pages of indictment, the authors refuse to make the enemy the British people — they separate the prince from the population, the regime from the nation. "They too, have been deaf" records the failure without hatred, and the closing formula keeps the future open: war is a condition, not an identity, and it ends. This is how you declare total political separation without declaring eternal enmity. It also shows the document's real audience discipline: the fight is with the Crown's power, and burning the bridge to the British people would have cost the very foreign sympathy the whole declaration was written to win.
