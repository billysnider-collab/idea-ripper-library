But when (Plato) says that the World has been generated, we must not understand him, as if there were once a time, when the World was not; but because it is ever in generation, and shows forth something as a cause more ancient than its own constitution. And even the deity does not make the Soul ever existing of the World, but puts it in order. And in this way he might be said to make it, by arousing up, as if from a drowsiness or heavy sleep, and turning to himself both its mind and itself, so that, by looking to what is perceptible by his mind, it may, while eagerly seeking his notions, assume species and forms... For that, which is without, remains uncut; but that, which is within, is cut into seven circles, divided from the commencement into double and triple intervals. Now that, which is comprehended by the (portion) of the sphere remaining uncut, is very like to the same; but the cut (like to) the different. For the movement of the heaven, which embraces all things, is not-wandering, as being one and in order; but that, which is within, is various, and changed by risings and settings; and hence it is called wandering. But that, which is without, is carried along to the right, by being moved from east to west ; but that, which is within, conversely to the left, from west to east, meeting the world. — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Ch. 14, trans. [Why it matters]

The world was never created at a literal point in time; it is "generated" because it is perpetually becoming and depends on a higher cause. God "makes" the eternal World-Soul by waking it from a heavy stupor to gaze at his Ideas, stretching it into an uncut outer sphere (the Same) and seven counter-revolving inner planetary circles (the Different). Alcinous settles a fierce ancient controversy over the *Timaeus*: did Plato mean that the physical universe had a literal temporal beginning? No. To say the world is "generated" means two structural things: (1) it is perpetually in a state of becoming/flux, and (2) its order depends upon a causal principle ontologically prior ("more ancient") to itself. Likewise, the World-Soul is eternal; God does not fabricate its substance out of nothing, but *orders* it by waking it from a pre-cosmic stupor ("drowsiness or heavy sleep") and turning its gaze toward his own intelligible Ideas. As the awakened World-Soul contemplates the Ideas, it receives distinct forms and ratios. Because like is known by like, the World-Soul contains both indivisible (intelligible) and divisible (bodily) existence, plus Identity (the Same) and Difference. Stretched from the center of the cosmos to its outermost boundary and wrapping it round from the outside, the Soul governs the world through a dual-gear astronomy: * **The Uncut Outer Sphere (The Same):** Moves uniformly from east to west to the right, carrying the fixed stars in unbroken order. * **The Seven-Cut Inner Sphere (The Different):** Sliced according to harmonic double ($1, 2, 4, 8$) and triple ($1, 3, 9, 27$) intervals into the seven planetary orbits (Moon, Sun, Venus/Mercury, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn), rotating counter to the outer heaven from west to east and generating Time—the moving image of eternity—until all seven centers align on a single perpendicular line in the Great Year. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the Renaissance reception, Alcinous’s image of God waking the World-Soul *"ex profundo quodam somno"* (Ficino's Latin, noted by Burges 1854) is a master key to both cosmology and psychological initiation. Because Ficino’s Christian context required a temporal creation, he treated Alcinous’s eternal-generation reading cautiously as a philosophical metaphor for continuous creation (*creatio continua*): if the Father ever withdrew his illuminating gaze, the World-Soul would immediately sink back into its chaotic stupor (*karos*). Furthermore, the counter-rotation of the **Uncut Sphere of the Same** against the **Seven-Cut Sphere of the Different** (where Ficino translates "meeting the world" as *"superiori resistens"*, "resisting the higher sphere") maps the microcosm of the human head. Our highest contemplative intellect moves with the single, uniform revolution of the Eighth Sphere (east to west), while our discursive, emotional, and planetary faculties revolve in seven complex counter-rhythms (west to east). Spiritual awakening consists in subduing the seven inner wandering circles to the single, uncut motion of the outer sphere.
