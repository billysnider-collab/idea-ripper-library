Card #c947IR // #c947Synthesis

The four names are not a freedom — they are four renunciations. The book titles them: Knowledge, Renunciation, the Way, Rest. Each new self is bought by killing the previous one, and the whole arc is a житие — a saint's life with a predetermined sacred shape. The narrative that makes the suffering meaningful (a life lived as a hagiography) is the same structure that removes the freedom to be anything else. Vodolazkin's own framing says it: 'one cannot help being surprised that all these people bear a single name' — the single name is the cage that forces the several selves into one predestined story. Ripped from the novel (translated from the Russian by the cutter): «В таких случаях нельзя не испытывать удивления, что все эти люди носят одно имя.» — "In such cases one cannot help being surprised that all these people bear a single name."

— In our words · Source: Laurus (Eugene Vodolazkin)

[Use when]

The story you tell about your life (redemption, penance, purpose) has stopped being a map and started being a sentence. Ask: am I using this narrative, or is it using me?

[Why it matters]

This is the shadow side of card two. Substitution gives the grief a task, but the task hardens into a destiny. The cure (meaning) and the cage (closure) are the same structure. Arseny does not choose his life so much as he is chosen by the story he took on — and each of his four names is a self he had to die from.
