Card #c618IR // #c618Synthesis

Before you trust or fight a rationalization, name it as a doctrine. Raskolnikov's is this: humanity splits into 'ordinary' men, who must obey the law, and 'extraordinary' men, who hold an inner right to overstep any obstacle their idea requires. It is the cleanest statement of 'the rules apply to other people, not to me, because I'm special' — the exact shape that reappears in every justification of a transgression, from the boardroom to the revolution. Learn to hear the doctrine beneath the rhetoric.

— In our words · Source: Crime and Punishment (Fyodor Dostoevsky)

[Use when]

Use when you hear someone invoke a special class of people who may break rules the rest must keep. Use when reading a manifesto, a founder's letter, or a pitch that quietly sorts humanity into those-with-rights and those-without. Use when auditing your own excuses for why a rule doesn't apply to you.

[Why it matters]

Every self-justifying ideology can be reduced to a version of this two-tier move: an in-group with rights, an out-group with duties. Dostoevsky states it so plainly that it becomes a template you can recognize instantly elsewhere. Once you can name the doctrine, you can stop being moved by its language and start asking what it is licensing.
