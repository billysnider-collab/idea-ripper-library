Card #c602IR // #c602Synthesis

The poem's whole thesis is that gratitude (kritajnata) is the single trait that separates the noble from the base - independent of wealth, birth, or station. A dog fed a little food and drink becomes a good, grateful person; a man served with gold and jewels corrupts his heart. Test character the same way: give someone a small, real favor, then watch whether they remember it and act on it. The remembering is the whole signal. "A wicked man, though served constantly with virtues, never becomes good by any means... but you, fed by a little food and drink, are a good person, a grateful one." (vv. 3, 26)

— In our words · Source: Śvānastavakāvyam (The Dog-Praise Poem) (Jñāneśvara of Davuldeṇa)

[Use when]

Use when deciding who to trust, hire, or befriend. Use when weighing a large commitment on someone whose 'goodness' you've only seen in words. Use when you want a portable test of character rather than a resume.

[Why it matters]

It gives a cheap, reliable character probe that money and status cannot game. Wealth buys ingratitude as easily as it buys loyalty, but a small favor with no strings is something only a grateful person converts into loyalty. It also demotes the usual proxies (rank, riches, lineage) and replaces them with one observable behavior.
