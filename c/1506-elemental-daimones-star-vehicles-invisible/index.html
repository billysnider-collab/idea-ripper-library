There are other Demons likewise, which a person might call created gods, according to each of the Elements. Some are visible, others invisible, in Aether (hot air) and Fire, and in Air (cold air) and Water; so that no part of the World is without a share of life, nor of a living being superior to the nature of man. To these are committed all under the Moon, and upon the Earth... and from whom are rumours, and voices (from heaven), and dreams, and oracles, and whatever is made an art of by mortals in the way of prophecy... [T]he artificer of the Universe sent down upon earth the souls of this race, equal in number to the stars; and after he had placed each soul in a star, as in a vehicle connected with it, he did, in order that he might be without blame, lay down laws, fixed by Fate, after the manner of a Law-giver ; that from the body should arise mortal affections, first, sensations, then pleasure and pain, and fear and anger, and that the souls, which obtained a mastery over these (feelings), and were not controlled by them, should live justly, and arrive at the star, connected with them... The gods then, in a leading manner, moulded man from Earth and Fire and Air and Water, after borrowing certain portions with the view of repaying them. And, after putting them together with invisible bolts, they worked out some one body, and bound the master portion of the soul, sent down to the head, after placing as a substratum the brain in the manner of a ploughed field... — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Chs. 15–17, trans. [Why it matters]

To ensure no zone of the universe lacks higher life, created elemental *daimones* administer the sublunary world and all divination. Meanwhile, the supreme God sows immortal human souls into companion star-vehicles under karmic laws of mastery and return, while the younger gods rivet mortal bodies together from borrowed elemental loans. Alcinous completes the descent from the highest First Principle down to human anatomy through a strict delegation of cosmic labor. First, by the **Principle of Plenitude**, no region of the cosmos can be left devoid of rational life superior to man. Therefore, beneath the planetary gods, each sublunary element (Aether, Fire, Air, Water) is populated by visible and invisible *daimones*—ontological intermediaries and "created gods" who administer everything below the Moon in imitation of the Father and operate the entire machinery of divination (omens, voices, dreams, and prophetic arts). Second, why don't the supreme Father's own hands mold the human body and the lower animals? Because **whatever the supreme God fabricates directly must be immortal**. To keep mortal creatures mortal—and to remain blameless for human moral failings—the Father manufactures only the immortal "master portion" of the human soul, assigns each soul to a companion star as its native *vehicle* (*ochema*), and proclaims the invariant laws of Fate: embodiment will necessarily generate four primal passions (pleasure, pain, fear, anger); souls that master these passions return to their native star, while souls conquered by injustice transmigrate downward. He then delegates the physical fabrication of man to his "offsprings" (the younger, celestial/daimonic gods). These younger gods **borrow small portions of Earth, Fire, Air, and Water as a temporary loan to be repaid at death**, riveting them together with "invisible bolts" (sub-microscopic geometric fasteners), planting the divine rational soul in the ploughed field of the brain at the top of the head, and constructing the marrow, bone, sinews, flesh, and viscera out of smooth and fermented elemental triangles. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the Renaissance occult and Platonic synthesis (Ficino, Agrippa, Pico), Chapters 15–17 are foundational texts for elemental demonology, the astral body, and occult medicine. First, Alcinous’s classification of elemental *daimones* distributed across Aether, Fire, Air, and Water—who mediate all dreams, oracles, and prophetic voices—is the direct classical source for the Renaissance hierarchy of elemental spirits (later popularized by Paracelsus and Agrippa). Second, as Burges (1854) notes, Ficino translates the star-assignment as *"quamlibet antea cognato astro, tamquam vehiculo"* ("each soul previously placed in its cognate star, as in a vehicle"). In Neoplatonic and Florentine esotericism, this *ochema* or **Star-Vehicle** is the luminous, subtle body (*corpus aethereum*) acquired by the soul in heaven before its descent into flesh. Every human soul has a specific "cognate star" (*astrum cognatum*) that governs its innate genius; the goal of theurgy and contemplative virtue is to master the four elemental passions so the soul can re-ascend in its purified vehicle to its native star. Meanwhile, the physical body is recognized as a temporary, bolted-together **elemental debt** borrowed from the planetary and elemental rulers, which must be dissolved and paid back with interest when the soul departs.
