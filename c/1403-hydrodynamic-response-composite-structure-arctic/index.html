Card #c1403IR // #c1403Synthesis
In tow-tank tests of composite hull plates in ice, the conventional hull form performed worst — with no bow wave it took violent direct ice impacts, and ice was forced under the hull toward the propellers — while the tumblehome form (Zumwalt-style) shed ice around the hull with the lowest increase in peak force. Small craft without bulbous bows face exactly this flow pattern in Arctic sea lanes.

— Source: Hydrodynamic response of a composite structure in an Arctic environment (Scott H. Bolstad)

[Use when]

Test the shape, not the material — in a new environment, geometry decides before strength does.

[Why it matters]

As Arctic sea lanes open, navies want small composite-hulled craft (like the M80 Stiletto) operating in ice — this is one of the few experimental answers to which hull shape survives it, and it validates a cheap LDPE ice-equivalent method that skips energy-hungry freezing ice tanks.
