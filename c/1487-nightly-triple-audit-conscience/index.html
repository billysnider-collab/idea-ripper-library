: PERFECTION Let not sleep e'er close thy tired eyes Without thou ask thyself: What have I omitted and what done ? (22). Abstain thou if 'tis evil; persevere if good (23). Meditate upon my counsels; love them; follow them; To the divine virtues will they know how to lead thee (24). — *The Golden Verses of Pythagoras*, Perfection, trans. Nayan Louise Redfield (1917), p. 119. **Surface Gloss:** Never go to sleep before reviewing every action of your day in sequence: asking where you transgressed, what you accomplished, and what duty you left undone, rebuking yourself for errors and rejoicing in the good. **The Pre-Sleep Triple Replay:** Never let your brain compile the day's code into long-term memory while dirty. Before sleep, replay the day chronologically from morning to night and run the three-part query: (1) *Where did I cross a line?* (2) *What did I actually ship?* (3) *What required duty did I drop?* Feel the sting of the errors, savour the wins, and wake up recalibrated.

[Use when]

Building a high-performance evening shutdown routine, improving episodic memory, or closing the gap between your stated values and daily execution. ---

[Why it matters]

Never go to sleep before reviewing every action of your day in sequence: asking where you transgressed, what you accomplished, and what duty you left undone, rebuking yourself for errors and rejoicing in the good. This marks the threshold of *Teleiotēs* (Perfection). In the facing Greek (p. 118), the initiate must not welcome sleep onto soft eyes before going over each of the day's deeds **three times** (*tris hekaston epelthein*) from start to finish, asking three diagnostic questions: 1. *Pēi parebēn?* (Where did I overstep/transgress the line?) 2. *Ti d' erexa?* (What did I actually do/accomplish?) 3. *Ti moi deon ouk etelesthē?* (What duty of mine was left unfulfilled?) This practice served two functions in the Pythagorean school: first, **mnemonic training** (Pythagoreans systematically exercised exact sequential recall of every daily event to strengthen the soul's retention across incarnations); second, **affective conditioning**—applying sharp internal rebuke (*epiplēsse0*) to base actions and active joy (*terpou*) to noble ones. This daily feedback loop places the practitioner directly "into the footsteps of divine virtue." [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Ficino, Pico, and later Giordano Bruno revered this nightly Pythagorean examination as the preparation for prophetic dream-vision and astral ascent during sleep. In Neoplatonic psychology, when the physical senses shut down in sleep, the rational soul (*nous*) is freed to commune with higher intelligences—provided it is not dragged down by the unexamined dross and passions of the waking day. By auditing the day three times in reverse or chronological order before closing the eyes, the initiate untangles his astral body from daytime karmic knots, clearing the mirror of the imagination to receive divine oracles in the night.
