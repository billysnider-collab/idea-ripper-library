IV. Satyrus tells us that he used to say that he had been present when Empedocles was practising magic; and that he professes this science, and many others too in his poems when he says:— / And all the drugs which can relieve disease, / Or soften the approach of age, shall be / Revealed to your inquiries; I do know them, / And I to you alone will them disclose. / You shall restrain the fierce unbridled winds, [...] when you give the word / Hell shall restore its dead. / V. And Timæus, in his eighteenth book, says, that this man was held in great esteem on many accounts; for that once, when the etesian gales were blowing violently, so as to injure the crops, he ordered some asses to be flayed, and some bladders to be made of their hides, and these he placed on the hills and high places to catch the wind. And so, when the wind ceased, he was called wind-forbidder (κωλυσανέμας). [...] XI. [...] But Hippobotus says that he rose up and went away as if he were going to mount Ætna; and that when he arrived at the crater of fire he leaped in, and disappeared, wishing to establish a belief that he had become a God. But afterwards the truth was detected by one of his slippers having been dropped. For he used to wear slippers with brazen soles. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book VIII, Sections IV–V, XI, trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Empedocles of Agrigentum combined democratic statesmanship and natural engineering (catching crop-destroying winds with ass-hide bags, sweetening pestilential rivers) with theatrical self-deification, culminating in the legend that he leapt into Etna to vanish as a god, only for the volcano to spit back his bronze sandal. **Pair Engineering Fixes with Mythic Staging (But Watch the Bronze Sandal):** When solving a massive civic or organizational crisis (sweetening a poisoned river, blocking a destructive gale), pure technocratic competence is often ignored; wrapping genuine physical engineering in unforgettable theatrical presence ("purple robe and Delphic garland") commands instant public compliance. Just never fake your exit—the volcano always spits back one bronze shoe.

[Why it matters]

Empedocles of Agrigentum combined democratic statesmanship and natural engineering (catching crop-destroying winds with ass-hide bags, sweetening pestilential rivers) with theatrical self-deification, culminating in the legend that he leapt into Etna to vanish as a god, only for the volcano to spit back his bronze sandal. Diogenes’ *Life of Empedocles* exposes the exact fault-line in fifth-century BCE Sicily between the archaic *iatromantis* (healer-prophet/wonder-worker) and the rational *physikos* (natural philosopher) and democratic reformer. Look at how Diogenes’ sources split every miracle into two competing registers: 1. **The Theurgic Register (Empedocles' own hexameters + Heraclides Ponticus):** Empedocles walks through Agrigentum in purple robes, golden fillets, Delphic garlands, and bronze-soled sandals, declaring "I, an immortal God, no longer mortal, now live among you" (VIII.6), promising Pausanias mastery over winds, rain, and the dead, preserving a breathless woman for thirty days, and vanishing in a midnight blaze of light. 2. **The Physical/Political Register (Timaeus + Diodorus + Hippobotus):** He is a radical anti-tyrannical democrat who executes oligarchic dinner-bullies (VIII.9), breaks up the Council of a Thousand,sweetens Selinus’s plague-river by diverting two fresh streams into its channel at his own expense, blocks the Etesian gales with hill-top wind-barriers of stretched skins, and either dies in Peloponnesian exile or has his staged apotheosis exposed when Etna ejects his bronze sandal. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In Renaissance natural magic (Pico, Agrippa, Campanella), the tension between Empedocles' miracles and his physical engineering disappears: *magia naturalis* is precisely the manipulation of the four elemental roots—Jove (fire), Juno (earth), Pluto (air), and Nestis (water), bound by Friendship and parted by Discord (VIII.12)—through their natural sympathies and antipathies. Diverting two living rivers to heal a fetid stream or stretching hides on ridges to break a gale is elemental medicine applied to a landscape, while preserving the breathless woman (*apnous*) for thirty days demonstrates mastery over the subtle etheric tie before putrefaction sets in. Yet the bronze sandal cast up by Etna remains the permanent emblem of failed or hubristic theurgy: fire consumes the mortal flesh, but the heavy metallic pride of the theatrical magus cannot be digested by the element and is thrown back as evidence.
