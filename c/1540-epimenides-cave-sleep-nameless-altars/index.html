He once, when he was sent by his father into the fields to look for a sheep, turned out of the road at mid-day and lay down in a certain cave and fell asleep, and slept there fifty-seven years... and when he was recognized he was considered by the Greeks as a person especially beloved by the Gods, on which account when the Athenians were afflicted by a plague, and the priestess at Delphi enjoined them to purify their city, they sent a ship... to invite Epimenides to Athens; and he, coming there in the forty-sixth Olympiad, purified the city and eradicated the plague for that time; he took some black sheep and some white ones and led them up to the Areopagus, and from thence he let them wherever they chose, having ordered the attendants to follow them, and wherever any one of them lay down they were to sacrifice him to the God who was the patron of the spot, and so the evil was stayed; and owing to this one may even now find in the different boroughs of the Athenians altars without names... — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives*, Book I, Epimenides II–III (trans. [Why it matters]

After sleeping fifty-seven years in a Cretan cave, Epimenides is summoned to cure an Athenian plague; he releases black and white sheep from the Areopagus, sacrificing each wherever it lies down to the unnamed local deity of that spot. With Epimenides of Knossos, Diogenes introduces the iatromantis (healer-seer) and ritual purifier (*kathartes*) into the genealogy of wisdom. Every detail of his biography follows archaic Greek shamanic mechanics: turning aside at *mid-day* (the hour of epiphany when chthonic and rustic powers are active), entering a Cretan cave (the initiatory womb associated with the Curetes and Dictaean Zeus), undergoing a fifty-seven-year incubation sleep that severs him from ordinary generational time, and subsisting on minute magical rations stored in a bull's hoof so he never excretes (Section X—a body freed from mortal metabolic decay). When Athens is paralyzed by *miasma* (ritual pollution from the Cylonian blood-guilt), Solon's rational statutes cannot touch the panic. Epimenides solves the crisis by **offloading diagnostic agency to the animals**: releasing black (chthonic) and white (ouranian) sheep from the homicide court (Areopagus) and erecting "altars without names" (*bomoi anonymoi*) wherever a sheep beds down. He bypasses theological debate over *which* god is angry by letting the animal's rest-point define the sacred site and addressing the local power generically. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In the Ficinian and Orphic-Pythagorean reception, Epimenides is a key link in the Cretan-Pythagorean chain (tradition held that Pythagoras later descended into the same Cretan cave with Epimenides). The fifty-seven-year sleep is read not as biological coma, but as *ecstasis*—the prolonged withdrawal of the rational soul from the senses into the caves of the intelligible world, where it converses with the Nymphs (the generative powers of the World-Soul) and feeds on ambrosial intellect rather than coarse matter, leaving no excremental residue. The "altars without names" are celebrated by Renaissance Christian Platonists (who inevitably connected them to the Athenian altar "To the Unknown God") as the supreme theurgic recognition of the ineffable Deity who stands behind all local *daimones* and exceeds every human name.
