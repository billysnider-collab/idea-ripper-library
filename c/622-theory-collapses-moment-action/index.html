Card #c622IR // #c622Synthesis

A rationalization can survive any amount of thinking and still fail the one test that matters: doing it. Raskolnikov's theory was flawless on paper, but the act exposed it — he didn't 'overstep,' he only killed. 'I didn't kill a human being, but a principle! I killed the principle, but I didn't overstep, I stopped on this side.... I was only capable of killing.' The ideology promised transcendence and delivered only a crime. Judge any doctrine not by its argument but by what it actually makes you capable of doing.

— In our words · Source: Crime and Punishment (Fyodor Dostoevsky)

[Use when]

Use when deciding whether to trust an ideology — ask what its believers actually do, not what they say. Use when you notice a gap between a plan's grand rationale and the small, sordid act it actually produces. Use when auditing yourself: does the deed match the doctrine, or only the crime?

[Why it matters]

This is the theory collapsing under its own psychological weight — exactly what the novel is built to show. The distance between the idea ('overstep, become Napoleon') and the deed ('I was only capable of killing') is the whole tragedy. It teaches that the real test of a worldview is execution, not articulation: a theory you can state but not live is already dead.
