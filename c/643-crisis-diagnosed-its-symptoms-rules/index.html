Card #c643IR // #c643Synthesis

A framework doesn't collapse cleanly — it enters 'crisis,' and the tell is behavioral, not a single decisive event. The rules loosen, people will 'try anything,' explicit discontent surfaces, and the field splinters into competing versions of itself. You diagnose crisis by its symptoms: first-principles arguments, contradictory 'fixes,' and open complaint.

— In our words · Source: The Structure of Scientific Revolutions (Thomas S. Kuhn)

[Use when]

Use to read a company or movement in trouble: if everyone is suddenly debating first principles, proposing contradictory 'fixes,' and openly complaining, you are in crisis — the old way has lost its grip. Use to distinguish 'in crisis' from 'hard times': crisis means the shared framework itself is being questioned, not just the results.

[Why it matters]

Kuhn lists the symptoms of the transition from normal to extraordinary research: 'the proliferation of competing articulations, the willingness to try anything, the expression of explicit discontent, the recourse to philosophy and to debate over fundamentals.' And separately: 'proliferation of versions of a theory is a very usual symptom of crisis.' The framework in crisis loses its grip on what counts as a valid move, so everything becomes a candidate move.
