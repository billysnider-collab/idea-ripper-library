From whence we must conceive that these virtues follow (each other) in turn. For as Fortitude is preservative of a lawful dogma, so it is of right reason. ... Now no one is able to see what is good, while it is rendered obscure by cowardice, and the feelings that follow upon cowardice. And nearly in the same manner a person is unable to act with prudence in union with intemperance, or generally through being subdued by any feeling. ... The perfect virtues therefore are thus inseparable from each other. ... And we must consider that some Virtues lead and others follow; and that the leaders are those, which are in the (portion), influenced by reason, from whom the rest obtain their perfection; but the followers are those in the portion affected by suffering; for these work out what is right, not according to the reason that is in them—for they have it not—but according to that, which is bestowed upon them by Prudence, (and) generated by custom and practice. ... Prudence however, as being a science, imparts to each (subordinate virtue) its own peculiarity, just as the pilot gives to the sailors certain orders, not contemplated by them, and they obey him. — Alcinous, *Handbook of Platonism*, Chs. 28–29, trans. George Burges (1854), pp. 300–303. *Surface Gloss:* Perfect virtues are mutually inseparable: you cannot be prudent while cowardly or intemperate, nor truly brave without prudence. Yet within the soul, rational Prudence is the only true science; the passionate virtues cannot be taught, obeying Prudence like sailors following a pilot's uncomprehended orders. **The Pilot-and-Deckhand Architecture (Mutual Lock + Asymmetric Comprehension):** High-level competence is an all-or-nothing circuit: your strategic clarity (Prudence) is only as good as your nerve under fire (Fortitude) and your impulse control (Temperance)—if one breaks, it blinds the others. Yet your lower habits don't need to "understand" the strategy; they just need to be drilled until they obey the pilot's call on contact.

[Use when]

Evaluating executive candidates who claim "brilliant judgment" despite glaring personal vices, or designing training systems that separate strategic comprehension (for the helm) from drilled execution (for the deck). ---

[Why it matters]

Alcinous executes a delicate synthesis between Stoic rigorism and Platonic-Aristotelian tripartition. First, he distinguishes imperfect "natural qualities" (*euphyiai*—e.g., reckless soldiers called "brave" by analogy) from **Perfect Virtues** (*teleiai aretai*). Perfect virtues admit of no degree (neither extension nor remission, *epitasis* nor *anesis*—they are absolute straight lines) and exhibit **mutual entailment** (*antakolouthia*, "following each other in turn"): if you have one perfect virtue, you must have them all. Why? Because Fortitude is defined (*Republic* 429c) as "the preservation of a lawful dogma" concerning what is terrible; a lawful dogma is right reason; and right reason comes only from Prudence. Conversely, Prudence cannot see the good if cowardice or intemperance fogs the lens. Second, he explains *how* this unity operates across a split soul: only the rational part possesses theoretical science (*episteme* and *theorema*). The virtues of the passionate part (Fortitude and Temperance) have no internal reason of their own and *cannot be taught* (*ouk didaktai*); they achieve perfection strictly as "follower" virtues, executing habits drilled into them by Prudence, exactly as deckhands execute the navigation orders of a ship's pilot (*kybernetes*) without understanding celestial navigation themselves. [INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Burges (1854) notes that Ficino translated *antakolouthia* as *conjugatione quadam se invicem consequi* ("by a certain conjugation they follow each other in turn") and rendered the sailors' state as obeying commands *minime inspecta* ("not at all inspected/beheld by them"). In the esoteric reception (Macrobius, Ficino, and the Renaissance emblem tradition of the *Ship of the Soul*), the "Conjugation of the Virtues" is the seal of the adept. A man who appears brave in battle but is a slave to lust in his tent does not possess the *virtue* of Fortitude at all—only an animal temperament (*dotes naturae*). True virtue is a fourfold harmonic chord (the *Tetraktys* of the soul: Prudence, Fortitude, Temperance, Justice) that either rings as a single indivisible whole or shatters completely. Within this sacred vessel, the lower powers must be reduced to silent, automatic liturgical obedience to the Noetic Pilot, acting rightly without demanding to understand the mysteries of the helm.
