A forager–farmer frontier on the Bothnian Arc persisted for over four millennia after agriculture arrived in the 4th–3rd millennia BCE, with subsistence ideologies — not geography alone — dividing communities; post-glacial land uplift was itself an active agent shaping how people mapped their world.

— In our words · Source: Local Communities of the Bothnian Arc in a Prehistoric World (Aki Hakonen)

[Use when]

A better technology doesn't automatically win — ideologies of subsistence can hold a frontier open for millennia.

[Why it matters]

Four thousand years of neighbors living differently side by side undercuts the assumption that new technologies inevitably displace old ways of life — transitions can stay stubbornly plural.
