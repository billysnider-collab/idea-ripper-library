And let us speak first of Matter. This then he calls a mould, that receives every impression, and a nurse, and a mother, and a space, and a thing subjective and tangible, (and) without sensation, and to be apprehended by spurious reasoning ; and that it possesses a peculiarity of such a kind, that it receives all creations, and has the reputation of a nurse by nourishing them, and admits all forms, being itself without Figure, and Quality, and Species, but moulded into such, and fashioned, as if it were a mould, and put into a form by them, possessing no peculiar figure or quality. For there would not be any thing properly prepared for various configurations and forms, unless it were itself without Quality, and not partaking of these species, which it must receive. For we see that those, who prepare sweet-smelling ointments from oil, make use of the most sweet-scented ; and those, who are desirous of fabricating figures from wax or clay, smooth down those substances, and render them shapeless in order that they may receive (new shapes)... and being such, it would be neither a body nor without a body, but a body in posse, as we understand of copper, that it is a statue in posse, because, after having received the form, it will become a statue. — Alcinous, *Handbook*, Ch. 8, trans. George Burges (1854) **Surface Gloss:** Primary Matter is the utterly formless, qualityless receptacle and space that receives all physical shapes. Like odorless base oil used by perfumers or smoothed-down wax, it must lack every quality of its own so it can take on any form, existing as body only potentially (*in posse*). *(Note: Burges’s text prints "most sweet-scented" where his footnote corrects the Greek to "most without scent.")* The **Perfumer’s Base Rule**: a medium’s capacity to accurately host, transmit, or adapt to diverse signals is inversely proportional to how much "native scent" (rigid bias, pre-baked style, or structural baggage) it carries. [Why it matters]

Primary Matter is the utterly formless, qualityless receptacle and space that receives all physical shapes. Like odorless base oil used by perfumers or smoothed-down wax, it must lack every quality of its own so it can take on any form, existing as body only potentially (*in posse*). *(Note: Burges’s text prints "most sweet-scented" where his footnote corrects the Greek to "most without scent.")* Embarking on Theology (the study of the Three Principles), Alcinous begins from the bottom with Primary Matter (*hule*). Combining the receptacle-space of the *Timaeus* with the Aristotelian doctrine of potentiality, he defines Matter as the absolute substrate of physical becoming. If a medium is going to receive every possible physical shape, temperature, and color without distorting them, it must possess zero intrinsic qualities of its own—just as perfumers select the most *odorless* base oil so it will not spoil the added fragrance, and sculptors flatten out every previous crease in their clay. Because the mind knows things through their forms, and senses perceive things through their qualities, Qualityless Matter can neither be grasped by pure intellect nor felt by the senses; it is apprehended only by "spurious reasoning" (stripping away all predicates in thought). Ontologically, it is neither actual body nor pure incorporeal spirit, but *body in posse* (potential body), waiting like uncast bronze for the imprint of form. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** Burges (1854) notes the glaring manuscript corruption in the Greek (*euosmotato*, "most sweet-scented," for *anosmotato*, "most scentless"), and points out that where the Greek says clay is smoothed down, Ficino translates *"donec figurae pristinae deleantur"* ("until the former figures are completely erased"). In Renaissance Hermetic and alchemical Platonism, this passage describes the *Prima Materia* and the state of the receptive vessel. Before a talisman, an alchemical tincture, or a human soul can receive a new celestial form from above, its "previous figures must be erased" and reduced to the condition of scentless oil or virgin wax. If the vessel retains its own stubborn egoic qualities or partisan habits, it adulterates the descending influx. Simultaneously, Matter’s apprehension by "spurious reasoning" (*adulterina ratiocinatione* in Ficino) marks the dark abyss at the base of the cosmic ladder—the mirror image of the super-essential One at the top, which is likewise beyond all form, though by excess rather than privation.
