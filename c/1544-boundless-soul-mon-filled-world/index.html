“The following may be set down in a general manner as his main principles: that everything is created from fire, and is dissolved into fire; that everything happens according to destiny, and that all existing things are harmonized, and made to agree together by opposite tendencies; and that all things are full of souls and dæmones. He also discussed all the passions which exist in the world, and used also to contend that the sun was of that precise magnitude of which he appears to be. One of his sayings too was, that no one, by whatever road he might travel, could ever possibly find out the boundaries of the soul, so deeply hidden are the principles which regulate it. He used also to call opinion the sacred disease; and to say that eye-sight was often deceived.” — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book IX, *Life of Heraclitus*, VI (trans. C. D. Yonge, 1853) **Surface Gloss:** The cosmos is saturated with souls and intermediary *dæmones*, while the human soul itself has no discoverable spatial boundary because its governing principle is unfathomably deep; ordinary human opinion is a form of epilepsy. **Opinion as Cognitive Epilepsy**: Treat unexamined certainty ("opinion") not as a minor intellectual error, but as a seizure that severs the mind from shared reality. Conversely, treat the mind's capacity for structural insight as non-local and boundless: you never hit the "edge" of understanding by digging deeper into the governing principles of a problem, only by stopping at surface impressions.

[Why it matters]

The cosmos is saturated with souls and intermediary *dæmones*, while the human soul itself has no discoverable spatial boundary because its governing principle is unfathomably deep; ordinary human opinion is a form of epilepsy. In Heraclitus' Ionian framework (inheriting and radicalizing Thales' maxim that all things are full of gods), the cosmos is not dead matter moved from outside; it is continuous living fire, and therefore "full of souls and dæmones"—where *dæmones* are ontological intermediaries and local concentrations of the fiery, intelligent life-principle, never evil spirits. Because the human soul (*psychē*) is itself a bright, warm exhalation continuous with the cosmic fire and its steering *logos* ("the principles which regulate it"), it cannot be mapped like a physical territory: "no one, by whatever road he might travel, could ever possibly find out the boundaries of the soul." Against this deep, self-regulating interiority stands human "opinion" (*oiēsis*—smug, unexamined private belief), which Heraclitus diagnoses as "the sacred disease" (epilepsy): a sudden seizure that drops a person out of shared waking reality into private convulsion. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** To Ficino and the Renaissance magi, Diogenes' pairing of "all things are full of souls and dæmones" with the unfathomable depth of the soul was a cornerstone text for natural and sympathetic magic. In the Neoplatonic hierarchy, *dæmones* are the aerial and fiery intermediary beings that transmit celestial influences down into terrestrial matter, ensuring that no corner of nature is devoid of sentient presence. Meanwhile, the soul's lack of boundaries proved its divine, non-spatial nature: because the human soul contains the *rationes* (principles) of all things in its depths, traveling inward along any road leads directly into the infinite *Logos*. Private "opinion" is the epileptic seizure of the fallen imagination, cutting the soul off from theliving, dæmon-threaded cosmos and trapping it in the hallucinations of unassisted eyesight.
