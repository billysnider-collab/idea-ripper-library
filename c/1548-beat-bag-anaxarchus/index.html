[Why it matters]

Captured by the Cypriot tyrant Nicocreon and pounded to death in a stone mortar with iron pestles, the philosopher Anaxarchus mocks the tyrant by declaring that the pestles only crush his outer "bag," not himself, and spits his own bitten-off tongue in the tyrant's face. Anaxarchus of Abdera sits in Diogenes' succession as the crucial bridge between Democritean atomism and Pyrrhonian scepticism (he was Pyrrho's direct master on Alexander's eastern campaign). Known as *Eudaimonikos* ("the Happy") for his unshakeable evenness of temper, Anaxarchus operationalizes extreme epistemological scepticism—inherited from Metrodorus of Chios, "who said that he was not even sure that he knew nothing"—as total physical and political invulnerability. Whether pricking Alexander's delusion of divinity by pointing to his mortal blood, or enduring Nicocreon's iron pestles, Anaxarchus treats the physical body as a mere *thylakos* ("bag" or leather sack) housing the autonomous philosophical self. By severing his own tongue and spitting the organ of speech back at the tyrant, he converts the tyrant's ultimate instrument of torture into a final demonstration that external force can only pulverize the container, never the governing disposition. **[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC]** In Platonic, Hermetic, and Renaissance emblem traditions, Anaxarchus' "bag" (*thylakos*) was immediately assimilated to the Orphic and Platonic doctrine of the body as the prison (*sēma*), tunic (*chitōn*), or leather sack of the immortal soul—a parallel Diogenes himself invites in his epigram ("Beat, Anaxarchus is in heav'n with Jove"). For Renaissance humanists and Neoplatonists, Anaxarchus and Zeno the Eleatic (who performs the same tongue-spitting defiance in Book IX, *Zeno*, V) were secular martyrs of the *mens* (the higher intellect): when the philosopher detaches his identity completely from the elemental vehicle, even the alchemical *mortarium* (the mortar and pestle of Saturnian Nigredo and torture) only breaks the gross husk and liberates the pneumatic spark.
