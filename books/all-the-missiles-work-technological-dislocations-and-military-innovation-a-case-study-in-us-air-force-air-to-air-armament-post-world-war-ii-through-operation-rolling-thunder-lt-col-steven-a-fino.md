## Steal

The USAF's air-to-air armament history reveals "technological dislocations": an unwavering commitment to existing technologies plus fascination with the promise of new ones blinds an institution to a changing strategic environment. The prescriptions: market new tech with a keen marketing strategy to outmaneuver bureaucratic skepticism, prefer innovative systems integration over outright systems acquisition, and rely on credible innovators and courageous commanders willing to act on their subordinates' recommendations.

## Why it matters

A theory of technological dislocation built from the USAF's missile-era failures that reads as a manual for the current drone/AI military-innovation wave — the same bureaucratic dynamics repeat with every new weapon class.

## Use when

Innovation dies in the middle — incumbents committed to the old plus dreamers chasing the new both miss the environment; integration beats acquisition.
