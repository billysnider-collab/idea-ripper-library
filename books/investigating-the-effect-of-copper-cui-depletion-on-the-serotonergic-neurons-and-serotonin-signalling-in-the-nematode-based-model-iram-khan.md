## Steal

Depleting copper (CuI) with tetrathiomolybdate in C. elegans selectively destroys serotonergic HSN neurons — broken axonal extensions, degraded egg-laying from impaired serotonin release to the vulval muscles — while pharyngeal ADF/NSM neurons stay structurally and functionally intact.

## Why it matters

It demonstrates differential susceptibility in a clean model — the same trace-element deficiency destroys one neuron population and leaves another untouched, mirroring how Menkes disease patients show selective neurodegeneration.

## Use when

Vulnerability is cell-type specific — a toxin's damage map tells you more about the system's architecture than about the toxin itself.
