Seizing the Digital High Ground: Military Operations and Politics in the Social Media Era

author not named in IA metadata · Thesis · 1 rips

Masters thesis — National Defense University (Joint Advanced Warfighting School), 2015. Full text: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA622701

Unless the military vastly improves its understanding of social media's utility and invests more in applying it, it risks losing relevance and becoming a blunt instrument for executing political aims and providing security in the 21st century; the British military's current doctrinal view is limited, best characterised as caution, with opinion divided on relative threats and opportunities.

Why it matters: It names the mechanism of modern information warfare: social media has decisively altered the speed, scrutiny, and sensitivity of the military's operational environment — imposing values and manipulating the news is increasingly difficult for governments and Great Powers.

Treat a new information environment as a threat to be policed and you fall behind; treat it as terrain to be seized and you stay relevant — caution is a doctrine, not a strategy.
