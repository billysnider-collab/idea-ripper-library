The Botanical Law of Cruelty

Crush humanity out of shape once more, under similar hammers, and it will twist itself into the same tortured forms. Sow the same seed of rapacious license and oppression over again, and it will surely yield the same fruit according to its kind.

— In our words · Source: A Tale of Two Cities (Charles Dickens)

[Use when]

Executives cut wages to the bone and mock worker complaints. Warn the board that the staff is learning to sabotage the firm.

[Why it matters]

Rulers think terrified silence means the crowd is broken forever. Instead, every brutal rule teaches the underclass how to act when power flips. The bosses eventually pay in blood to monsters they trained themselves.

The Blade Eats Its Makers

I see a beautiful city and a brilliant people rising from this abyss, and, in their struggles to be truly free, in their triumphs and defeats, through long years to come, I see the evil of this time and of the previous time of which this is the natural birth, gradually making expiation for itself and wearing out.

— Source: A Tale of Two Cities (Charles Dickens)

[Use when]

A newly promoted boss plans a public bloodbath to fix company culture. Point out that the survivors will spend years fighting each other in fear.

[Why it matters]

Condemned witnesses see what angry mobs miss: violence never cleans a room fast. Instead, terror poisons the air for decades after the tyrants die. Two generations pay in grief before the fever finally burns out.

The Quota Numbness

When severe punishment becomes a high-volume daily schedule, it loses all power to scare people; staff treat human ruin as a math quota while bystanders turn it into dark jokes.

[Why it matters]
