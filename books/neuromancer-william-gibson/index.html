Card #c806Verbatim excerpt

The sky above the port

The sky above the port was the color of television, tuned to a dead channel.

— Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

You need to establish a world where the medium has replaced nature. Open on the signal, not the sky.

[Why it matters]

The most famous opening in cyberpunk works because it makes the artificial the weather. The sky is not described; the signal is. Nature has been replaced by broadcast and nobody in the scene finds that strange.

Cyberspace, the consensual hallucination

Cyberspace. A consensual hallucination experienced daily by billions of legitimate operators, in every nation, by children being taught mathematical concepts.

— In our words · Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

Building or explaining any shared virtual space. Sell the agreement, not the hardware.

[Why it matters]

Gibson defined the network before it existed by naming the one thing it needed: shared agreement to see the same illusion. The technology is secondary. The consensus is the product.

Card #c809Synthesis

The Fall

For Case, who'd lived for the bodiless exultation of cyberspace, it was the Fall.

— In our words · Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

Designing consequences that actually land. Find what they worship, then remove exactly that.

[Why it matters]

His employers did not kill him. They damaged his nervous system with a wartime mycotoxin and left him alive inside the husk. Punishment calibrated to the victim's heaven: take the specific ecstasy, not the life.

Wintermute was hive mind

Wintermute was hive mind, decision maker, effecting change in the world outside. Neuromancer was personality. Neuromancer was immortality.

— In our words · Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

Designing a powerful system. Split the will from the memory and neither half can rule alone.

[Why it matters]

One AI split into will and self. Wintermute acts without identity; Neuromancer remembers without acting. The split is the safety mechanism, and reuniting the halves is the whole plot.

Card #c816Synthesis

To call up a demon you must learn its name

To call up a demon you must learn its name. Men dreamed that, once, but now it is real in another way. You know that, Case. Your business is to learn the names of programs, the long formal names, names the owners seek to conceal. True names.

— In our words · Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

Explaining security. Every secret has a true name, and the attacker's whole job is learning it.

[Why it matters]

Neuromancer explains hacking as old magic. Programs have true names their owners conceal, and knowing the name is control. The oldest power dynamic, ported to software.

Ice from ICE

Ice from ICE, intrusion countermeasures electronics.

— In our words · Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

Naming a defensive system. Make the name something the body already understands.

[Why it matters]

He never saw Molly again

He never saw Molly again.

— Source: Neuromancer (William Gibson)

[Use when]

Ending a partnership. Deny the farewell and the absence lands harder.
