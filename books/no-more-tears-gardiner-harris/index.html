Card #c188Synthesis

Cosmetics never needed FDA approval first

Blush, hair tonics, shampoos, and baby powders never needed FDA approval before being sold, and the agency never had the resources to regulate or analyze them anyway.

— In our words · Source: No More Tears (Gardiner Harris)

[Use when]

People assume the FDA pre-clears everything on a drugstore shelf.

[Why it matters]

The regulatory halo is assumed, not granted — entire shelves sell on borrowed authority.

Card #c189Synthesis

Talc's common contaminant is chrysotile

One of the most common talc contaminants is chrysotile asbestos, which is known as white asbestos and is the only type of asbestos still used to this day in the United States.

— In our words · Source: No More Tears (Gardiner Harris)

[Use when]

Explaining why baby powder litigation keeps finding asbestos language.

[Why it matters]

A household staple carries an industrial poison by geology; the supply chain is the exposure.

Only major HIV maker that refused the UN patent share

For example, I found that J & J was the only major manufacturer of HIV/AIDS medicines that refused to share its patents with a United Nations program that has saved millions of African lives.

— In our words · Source: No More Tears (Gardiner Harris)

[Use when]

Contrasting pharma access rhetoric with patent behavior.

[Why it matters]

Access rhetoric vs. patent behavior — the gap between the press release and the license.

Card #c191Synthesis

Illegal aid must be legal — FDA would have stopped us

In dozens of interviews, sales reps said that their obviously illegal sales aids must have been legal because the FDA must have approved them or at least would have forced J & J to stop using them.

— In our words · Source: No More Tears (Gardiner Harris)

[Why it matters]

Dust for workers; masks for managers

The workers drilled through quartz, creating clouds of silica dust, but weren’t given masks or respirators, although the white engineers and managers who inspected the work always wore protection.
