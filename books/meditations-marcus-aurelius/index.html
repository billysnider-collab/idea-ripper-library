Card #c201Verbatim excerpt

The morning briefing

Betimes in the morning say to thyself, This day I shalt have to do with an idle curious man, with an unthankful man, a railer, a crafty, false, or an envious man; an unsociable uncharitable man.

— Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When you walk into a day of difficult people.

[Why it matters]

Pre-decide your response to difficult people before the day starts — the obstacle named in advance loses its ambush.

Card #c202Synthesis

The inner citadel

At what time soever thou wilt, it is in thy power to retire into thyself, and to be at rest, and free from all businesses. A man cannot any whither retire better than to his own soul; he especially who is beforehand provided of such things within, which whensoever he doth withdraw himself to look in, may presently afford unto him perfect ease and tranquillity.

— In our words · Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When escape feels like the only option.

[Why it matters]

The one retreat no circumstance can take: tranquillity as an inward order, not a place.

Card #c203Verbatim excerpt

Get up — it is your work

In the morning when thou findest thyself unwilling to rise, consider with thyself presently, it is to go about a man's work that I am stirred up.

— Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When the snooze button is winning the argument.

[Why it matters]

Duty reframed as nature: you were made for the work, not the warm bed.

Card #c204Synthesis

The soul takes the dye of its thoughts

Such as thy thoughts and ordinary cogitations are, such will thy mind be in time. For the soul doth as it were receive its tincture from the fancies, and imaginations. Dye it therefore and thoroughly soak it with the assiduity of these cogitations.

— In our words · Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When choosing what to feed your attention.

[Why it matters]

Attention is the mechanism of character: soak the mind deliberately, or it soaks up whatever surrounds it.

Card #c205Verbatim excerpt

The best revenge

The best kind of revenge is, not to become like unto them.

— Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When revenge feels righteous.

[Why it matters]

Retaliation is assimilation — the only clean victory is refusing the opponent’s shape.

Card #c206Synthesis

See things unadorned

This is the carcass of a fish; this of a bird; and this of a hog. And again more generally; This phalernum, this excellent highly commended wine, is but the bare juice of an ordinary grape. This purple robe, but sheep's hairs, dyed with the blood of a shellfish.

— In our words · Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When luxury or status is doing your thinking for you.

[Why it matters]

The Stoic stripping exercise: name things by what they physically are and the added glamour falls off.

Card #c207Synthesis

Use the impediment

So may every reasonable creature, what crosses and impediments soever it meets with in the course of this mortal life, it may use them as fit and proper objects, to the furtherance of whatsoever it intended and absolutely proposed unto itself as its natural end and happiness.

— In our words · Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When a setback looks like the end of the plan.

[Why it matters]

The obstacle as raw material: whatever blocks the action can be made the object of the action.

Card #c208Synthesis

Nobody can hinder the right act

there is nobody that can hinder thee, but that thou mayest always both do and speak those things which are agreeable to that nature, whereof thou art a part.

— In our words · Source: Meditations (Marcus Aurelius)

[Use when]

When external pressure is the excuse.

[Why it matters]

The inner-freedom claim: no one can stop you from doing and speaking what accords with your nature.
