A Marriage at Sea — Sophie Elmhirst

5 stealable ideas · 5 keep

Best stealables

Self-doubt can be someone else’s power source

passage keep

Maurice might never have seen his debilitating self-doubt as a gift, but it had given Maralyn power.

Use when: you’re writing about a weakness that became a partner’s strength — the trait he cursed was the one she ran on.

Needing no one is not an achievement

passage keep

We had found self-knowledge, self-reliance and proved our emotional self-sufficiency.” As if it were an achievement, to need no one else.

Use when: you’re puncturing the pride of total independence — the loner’s trophy nobody should want.

The calm after holds the memory of violence

connection keep

The calm before a storm claims all the attention, but the calm after is just as threatening, containing, as it does, the memory of violence.

Use when: you’re describing aftermath dread — the dangerous part isn’t the storm, it’s what the quiet now contains.

Safety first, comfort second, speed last

evidence keep

According to Hiscock, seaworthiness, comfort, and speed were the most important features, in that order.

Use when: you’re arguing for ranking priorities out loud — name the order instead of pretending everything matters equally.

Modern conveniences quietly rewrite a life

passage keep

Their life was smoothed by things they’d never owned before: central heating, a telephone, a washing machine.

Use when: you need a small, concrete image of how comfort changes people — not the revolution, the washing machine.
