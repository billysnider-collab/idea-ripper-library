Tragedy and Hope

by Carroll Quigley

17 rips · Understanding power · Seeing clearly · Deciding under uncertainty

Grand-strategy history — Quigley's 1966 classic on Western civilization's cycles, financial capitalism, the Anglo-American network, and the shape of the twentieth century.

argument #33

The West kept reforming instead of dying

Quigley's whole theory pivots here: every other civilization went expansion, crisis, universal empire, decay — the West broke the cycle by reorganizing itself, repeatedly, instead of consolidating into one empire.

Western Civilization, on the contrary, did not pass from the Age of Crisis to the Age of Universal Empire, but instead was able to reform itself and entered upon a new period of expansion. Moreover, Western Civilization did this not once, but several times. It was this ability to reform or reorganize itself again and again which made Western Civilization the dominant factor in the world at the beginning of the twentieth century.

Why it matters

Quigley's whole theory pivots here: every other civilization went expansion, crisis, universal empire, decay — the West broke the cycle by reorganizing itself, repeatedly, instead of consolidating into one empire.

Use when: When someone declares a civilization finished because its growth slowed — ask whether it is dying or reorganizing.

concept #34

The four marks of an Age of Conflict

A diagnostic checklist for civilizational stall: the economics slow first, then the politics turn violent, then the culture turns superstitious — in that order, core before periphery.

(a) it is a period of declining rate of expansion; (b) it is a period of growing tensions and class conflicts; (c) it is a period of increasingly frequent and increasingly violent imperialist wars; and (d) it is a period of growing irrationality, pessimism, superstitions, and otherworldliness.

Why it matters

A diagnostic checklist for civilizational stall: the economics slow first, then the politics turn violent, then the culture turns superstitious — in that order, core before periphery.

Use when: When growth stalls and the culture war gets weirder at the same time — check the sequence, not just the symptoms.

mechanism #35

Expansion petrifies into vested interests

The engine of every Quigley cycle: the organization that created the expansion hardens into the thing blocking the next one — profit shifts from making more to guarding what exists.

In time, this system of commercial capitalism became petrified into a structure of vested interests in which profits were sought by imposing restrictions on the production or interchange of goods rather than by encouraging these activities.

Why it matters

The engine of every Quigley cycle: the organization that created the expansion hardens into the thing blocking the next one — profit shifts from making more to guarding what exists.

Use when: When an industry's profits come from blocking entrants rather than serving customers — you are looking at petrification, not competition.

definition #36

Four capitalists, four profit sources

Quigley's cleanest taxonomy: each stage of capitalism is defined by where profit is extracted — and each stage's profit source becomes the next stage's target for capture.

The commercial capitalist sought profits from the exchange of goods; the industrial capitalist sought profits from the manufacture of goods; the financial capitalist sought profits from the manipulation of claims on money; and the monopoly capitalist sought profits from manipulation of the market to make the market price and the amount sold such that his profits would be maximized.

Why it matters

Quigley's cleanest taxonomy: each stage of capitalism is defined by where profit is extracted — and each stage's profit source becomes the next stage's target for capture.

Use when: When classifying a business or an era — ask where the profit actually comes from, not what the brochure says.

The bankers' 1914 prediction — that the war must end in months for lack of gold — exposed the core fallacy: money is the scoreboard, organization is the game.

Wars, as events have proved since, are not fought with gold or even with money, but by the proper organization of real resources.

concept #39

Four ways to pay for a war

Sixteen words that decode a century of war finance: every government's funding mix is a choice about who pays — present taxpayers, savers, or the unborn.

a. Taxation gives no inflation and no debt. b. Fiat money gives inflation and no debt. c. Bank credit gives inflation and debt. d. Sales of bonds give no inflation but give debt.

Why it matters

Sixteen words that decode a century of war finance: every government's funding mix is a choice about who pays — present taxpayers, savers, or the unborn.

Use when: When a government announces how it will fund a crisis — run it through the four cells and see who is really paying.

mechanism #40

A world system of financial control in private hands

Quigley's most-quoted claim, stated as the bankers' own program: not conspiracy theory but institutional design — private, feudal, coordinated through central banks, apexed at the BIS in Basle.

the powers of financial capitalism had another far-reaching aim, nothing less than to create a world system of financial control in private hands able to dominate the political system of each country and the economy of the world as a whole. This system was to be controlled in a feudalist fashion by the central banks of the world acting in concert, by secret agreements arrived at in frequent private meetings and conferences.

Why it matters

Quigley's most-quoted claim, stated as the bankers' own program: not conspiracy theory but institutional design — private, feudal, coordinated through central banks, apexed at the BIS in Basle.

Use when: When central bankers meet in Basle and markets move on the minutes — this is the architecture Quigley described.

mechanism #41

How central banks dominate governments

The mechanism, named with names: dominance through Treasury loans, exchange manipulation, the business cycle itself — and the revolving door as the enforcement arm.

Each central bank, in the hands of men like Montagu Norman of the Bank of England, Benjamin Strong of the New York Federal Reserve Bank, Charles Rist of the Bank of France, and Hjalmar Schacht of the Reichsbank, sought to dominate its government by its ability to control Treasury loans, to manipulate foreign exchanges, to influence the level of economic activity in the country, and to influence cooperative politicians by subsequent economic rewards in the business world.

Why it matters

The mechanism, named with names: dominance through Treasury loans, exchange manipulation, the business cycle itself — and the revolving door as the enforcement arm.

Use when: When a central banker becomes a political oracle — ask which of the four levers is doing the talking.

evidence #42

The BIS: a private bank owned by central banks

The apex of Quigley's system in one paragraph: a private bank, owned by central banks that were themselves private corporations, settling the world's money by bookkeeping instead of shipping gold.

The B.I.S. as a private institution was owned by the seven chief central banks and was operated by the heads of these, who together formed its governing board. They made agreements on all the major financial problems of the world, as well as on many of the economic and political problems, especially in reference to loans, payments, and the economic future of the chief areas of the globe.

Why it matters

The apex of Quigley's system in one paragraph: a private bank, owned by central banks that were themselves private corporations, settling the world's money by bookkeeping instead of shipping gold.

Use when: When someone says central banks are public institutions — the BIS charter says otherwise.

argument #43

Rhodes's aim: federate the English-speaking peoples

The motive behind the network, in Quigley's telling: the Rhodes Scholarships were not philanthropy but a personnel system — training an imperial ruling class under the cover of education.

These purposes centered on his desire to federate the English-speaking peoples and to bring all the habitable portions of the world under their control. For this purpose Rhodes left part of his great fortune to found the Rhodes Scholarships at Oxford in order to spread the English ruling class tradition throughout the English-speaking world as Ruskin had wanted.

Why it matters

The motive behind the network, in Quigley's telling: the Rhodes Scholarships were not philanthropy but a personnel system — training an imperial ruling class under the cover of education.

Use when: When an elite fellowship claims to be about merit — ask what ruling tradition it was built to spread.

evidence #44

The secret society of February 5, 1891

Quigley's documentary core for the network claim: a dated founding, a named executive committee, a Circle of Initiates, and an outer circle that became the Round Table — the organizational chart of the Anglo-American establishment.

This association was formally established on February 5, 1891, when Rhodes and Stead organized a secret society of which Rhodes had been dreaming for sixteen years. In this secret society Rhodes was to be leader; Stead, Brett (Lord Esher), and Milner were to form an executive committee; Arthur (Lord) Balfour, (Sir) Harry Johnston, Lord Rothschild, Albert (Lord) Grey, and others were listed as potential members of a "Circle of Initiates"; while there was to be an outer circle known as the "Association of Helpers" (later organized by Milner as the Round Table organization).

Why it matters

Quigley's documentary core for the network claim: a dated founding, a named executive committee, a Circle of Initiates, and an outer circle that became the Round Table — the organizational chart of the Anglo-American establishment.

Use when: When tracing how an idea becomes policy across decades — look for the personnel network, not just the institutions.

concept #45

Five outlooks, from Enlightenment to Inclusive Diversity

Quigley's cultural periodization: the twentieth century's new outlook is not a doctrine but a condition — diversity as the operating system, replacing the materialist certainties that ended in 1945.

Over the past two centuries, there have been five such stages: the Enlightenment in 1730-1790, the Romantic Movement in 1790-1850, the Age of Scientific Materialism in 1850-1895, the Period of Irrational Activism of 1895-1945, and our new Age of Inclusive Diversity since 1945.

Why it matters

Quigley's cultural periodization: the twentieth century's new outlook is not a doctrine but a condition — diversity as the operating system, replacing the materialist certainties that ended in 1945.

Use when: When a debate feels like two centuries talking past each other — check which outlook each side inhabits.

contradiction #46

The twentieth century began in 1950

Quigley's dating refuses the calendar: the 'twentieth century' is not 1900-1999 but the postwar order — and 1895-1950 was a 55-year transition, one of the most awful periods in all human history.

The time covered by this book seems to this historian to fall into three periods: the nineteenth century from about 1814 to about 1895; the twentieth century, which did not begin until after World War II, perhaps as late as 1950; and a long period of transition from 1895 to 1950.

Why it matters

Quigley's dating refuses the calendar: the 'twentieth century' is not 1900-1999 but the postwar order — and 1895-1950 was a 55-year transition, one of the most awful periods in all human history.

Use when: When periodizing any era — date it by the system change, not the round number.

passage #48

The nineteenth century's epitaph

Quigley refuses nostalgia for the pre-1914 world: the horrors of 1914-1945 did not come from nowhere — they were the nineteenth century's underlying evils emerging 'in all their nakedness.'

the nineteenth century was, however hopeful in its general processes, a period of materialism, selfishness, false values, hypocrisy, and secret vices.

Why it matters

Quigley refuses nostalgia for the pre-1914 world: the horrors of 1914-1945 did not come from nowhere — they were the nineteenth century's underlying evils emerging 'in all their nakedness.'

Use when: When someone romanticizes a golden age — ask what evils were working underneath it.

evidence #49

The international Anglophile network

The book's most famous admission, in Quigley's own voice: a named network — the Round Table Groups — operating internationally for a generation, studied by him for twenty years with two years of access to its papers and secret records.

There does exist, and has existed for a generation, an international Anglophile network which operates, to some extent, in the way the ... Right believes the Communists act. In fact, this network, which we may identify as the Round Table Groups, has no aversion to cooperating with the Communists, or any other groups, and frequently does so. I know of the operations of this network because I have studied it for twenty years and was permitted for two years, in the early 1960's, to examine its papers and secret records.

Why it matters

The book's most famous admission, in Quigley's own voice: a named network — the Round Table Groups — operating internationally for a generation, studied by him for twenty years with two years of access to its papers and secret records.

Use when: When the establishment is dismissed as a figure of speech — Quigley studied this one for twenty years and read its secret records.
