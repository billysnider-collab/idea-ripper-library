Courage Can Save Us

by Rye Barcott

23 rips · Hiring and leading · Negotiating · Deciding under uncertainty

Political courage — Rye Barcott profiles ten veterans in Congress and statehouses, Republicans and Democrats, on how they cut deals, defied their own parties and decided which fights to take.

mechanism #1322

Fill the reservoir of trust before you need it

Young and Chuck Schumer met on stationary bikes in the Senate gym while leading opposing campaign committees.

"But Todd had made a point of building relationships across the aisle, what he calls “a reservoir of trust and goodwill.”" "“We also discovered a mutual animus toward the Chinese Communist Party,” Todd says."

Why it matters

Young and Chuck Schumer met on stationary bikes in the Senate gym while leading opposing campaign committees. Months of small talk surfaced a shared enemy. That became the CHIPS Act, a bill to bring semiconductor manufacturing back to the United States.

Use when: You'll eventually need a deal with a rival and have no reason yet to talk to them.

Deliver an ironclad bloc

Brian Fitzpatrick sent the White House a daily whip count on the infrastructure bill.

"Brian assured him he had thirteen Republican votes, “ironclad.”" "All thirteen Republicans in Brian’s bloc held firm and provided the winning margin."

Why it matters

Brian Fitzpatrick sent the White House a daily whip count on the infrastructure bill. A small, disciplined bloc that keeps its word becomes the deciding vote. That made a handful of minority-party members essential to the president's signature bill.

Use when: You lead a small group in a close contest and could trade its unity for influence.

Arguments (1)

argument #1325

Be ready to be misunderstood by your own side

Young says the right vote is usually clear.

"This is not hard if you feel like you don’t share the values of a particular group of constituents. But it becomes very challenging—and requires what I would call moral courage—to stand your ground with people whose values you share."

Why it matters

Young says the right vote is usually clear. The hard part is holding it when people he loves and respects disagree. He sees himself as their trustee, with no duty to follow a daily opinion poll.

Use when: People you respect are pressing you to reverse a decision you still believe is right.

Concepts (1)

concept #1324

It's always easier to vote no

During the 2011 debt-ceiling standoff, many Republicans privately backed the compromise and voted against it in public.

"“It’s always easier to vote no,” says John Connell, Todd’s chief of staff for fifteen years. “But we had to compromise on stuff. He always took those votes—acknowledged their shortcomings—but approached it with kind of a realistic perspective. And he never hid the ball from his constituents.”"

Why it matters

During the 2011 debt-ceiling standoff, many Republicans privately backed the compromise and voted against it in public. Young voted yes and explained the flaws openly. Owning an imperfect deal out loud builds more credibility than a safe no.

Use when: You support a compromise that has obvious flaws and opposing it would be easier.
