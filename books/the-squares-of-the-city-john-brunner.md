## Steal

When the players in a system look irrational, stop analyzing personalities and reconstruct the game whose rules make their moves legal.

## Why it matters

Hakluyt's confusion in Vados ends when he sees the city's factions as pieces in a chess game played with real lives; the chaos becomes a sequence of legal moves. A ruleset predicts the next move better than any theory of motives.

## Use when

A reorg or market behaves "crazily": list the players, their allowed moves and what each move wins, then test whether that predicts the next event.

## Steal

Treat a polite stall as the other side's move, and ask who gains from your waiting.

## Why it matters

Hakluyt meets vagueness at every practical turn and slowly realizes the delay is deliberate: it keeps the expert idle while the real game plays out. Courteous inaction is still a move.

## Use when

A counterparty keeps pushing back "final" sign-off while a deadline that favors them gets closer.

## Steal

Before accepting a mandate, verify that the sponsor wants the problem solved rather than seen to be solved.

## Why it matters

Hakluyt, a famous foreign traffic expert, is brought in to fix a showplace city's traffic problem, but the power structure needs nothing to change, so his reputation serves as proof that something is being done. The fee is the same either way; the outcome is not.

## Use when

You're offered a high-profile advisory role where the sponsor won't give you authority, data or a success metric.
