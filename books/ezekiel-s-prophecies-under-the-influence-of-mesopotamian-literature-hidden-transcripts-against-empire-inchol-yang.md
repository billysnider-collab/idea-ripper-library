## Steal

The book of Ezekiel — long read as pro-Babylonian for never criticizing the Neo-Babylonian Empire — is actually resistance literature: it uses Mesopotamian divine-abandonment motifs and encodes criticism of Babylon through the Babylonian New Year (akitu) festival's own symbolism, announcing YHWH's temple as the world's center and YHWH's kingship over even Marduk.

## Why it matters

It's a case study in how the powerless talk back — encoding dissent in the empire's own symbols so the message passes censors while the intended audience gets it.

## Use when

When you can't attack power directly, steal its symbols — subversion works best in the language of the thing being subverted.
