## Steal

Before you prosecute the betrayal, check whether the rival is your own detached self.

## Why it matters

Codling sees his wife Mildred with a man in the rocking chair and builds the infidelity case — then the story pulls the floor out: the man is Codling himself, the part of him that drifted loose ("of course, he was upstairs with Mildred, it was himself, the other bit of him"). The jealousy was real and also wrong. The tale ends not with exposure but with a birth: "We'll call him Gabriel." Suspicions feel like evidence until the frame widens enough to include you.

## Use when

You're certain someone is working against you: ask what the scene looks like if you are the stranger in it.

## Steal

Price every free offer by finding where the cost will be collected before you accept it.

## Why it matters

Tom Toole, sixty and chasing his lost youth, holds that it is "seldom possible to get something in return for quite nothing," and that even when you do, the danger matches the delight. Something-for-nothing is a loan against undisclosed collateral.

## Use when

A vendor offers a free tier or a free migration: find the lock-in, data-rights or renewal clause before you sign.
