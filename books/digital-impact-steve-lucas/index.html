Digital Impact

by Steve Lucas

21 rips · Technology and AI · Hiring and leading · Making things

Enterprise AI — Boomi CEO Steve Lucas, with CIOs and executives from Ford, Deloitte, Red Hat and the Red Cross, on why AI projects stall on disconnected systems and data, and what the teams that got it working did differently.

Mechanisms (3)

mechanism #1300

Ask them for the car, not the chassis

The American Cancer Society's CIO, Ricky Koch, builds small, complete integrations with a little data first.

"“We’ve learned to bite off smaller pieces with our integrations, get them working well, and then expand." "When they ask for a car, they want to see a car. They don’t want to see a chassis and then hear you say ‘once that’s right, we’ll put on the hood, the engine, and everything else.’"

Why it matters

The American Cancer Society's CIO, Ricky Koch, builds small, complete integrations with a little data first. Mistakes surface early, and business teams see something that works. A half-built big system shows them nothing they can use.

Use when: You're building something large for non-technical stakeholders who need to see progress.

mechanism #1309

Let manufacturing see the design early

Fields found pockets of Ford each analyzing only their own data.

"When we started on digital transformation at Ford, I created a single data and analytics group." "So, if the engineers had this great new idea for an engine, transmission, or HVAC system, the manufacturing folks could say, “Hold on, if you design it that way, it will cost more, and servicing it will be harder.”"

Why it matters

Fields found pockets of Ford each analyzing only their own data. Pooling it let downstream teams flag costs before designs were locked. Catching the problem late had meant redone work, extra cost and slipped timelines.

Use when: Teams downstream discover problems only after the upstream team has committed.

mechanism #1311

Look for the damaged plant, not the bug

Nature Fresh Farms' camera robots couldn't find pests, which hid under leaves and fled the noise.

"“The moment this robot makes noise coming through, it just flies away. So, we decided not to look for bugs hurting the plant and look for plants impacted by bugs. That’s always a sure-tell sign where they are.”"

Why it matters

Nature Fresh Farms' camera robots couldn't find pests, which hid under leaves and fled the noise. Switching the target from the elusive cause to its visible effect made detection work.

Use when: You keep failing to detect a cause directly and its effects are easy to see.

Concepts (1)

concept #1306

Data is the new sand

Oil is scarce and data isn't.

"In my opinion, “data is the new oil” was always a simplistic and flawed concept." "There’s no gold star for the business with the most data."

Why it matters

Oil is scarce and data isn't. Lucas argues most stored data is sand: disconnected, unused and a storage cost on the ledger. Value comes from precise, accurate slices put to work, which calls for planning around demand instead of hoarding supply.

Use when: Your organization is collecting everything in the hope it becomes useful later.

Evidence (5)

evidence #1301

Ship a limited scope, then iterate

The Credit Union of Colorado switched from multi-year launches to small releases.

"“Previously, we were built for large-scale project implementations. We’d take three to five years and roll them out only when they were as close to perfect as we could get them." "We’ll take a limited scope, implement, and iterate. We move a lot faster.”"

Why it matters

The Credit Union of Colorado switched from multi-year launches to small releases. It built integrations nearly five times faster, and its new digital member experience contributed to a 5% leap in NPS scores. Aiming for perfect at launch had mostly made launches late.

Use when: Your team holds releases until they're nearly perfect and projects take years.

evidence #1302

Efficiency isn't always the right measure

Kalyra, an aged-care provider, nearly bought software to cut client phone calls.

"They want to pick up the phone because that might be their weekly conversation with somebody." "Efficiency isn’t always the right measure for success. Asking, ‘What is the value that we’re adding?’ is a better approach."

Why it matters

Kalyra, an aged-care provider, nearly bought software to cut client phone calls. A regional manager pointed out that many clients live alone. For them the call was part of the service, and cutting it would have saved staff time at the cost of what clients valued.

Use when: You're automating away a customer touchpoint and measuring only time saved.

question #1303

Ask whose life gets easier

Larry Quinlan, Deloitte's former global CIO, says the implementations that succeeded were the ones tightly tied to the people using them.

"Instead, ask yourself, “How will this initiative make life easier for the actual people who have to use it?”" "It actually creates failure – expensive failure."

Why it matters

Larry Quinlan, Deloitte's former global CIO, says the implementations that succeeded were the ones tightly tied to the people using them. Digital for its own sake gets redone, delayed and abandoned, and careers stall with it.

Use when: You're approving a technology project justified by the system rather than the user.

rule #1305

The three Ps, and the biggest P

Quinlan's test for a transformation: what will people get from it, how can you prove they're getting it, and did you ask whether they liked it.

"I would advise always thinking about the three “Ps” – people, process, and platforms. By far, the biggest “P” of all is people."

Why it matters

Quinlan's test for a transformation: what will people get from it, how can you prove they're getting it, and did you ask whether they liked it. Without those answers, he says, projects flounder.

Use when: You're planning a rollout and the plan is mostly about the platform.

rule #1312

Be willing to destroy the path

Bradley says the farm has failed many times on AI projects.

"The biggest stigma with AI is thinking that when you’re going down a path, you have to stick to it. We’ve learned that sometimes you have to destroy that path, go back to the data, and start rebuilding it in a new way."

Why it matters

Bradley says the farm has failed many times on AI projects. Treating each approach as disposable kept failures cheap, and the farm's yield runs eight to ten times traditional farming per square meter.

Use when: A project is failing and the team feels committed to the approach it started with.
