Mechanisms (13)

mechanism #1163

Break three dozen pots into test tiles

Palissy could not afford to fire whole pieces for every guess at the white enamel.

"He began by breaking three dozen new earthen pots, the pieces of which he covered with different materials which he had compounded, and then took them to a neighbouring glass-furnace to be baked."

Why it matters

Palissy could not afford to fire whole pieces for every guess at the white enamel. Shards let him test many compounds in one firing, and the hotter glass furnace gave him "a glimmer of hope."

Use when: Each full test of an idea is expensive and you are testing one variable at a time.

mechanism #1164

Notice what turns white in the fire

Wedgwood studied chemistry in his spare hours and caught one small change in color.

"he noticed that a certain earth containing silica, which was black before calcination, became white after exposure to the heat of a furnace."

Why it matters

Wedgwood studied chemistry in his spare hours and caught one small change in color. Mixing silica into the local red clay gave him the white body behind English earthenware.

Use when: You keep seeing a small anomaly in your materials and haven't asked what it would let you make.

mechanism #1166

Learn the hand craft before you build the machine

Heathcoat mastered the motions himself before trying to mechanize them.

"He first studied the art of making the Buckingham or pillow-lace by hand, with the object of effecting the same motions by mechanical means."

Why it matters

Heathcoat mastered the motions himself before trying to mechanize them. His master called him "undaunted by failures and mistakes," and the result was the bobbin-net machine.

Use when: You are automating a process you have never done by hand.

mechanism #1168

The spider's net, the lobster shell, the shipworm

Three engineers, three borrowed designs: Brown's suspension bridge from a web, Watt's jointed river pipe from a lobster shell, Brunel's tunneling shield from a boring worm.

"he saw a tiny spider's net suspended across his path. The idea immediately occurred to him, that a bridge of iron ropes or chains might be constructed in like manner" "Sir Isambert Brunel took his first lessons in forming the Thames Tunnel from the tiny shipworm"

Why it matters

Three engineers, three borrowed designs: Brown's suspension bridge from a web, Watt's jointed river pipe from a lobster shell, Brunel's tunneling shield from a boring worm. Each was already studying the problem when he saw the model.

Use when: You are designing a structure and haven't asked what in nature already solves it.

mechanism #1173

Pay someone a crown to drag you out of bed

Willpower alone failed.

"He then called his servant, Joseph, to his help, and promised him the reward of a crown every time that he succeeded in getting him up before six." "he was accustomed to say that he owed to Joseph three or four volumes of his Natural History."

Why it matters

Willpower alone failed. Buffon paid for each success, and Joseph earned his pay by ignoring protests and threats, once with a basin of ice water. The precommitment worked where resolutions hadn't.

Use when: You keep breaking a resolution you've made to yourself many times.

mechanism #1174

Pack the box, one thing at once

Cecil never left a task half done to return to later.

"'Method,' said the Reverend Richard Cecil, 'is like packing things in a box; a good packer will get in half as much again as a bad one.'" "'The shortest way to do many things is to do only one thing at once;'"

Why it matters

Cecil never left a task half done to return to later. De Witt's version was "One thing at a time": think of nothing else until the current dispatch is finished.

Use when: You have ten things open and none of them is getting done.

mechanism #1181

Pay your own bills

Wellington's servant had speculated with the money and left the bills unpaid.

"'I make a point,' said he to Mr. Gleig, 'of paying my own bills, and I advise every one to do the same; formerly I used to trust a confidential servant to pay them, but I was cured of that folly by receiving one morning, to my great surprise, duns of a year or two's standing."

Why it matters

Wellington's servant had speculated with the money and left the bills unpaid. Keeping "an accurate detailed account of all the moneys received and expended" was the fix.

Use when: Someone else handles your money and you haven't looked at the accounts in months.

Arguments (1)

argument #1176

Too much fame too early

This is Smiles's verdict on Benjamin West, a child prodigy.

"West might have been a greater painter, had he not been injured by too early success: his fame, though great, was not purchased by study, trials, and difficulties, and it has not been enduring."

Why it matters

This is Smiles's verdict on Benjamin West, a child prodigy. He makes the same point about Rossini through Beethoven: facility that comes too easily can stop the hard practice.

Use when: Early praise is arriving faster than your skill is growing.

evidence #1167

Watch your daughters comb their hair

Heilmann had a professionally built model and still couldn't make it work.

"he found himself almost unconsciously watching his daughters coming their long hair and drawing it out at full length between their fingers." "if he could successfully imitate in a machine the process of combing out the longest hair and forcing back the short by reversing the action of the comb, it might serve to extricate him from his difficulty."

Why it matters

Heilmann had a professionally built model and still couldn't make it work. The answer came from an ordinary motion at home, which he copied into the combing machine. ("coming" is the Gutenberg text's misprint for "combing".)

Use when: You've been stuck on a mechanism for months and have only looked at other machines.

evidence #1172

Rats ate the drawings, so draw better ones

Audubon lost two hundred original drawings.

"a pair of Norway rats had taken possession of the whole, and reared a young family among the gnawed bits of paper" "I felt pleased that I might now make better drawings than before; and, ere a period not exceeding three years had elapsed, my portfolio was again filled." "He had no draft, and was compelled to rake up from his memory facts, ideas, and expressions, which had been long since dismissed."

Why it matters

Audubon lost two hundred original drawings. In his own account he was flattened for days, then treated the redo as a chance to improve, and refilled the portfolio in under three years. Smiles pairs it with Carlyle, whose lent manuscript of the first French Revolution volume was burned as waste paper and rewritten from memory.

Use when: Months of work are gone and you can't face starting again.

Why it matters

After a failed first voyage the Admiralty refused Phipps another ship. He raised the money privately through a twenty-share company and found the Spanish wreck when a sailor sent a diver after a sea plant.

Use when: Your first attempt failed in public and the original backer won't fund a second.

Passages (1)

passage #1169

Trifles make perfection

The visitor saw no change; Michelangelo listed six.

"'I have retouched this part—polished that—softened this feature—brought out that muscle—given some expression to this lip, and more energy to that limb.' 'But these are trifles,' remarked the visitor. 'It may be so,' replied the sculptor, 'but recollect that trifles make perfection, and perfection is no trifle.'"

Why it matters

The visitor saw no change; Michelangelo listed six. Smiles pairs it with Poussin's answer on his reputation: "Because I have neglected nothing."

Use when: Someone asks why the last 5 per cent is taking so long.
