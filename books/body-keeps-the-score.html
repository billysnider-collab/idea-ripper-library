Three Avenues to Trauma Recovery

framework keep

Trauma recovery works through three pathways: top-down (talk, connect, understand), medication (dampen alarm), and bottom-up (body experiences that contradict helplessness).

Use when: When designing a treatment plan or self-help strategy for trauma, consider all three avenues and combine them as needed.

Trauma Reorganizes Perception

framework ungraded

Trauma changes how people perceive the world, causing them to superimpose past threats onto neutral present stimuli.

Use when: When a client overreacts to benign situations or misinterprets others' intentions as threatening.

Learned Helplessness Mechanism

framework ungraded

Inescapable shock leads to passivity even when escape becomes possible; the same mechanism operates in traumatized humans.

Use when: When someone seems unable to take action to improve their situation despite available options, or when you need to understand why traumatized people give up.

Four Truths Overlooked by Brain-Disease Model

framework ungraded

The brain-disease model ignores four fundamental truths: healing through relationships, language, self-regulation of physiology, and social change.

Use when: When critiquing purely biological approaches to mental health or designing holistic treatment plans.

Right-Brain Trauma Storage

framework ungraded

Traumatic memories are stored primarily in the right hemisphere as sensory and emotional fragments, while the left hemisphere's linguistic and sequencing functions are deactivated.

Use when: When talk therapy alone fails, consider that the trauma is held in non-verbal, sensory form and may require non-verbal approaches.

Trauma Reenactment Rule

rule ungraded

If you do something to a patient that you would not do to your friends or children, consider whether you are unwittingly replicating a trauma from the patient's past.

Use when: When providing care or interacting with someone who has a trauma history, especially in situations involving physical restraint, force, or institutional routines.

Physical Experience to Restore Control

rule ungraded

To overcome learned helplessness, repeatedly guide the person to physically experience escape from a situation reminiscent of the trauma.

Use when: When treating trauma survivors who feel immobilized and powerless, especially those stuck in learned helplessness.

Effective Action vs. Immobilization

rule ungraded

If a person can take effective action to escape a threat, trauma is less likely; if they are immobilized, the stress response continues and trauma develops.

Use when: You are assessing someone after a traumatic event or designing prevention strategies.

Immobilization Without Fear

rule ungraded

If you want deep intimacy—embrace, sleep, sex—you must allow immobilization without fear; trauma survivors often cannot.

Use when: When working with traumatized clients who avoid physical closeness or intimacy.

Trauma Interrupts the Plot

quote ungraded

A precise metaphor for how trauma disrupts life's narrative continuity, leaving people stuck.

Use when: When explaining to clients why they feel fragmented or unable to move forward.

The Greatest Sources of Suffering

quote ungraded

A pithy reminder that self-deception and avoidance perpetuate psychological pain.

Use when: When a client is avoiding facing a painful truth or minimizing their experience.

Semrad's Rule of Experiential Knowledge

quote ungraded

Healing depends on acknowledging the reality of your body and feeling what you feel, not just intellectual insight.

Use when: When working with clients who are disconnected from their bodily sensations or who intellectualize their trauma.

Body Keeps the Score

quote ungraded

A concise metaphor for how unexpressed trauma continues to affect the body even when the mind denies it.

Use when: When explaining to clients or colleagues how trauma can manifest physically despite conscious avoidance.

Living Memorial Syndrome

claim ungraded

Some trauma survivors unconsciously keep their symptoms as a way to honor dead comrades or avoid betraying them.

Use when: When a client resists symptom reduction because they fear it means forgetting or dishonoring someone.

Numbing and Emotional Shutdown

claim ungraded

Trauma can cause a pervasive emotional numbness that feels like living behind a glass wall, often alternating with rage.

Use when: When a client reports feeling dead inside, unable to love, or disconnected from themselves and others.

Failure of Imagination

claim ungraded

Trauma impairs the capacity to imagine new possibilities, trapping people in a replay of the past.

Use when: When a client cannot envision a future or feels hopeless and stuck.

Compulsion to Repeat

claim ungraded

Traumatized people often seek out dangerous or painful situations, but this repetition leads only to further pain and self-hatred, not mastery.

Use when: When a client repeatedly returns to abusive relationships or self-destructive behaviors, to understand the dynamic and avoid reinforcing it.

Calming Techniques for Trauma

use_when ungraded

Use breathing at six breaths per minute, acupressure tapping, and mindfulness to manage overwhelming trauma sensations.

Use when: When a patient is flooded with intense emotions or flashbacks and needs grounding.

Recognizing Reenactments in Clinical and Legal Settings

use_when kill

When individuals repeatedly engage in illegal or self-destructive behaviors without clear motive, consider traumatic reenactment rather than criminality or mental illness.

Use when: In forensic or clinical evaluations where the patient's history is unknown and behavior seems irrational.

Anesthesia Awareness as Trauma Model

use_when keep

Anesthesia awareness provides a clear model of trauma: paralysis, pain, and inability to communicate create lasting PTSD.

Use when: When explaining how trauma occurs even in medical settings, or when advocating for awareness and prevention.
