All About Love — bell hooks

10 stealable ideas · 10 keep

Best stealables

Choose Life Over Death

argument keep

In biblical scripture the divine voice declares “I have set before you life and death, therefore choose life.” Embracing the spirit that lives beyond the body is one way to choose life.

Use when: you're framing a hard choice as the biblical one — life or death — and making the case for the move that chooses life.

Love as the Will to Extend Self

concept keep

Echoing the work of Erich Fromm, he defines love as “the will to extend one’s self for the purpose of nurturing one’s own or another’s spiritual growth.” Explaining further, he continues: “Love is as love does.

Use when: you're arguing that love is work, not a feeling — Fromm's definition is your quote: the will to extend yourself for someone's growth.

Love Casts Out Fear's Torment

argument keep

Significantly, we are told in biblical scripture that it is crucial that love casts out fear “because fear hath torment.” These words speak directly to the presence of anguish in our lives when we are driven by fear.

Use when: you're arguing that fear and love can't co-pilot a life — where fear runs the show, anguish follows, and love is the way out.

Love Completes and Intensifies Life

passage keep

Extolling the transformative power of love in his essay “Love and Need,” Merton writes: “Love is, in fact an intensification of life, a completeness, a fullness, a wholeness of life. . . .

Use when: you're answering the cynics who call love soft or decorative — Merton's line makes it an intensification of life itself.

Love Is a Verb, Not a Noun

definition keep

The word “love” is most often defined as a noun, yet all the more astute theorists of love acknowledge that we would all love better if we used it as a verb.

Use when: you're arguing that “I love you” means nothing without the doing — hooks makes the grammar the point: love is behavior, not a label.

Perfect Love Casts Out Fear

argument keep

I am especially fond of the biblical passage in the first epistle of John, which tells us: “There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment.

Use when: you're speaking to someone afraid to trust — the biblical line promises that perfect love displaces fear, not by denying it but by driving it out.

Perfect Love Releases Fear

concept keep

This scripture encourages us to find comfort in knowing that “perfect love casts our fear.” This is our reminder that even if fear exists it can be released by the experience of perfect love.

Use when: you're reassuring someone that being afraid doesn't disqualify them from love — the claim is that love releases fear, even while it's still present.

Renew the Spirit Endlessly

argument keep

The healing power of mind and heart is always present because we have the capacity to renew our spirits endlessly, to restore the soul.

Use when: you're arguing against despair as the last word — the mind and heart can renew themselves endlessly, so heartbreak isn't final.

Fear blocks the gift of love

passage keep

To receive the gift, we must first understand that “there is no fear in love.” But we do fear and fear keeps us from trusting in love.

Use when: you're naming the prerequisite everyone skips — receiving love requires facing the fear that keeps us from trusting it.

Love has to move past feeling into action

passage keep

Whether we are engaged in a process of self-love or of loving others we must move beyond the realm of feeling to actualize love.

Use when: you're arguing that feeling love isn't the same as loving — self-love or loving others, the feeling has to become practice.
