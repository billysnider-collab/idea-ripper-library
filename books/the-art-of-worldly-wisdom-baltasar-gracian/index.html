Card #c1063Synthesis

Hand the boss advice as something he forgot

"They will allow a man to help them but not to surpass them, and will have any advice tendered them appear like a recollection of something they have forgotten rather than as a guide to something they cannot find."

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

You have to correct someone above you who is proud of their judgment.

[Why it matters]

Gracián says superiors will concede you luck or temper but never better judgment. Framing a correction as a reminder lets the superior keep ownership of the good sense while still taking the advice.

Card #c1064Synthesis

Find the idol, then say the one word

"All men are idolaters, some of fame, others of self-interest, most of pleasure. Skill consists in knowing these idols in order to bring them into play." "First guess a man's ruling passion, appeal to it by a word, set it in motion by temptation"

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

Your good arguments keep failing on someone and you have not asked what they actually want.

[Why it matters]

The maxim is a three-step sequence: diagnose the dominant motive, name it briefly, then offer something that feeds it. He adds that the working motive is more often the lowest part of a person's nature than the highest.

Card #c1065Synthesis

Hold the dissent, say it later to a few

"To dissent from others' views is regarded as an insult, because it is their condemnation. Disgust is doubled on account of the thing blamed and of the person who praised it."

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

You think a popular plan is wrong and are tempted to say so in the all-hands.

[Why it matters]

Disagreeing in public offends twice: once for the thing blamed and once for the person who praised it. Gracián keeps the private judgment and voices it "before few and fit persons."

Show one purpose, strike with the other

"It lets a purpose appear in order to attract the opponent's attention, but then turns round and conquers by the unexpected." "It lets the first impulse pass by and waits for the second, or even the third."

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

An opening demand looks too obvious, or you are about to react to the other side's first move.

[Why it matters]

The maxim covers both sides of a feint. The attacker shows a decoy aim. The defender treats the first move as probably false and waits for the second or third before reacting.

Don't let them learn your tastes

"He that plays with cards exposed runs a risk of losing the stakes." "Do not even let your tastes be known, lest others utilise them either by running counter to them or by flattering them."

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

You are about to tell a counterpart which option you love before the terms are set.

[Why it matters]

Gracián extends secrecy from plans to tastes. A known preference gives the other side two levers: block what you like, or flatter it to soften you.

Ask after the meal, never after a refusal

"Surprise them when in a pleasant mood, when a repast of body or soul has just left them refreshed" "It is no use applying when another has been refused, since the objection to a No has just been overcome."

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

You need a yes from someone and can pick the moment to ask.

[Why it matters]

Timing gets three rules: ask when the person is in a good mood, avoid asking just after they refused someone else, and avoid asking after a loss. Having just said no once, they find the next no easy.

Call the break a cooling, never a fight

"Every one may be of importance as an enemy if not as a friend. Few can do us good, almost any can do us harm." "If, however, a breach is inevitable, let it be rather excused as a slackening of friendship than by an outburst of wrath"

— In our words · Source: The Art of Worldly Wisdom (Baltasar Gracián)

[Use when]

A partnership has to end and you want to say what you really think on the way out.

[Why it matters]

A loud rupture invites hidden enemies to use your declared one against you. Letting a relationship fade without a scene closes the door with fewer enemies made.
