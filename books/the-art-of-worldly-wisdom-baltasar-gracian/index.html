Mechanisms (23)

mechanism #1063

Hand the boss advice as something he forgot

Gracián says superiors will concede you luck or temper but never better judgment.

"They will allow a man to help them but not to surpass them, and will have any advice tendered them appear like a recollection of something they have forgotten rather than as a guide to something they cannot find."

Why it matters

Gracián says superiors will concede you luck or temper but never better judgment. Framing a correction as a reminder lets the superior keep ownership of the good sense while still taking the advice.

Use when: You have to correct someone above you who is proud of their judgment.

mechanism #1064

Find the idol, then say the one word

The maxim is a three-step sequence: diagnose the dominant motive, name it briefly, then offer something that feeds it.

"All men are idolaters, some of fame, others of self-interest, most of pleasure. Skill consists in knowing these idols in order to bring them into play." "First guess a man's ruling passion, appeal to it by a word, set it in motion by temptation"

Why it matters

The maxim is a three-step sequence: diagnose the dominant motive, name it briefly, then offer something that feeds it. He adds that the working motive is more often the lowest part of a person's nature than the highest.

Use when: Your good arguments keep failing on someone and you have not asked what they actually want.

mechanism #1067

Show one purpose, strike with the other

The maxim covers both sides of a feint.

"It lets a purpose appear in order to attract the opponent's attention, but then turns round and conquers by the unexpected." "It lets the first impulse pass by and waits for the second, or even the third."

Why it matters

The maxim covers both sides of a feint. The attacker shows a decoy aim. The defender treats the first move as probably false and waits for the second or third before reacting.

Use when: An opening demand looks too obvious, or you are about to react to the other side's first move.

mechanism #1068

Don't let them learn your tastes

Gracián extends secrecy from plans to tastes.

"He that plays with cards exposed runs a risk of losing the stakes." "Do not even let your tastes be known, lest others utilise them either by running counter to them or by flattering them."

Why it matters

Gracián extends secrecy from plans to tastes. A known preference gives the other side two levers: block what you like, or flatter it to soften you.

Use when: You are about to tell a counterpart which option you love before the terms are set.

mechanism #1069

When they grab the right side, take it anyway

Gracián says arguing the worse side out of pride means "you begin the fight already beaten." Take the better course yourself, and an obstinate opponent will often desert it to keep disagreeing.

"Thus the only way to drive him from the better course is to take it yourself, for his folly will cause him to desert it"

Why it matters

Gracián says arguing the worse side out of pride means "you begin the fight already beaten." Take the better course yourself, and an obstinate opponent will often desert it to keep disagreeing.

Use when: Your rival staked out the sensible position first and you feel pressure to oppose it.

Opening on the other person's interest gets them moving before your request arrives. Gracián recommends it most for people whose reflex answer is no.

Why it matters

Gracián's technique for bitter truths is distance in time. Discussing a live error as old history lets the person agree without having to defend their present self.

mechanism #1074

Satisfy the want, keep the dependence

Gracián would rather see men needing him than thanking him.

"The wise man would rather see men needing him than thanking him. To keep them on the threshold of hope is diplomatic, to trust to their gratitude boorish ; hope has a good memory, gratitude a bad one." "It is a subtle point to satisfy the desire and yet preserve the dependence."

Why it matters

Gracián would rather see men needing him than thanking him. Deliver enough to keep them engaged and hold back the part that ends their need.

Use when: You are about to give the client everything up front and lose your leverage for phase two.

mechanism #1077

Ask after the meal, never after a refusal

Timing gets three rules: ask when the person is in a good mood, avoid asking just after they refused someone else, and avoid asking after a loss.

"Surprise them when in a pleasant mood, when a repast of body or soul has just left them refreshed" "It is no use applying when another has been refused, since the objection to a No has just been overcome."

Why it matters

Timing gets three rules: ask when the person is in a good mood, avoid asking just after they refused someone else, and avoid asking after a loss. Having just said no once, they find the next no easy.

Use when: You need a yes from someone and can pick the moment to ask.

mechanism #1078

Pay the reward before it's earned

The same favor changes meaning with timing.

"the same gift which would afterwards be merely a reward is beforehand an obligation." "with men of lower stamp the honorarium paid beforehand acts rather as a bit than as a spur."

Why it matters

The same favor changes meaning with timing. Given afterward it settles a debt; given in advance it creates one. Gracián limits it to people who feel obligation.

Use when: You plan to reward a partner after delivery and wonder whether to give it now instead.

Why it matters

A loud rupture invites hidden enemies to use your declared one against you. Letting a relationship fade without a scene closes the door with fewer enemies made.

Use when: A partnership has to end and you want to say what you really think on the way out.

mechanism #1082

Don't show the sore point

Gracián says a known sore spot becomes the place everything knocks against, and complaining about it marks the target.

"nor complain about it, for malice always aims where weakness can be injured." "The wise never own to being hit, or disclose any evil, whether personal or hereditary."

Why it matters

Gracián says a known sore spot becomes the place everything knocks against, and complaining about it marks the target. In a negotiation that means keeping quiet about the term you can least afford to lose.

Use when: You're tempted to tell the other side which concession would hurt you most.

mechanism #1083

Show your teeth at the first encroachment

Gracián prices concessions as a chain: each one you grant makes the next harder to refuse.

"Give way to the first and you must yield to the second, and so on till the last, and to gain your point at last costs as much trouble as would have gained much more at first."

Why it matters

Gracián prices concessions as a chain: each one you grant makes the next harder to refuse. Pushing back at the first costs less than recovering ground after several.

Use when: A counterpart has just asked for a small extra that wasn't in the deal.

mechanism #1084

"Some men's No is thought more of than the Yes of others : for a gilded No is more satisfactory than a dry Yes." "Your refusal need not be point-blank : let the disappointment come by degrees. Nor let the refusal be final"

Why it matters

Gracián's refusal has a method: let the disappointment arrive in stages, leave some hope in place, and cover the gap with courtesy. He warns that people who lead with No get no credit even when they later give way.

The contest is unequal because only one side has a reputation at stake.

Why it matters

The contest is unequal because only one side has a reputation at stake. Gracián says even winning doesn't repay what you risked by stepping in.

Arguments (1)

argument #1065

Hold the dissent, say it later to a few

Disagreeing in public offends twice: once for the thing blamed and once for the person who praised it.

"To dissent from others' views is regarded as an insult, because it is their condemnation. Disgust is doubled on account of the thing blamed and of the person who praised it."

Why it matters

Disagreeing in public offends twice: once for the thing blamed and once for the person who praised it. Gracián keeps the private judgment and voices it "before few and fit persons."

Use when: You think a popular plan is wrong and are tempted to say so in the all-hands.

Time and I against any two

"You must pass through the circumference of time before arriving at the centre of opportunity." "Time's crutch effects more than the iron club of Hercules."

Why it matters

Gracián quotes the saying "Time and I against any two." The side without a deadline can let the side with one make the concessions.
