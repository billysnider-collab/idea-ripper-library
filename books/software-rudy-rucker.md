## Steal

Build the pitch around the one specific gap in the other person's life, not around your biggest feature.

## Why it matters

The boppers don't offer the old, sick Cobb Anderson money; they offer immortality: "We want to make you immortal, Dr. Anderson." A pitch lands when it names what the listener lacks, so be wary when a pitch to you is shaped around your own gap.

## Use when

Writing the opening line of a sales email: name the customer's specific pain before mentioning any feature.

## Steal

Before accepting a transformative offer, ask what substrate it runs on and who owns it.

## Why it matters

The boppers' immortality means becoming "a piece of software in the big boppers' memory banks": a copy, on their hardware, under their control. The price hides in the gap between the pitch (live forever) and the mechanism (run on our servers).

## Use when

Weighing an acquisition or platform deal that promises your product will "live on" inside their ecosystem.

## Steal

In any high-trust handoff, verify identity with a tell that only the original can produce.

## Why it matters

Anderson2, Cobb's robot double, offers to stand in for him unnoticed, yet Cobb spots its flat, glassy eyes. Substitution works because people deal with the interface, not the person, so pre-agreed tells are the defense.

## Use when

An "executive" urgently requests a wire transfer by voice or video: confirm with a pre-arranged code or a callback to a known number.

## Steal

Make something outlast you by copying its pattern into a new carrier before the old one fails, not by maintaining the original.

## Why it matters

Boppers face disassembly after ten months unless they have built a scion, a new body carrying their pattern, and Ralph Numbers has survived by doing it thirty-six times. Archives rot; working copies (students, forks, successors) persist.

## Use when

You're the only person who knows how a critical system works: train a successor who runs it while you are still around.
