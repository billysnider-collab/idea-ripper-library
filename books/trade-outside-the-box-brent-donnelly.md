## Steal

"Keynes asks us to imagine a newspaper which holds a contest where readers are rewarded for choosing which contestant will be chosen by the most other readers."

"The task is not to choose the most beautiful woman, but instead to choose the woman others will think is most beautiful."

## Why it matters

Donnelly uses Keynes' contest to explain why buying the cheapest stock on a spreadsheet can lose. Prices move on what other participants will believe next, and that is a different question from what's true.

## Use when

## Steal

"The simple definition of an ergodic system is one where the expected value of an activity performed by a group is the same for an individual carrying out the same activity over time."

"Nobody would choose to play this game 100 times in a row because the expected outcome is almost certain death."

## Why it matters

If 100 people play Russian roulette once for $10 million, the group's average is attractive. One person playing 100 times dies. Donnelly applies this to all-in betting: a positive average can still ruin the individual who repeats the bet.

## Use when

A bet looks good on average and you'll be making it over and over with the same bankroll.
