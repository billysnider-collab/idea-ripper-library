argument #141

The invisible government

The book’s opening thesis: democracy is governed by unseen manipulators of mass habit — a structural feature, not a conspiracy.

The conscious and intelligent manipulation of the organized habits and opinions of the masses is an important element in democratic society. Those who manipulate this unseen mechanism of society constitute an invisible government which is the true ruling power of our country. We are governed, our minds are molded, our tastes formed, our ideas suggested, largely by men we have never heard of.

Why it matters

The book’s opening thesis: democracy is governed by unseen manipulators of mass habit — a structural feature, not a conspiracy.

Use when: When “who really decides?” is the question.

mechanism #143

Rubber-stamp minds

Literacy did not produce independent minds; it produced identical imprints from identical stimuli — the precondition of mass persuasion.

But instead of a mind, universal literacy has given him rubber stamps, rubber stamps inked with advertising slogans, with editorials, with published scientific data, with the trivialities of the tabloids and the platitudes of history, but quite innocent of original thought. Each man’s rubber stamps are the duplicates of millions of others, so that when those millions are exposed to the same stimuli, all receive identical imprints.

Why it matters

Literacy did not produce independent minds; it produced identical imprints from identical stimuli — the precondition of mass persuasion.

Use when: When everyone suddenly agrees.

argument #144

The intelligent minorities

Bernays’s open claim: progress comes from small groups making the rest think what they please; minorities use propaganda continuously.

clearly it is the intelligent minorities which need to make use of propaganda continuously and systematically. In the active proselytizing minorities in whom selfish interests and public interests coincide lie the progress and development of America. Only through the active energy of the intelligent few can the public at large become aware of and act upon new ideas. Small groups of persons can, and do, make the rest of us think what they please about a given subject.

Why it matters

Bernays’s open claim: progress comes from small groups making the rest think what they please; minorities use propaganda continuously.

Use when: When a fringe idea goes mainstream overnight.

mechanism #145

Reach the group leaders

The targeting doctrine: persuade the leaders who dictate the public’s thoughts and the group follows — hence the propaganda specialist.

To reach and persuade the group leaders who dictate the public’s thoughts and actions is likewise expensive. For this reason there is an increasing tendency to concentrate the functions of propaganda in the hands of the propaganda specialist.

Why it matters

The targeting doctrine: persuade the leaders who dictate the public’s thoughts and the group follows — hence the propaganda specialist.

Use when: When planning how an idea actually spreads.

The job description of the new profession: bringing ideas to public consciousness via media and group formations.

The public relations counsel, then, is the agent who, working with modern media of communication and the group formations of society, brings an idea to the consciousness of the public.

argument #147

Induce our leaders to lead

The democratic paradox: the voice-of-God dogma makes elected men will-less servants, producing political sterility.

The great political problem in our modern democracy is how to induce our leaders to lead. The dogma that the voice of the people is the voice of God tends to make elected persons the will-less servants of their constituents.

Why it matters

The democratic paradox: the voice-of-God dogma makes elected men will-less servants, producing political sterility.

Use when: When leaders only follow polls.

mechanism #148

Influence the leaders, sway the herd

Mass psychology works without the mass: man feels himself a herd member alone in his room, so moving the leader moves the group.

If you can influence the leaders, either with or without their conscious coöperation, you automatically influence the group which they sway.

Why it matters

Mass psychology works without the mass: man feels himself a herd member alone in his room, so moving the leader moves the group.
