by Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz

12 cards

Andrew Aziz and performance psychologist Crede Sheehy-Kelly's 2025 system for building a complete trading system: strategy is a concept, a TradeBook is the manual — selection, identification, execution, review — and the edge is never one piece but the combination.

Card #c1Synthesis

A strategy is a concept; a TradeBook is a manual

There is a fundamental difference between a strategy and a TradeBook. A strategy outlines the overall concept—what to look for in a particular type of trade and how that trade generally works. A TradeBook, however, is much more practical, more like a manual.

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When someone pitches a 'strategy' with no rules for entries, exits, or review.

[Why it matters]

The book's spine sentence: a strategy only says what to look for, a TradeBook scripts selection, identification, planning, entries, exits, stops, position sizing, management, and psychology.

The edge is the combination, not one piece

The edge in trading doesn’t come from any one of these pieces on their own. It comes from combining them into a system that works for you. That’s what a TradeBook helps you build—a personalized, complete, and practical trading guide based on your tools, your mindset, and your strategy.

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When a trader keeps buying better tools instead of building a system.

[Why it matters]

Technology, strategy, and psychology each fail alone; the TradeBook is the overlap where all three meet, and that overlap is the only durable edge.

Card #c3Synthesis

Gamification traded against the retail trader

A November 2024 study shed light on this trend, analyzing 142 gamification features in the mobile trading apps of 17 major U.S. brokers. The research found that while these features successfully increased retail trading activity and income for brokers and market makers, they often led to lower returns and higher volatility for the retail traders themselves.

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When a flashy app makes trading feel like a game and you trade more than planned.

[Why it matters]

The technology chapter's warning: the tools were built to increase broker income, and they work — by making you trade more and win less.

Card #c6Synthesis

Your edge is a TradeBook, codified and identifiable

Bear Bull Traders calls your edge in the markets your “TradeBooks”, and we define your edge as a specific market opportunity codified in a specific TradeBook, which is repeatedly identifiable, and provides you with a positive expected value in profit, over time, when executed with discipline.

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When asked to name your edge and you can only describe vibes.

[Why it matters]

The formal definition: an edge is not a feeling about a setup, it is an opportunity written down, repeatedly recognizable, and positive-expectancy — the casino model of trading.

Card #c7Synthesis

The TradeBook answers: should I take this trade?

If you cannot put the trade opportunity into any of your TradeBooks, it means you should not take that trade. And this is the most important purpose of your TradeBooks. They help you answer the question: Should I take the trade or not?

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When a tempting setup does not quite fit your plan but you want it anyway.

[Why it matters]

The TradeBook's gatekeeping function: it converts 'this looks good' into a binary fit-or-skip decision, which is where most retail losses begin.

Card #c8Synthesis

Success is not being right most of the time

This highlights a vital aspect of trading: success is not about being right most of the time. Instead, it is about ensuring that your winning trades are large enough to outweigh your losses.

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When a low win rate tempts you to abandon a working system.

[Why it matters]

Seven years of ORB research: stopped out 75% of the time, profitable on 25%, and still beating the market — expectancy, not win rate, is the scoreboard.

Card #c9Synthesis

Drawdowns are asymmetric: 50% down needs 100% to recover

A 50% loss means you will need to double your account (100%) just to get back to even.

— In our words · Source: TradeBook: How to Build a Complete Trading System (Stock Market Trading and Investing) (Crede Sheehy-Kelly & Andrew Aziz)

[Use when]

When sizing up a trade, run the recovery math first.

[Why it matters]

Losses compound against you, which is why the stop loss is the foundation of the whole system: capital protection is the edge beneath the edge.
