## Steal

"“We’ve learned to bite off smaller pieces with our integrations, get them working well, and then expand."

"When they ask for a car, they want to see a car. They don’t want to see a chassis and then hear you say ‘once that’s right, we’ll put on the hood, the engine, and everything else.’"

## Why it matters

The American Cancer Society's CIO, Ricky Koch, builds small, complete integrations with a little data first. Mistakes surface early, and business teams see something that works. A half-built big system shows them nothing they can use.

## Use when

You're building something large for non-technical stakeholders who need to see progress.

## Steal

"“Previously, we were built for large-scale project implementations. We’d take three to five years and roll them out only when they were as close to perfect as we could get them."

"We’ll take a limited scope, implement, and iterate. We move a lot faster.”"

## Why it matters

The Credit Union of Colorado switched from multi-year launches to small releases. It built integrations nearly five times faster, and its new digital member experience contributed to a 5% leap in NPS scores. Aiming for perfect at launch had mostly made launches late.

## Use when

Your team holds releases until they're nearly perfect and projects take years.

## Steal

"When we started on digital transformation at Ford, I created a single data and analytics group."

"So, if the engineers had this great new idea for an engine, transmission, or HVAC system, the manufacturing folks could say, “Hold on, if you design it that way, it will cost more, and servicing it will be harder.”"

## Why it matters

Fields found pockets of Ford each analyzing only their own data. Pooling it let downstream teams flag costs before designs were locked. Catching the problem late had meant redone work, extra cost and slipped timelines.

## Use when

Teams downstream discover problems only after the upstream team has committed.
