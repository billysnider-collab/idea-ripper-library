Money as social tech — from grain and salt to empire and trust.

Card #c28Synthesis

Grain fixes the price of a day's work

A specific amount of grain corresponded to something else, such as a day’s work for a laborer, establishing a relationship between the price of food and the price of everything.

— In our words · Source: The History of Money (David McWilliams)

[Use when]

Explaining why 'real' value talk always smuggles a subsistence unit.

[Why it matters]

Money starts as a conversion table between calories and labor — prices are ratios before they are coins.

Card #c29Synthesis

Salary from salt

The modern word “salary” comes from this idea of being paid in salt or paid with a coin linked to salt, as too does the expression “worth his salt.” Vespasian meant business.

— In our words · Source: The History of Money (David McWilliams)

[Use when]

A wage metaphor needs its Roman logistics back.

[Why it matters]

Etymology is policy fossil: being 'worth your salt' is wage history wearing a proverb costume.

Card #c30Synthesis

Trust enough to fund a never-repaid loan

Can you imagine how much trust in money there must be in a society for people to finance a loan that they know is never actually going to be repaid and yet consider this to be a prudent form of saving?

— In our words · Source: The History of Money (David McWilliams)

[Use when]

A 'crazy' debt instrument is actually a trust ritual.

[Why it matters]

Credit culture can treat known non-repayment as normal — trust is not optimism, it is a shared fiction with rules.

Card #c31Synthesis

Did hyperinflation trigger empire's fall

Just as the expansion of empire took time, the disintegration happened gradually, but could the degeneration have been triggered by hyperinflation and the destruction of the credit system?

— In our words · Source: The History of Money (David McWilliams)

[Use when]

Decline narratives skip the currency death spiral.

[Why it matters]

Monetary collapse is offered as a possible trigger for imperial disintegration, not a sideshow.
