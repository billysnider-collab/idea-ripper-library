## Steal

*GOLDEN VERSES OF THE PYTHAGOREANS (1) PREPARATION Render to the Immortal Gods the consecrated cult; Guard then thy faith (2): Revere the memory Of the Illustrious Heroes, of Spirits demi-Gods (3). 7 PURIFICATION Be a good son, just brother, spouse tender and good father (4)
— *The Golden Verses of Pythagoras*, Preparation–Purification, trans. Nayan Louise Redfield (1917), p. 113. **Surface Gloss:** Honour the immortal gods according to established law, keep your sacred oaths, revere heroic ancestors and intermediary spirits, and fulfill your immediate duties to your family.

**The Stack-Alignment Protocol:** Before attempting high-level cognitive or creative transformation ("Perfection"), stabilize your baseline dependencies in descending order of scale: honour the macro-rules of your environment, keep your explicit commitments (word-as-bond), study the exemplars of your craft ("Heroes"), and keep your domestic house in order. You cannot do deep inner work while your foundational social contracts are in default.

## Why it matters

Honour the immortal gods according to established law, keep your sacred oaths, revere heroic ancestors and intermediary spirits, and fulfill your immediate duties to your family.

In ancient Pythagoreanism, ethics begins with *kosmos*—structural order. The initiate does not invent private worship but renders homage "as established by law" (*nomōi*, visible in the facing Greek on p. 113). Reality is a continuous vertical scale of being descending from the Immortal Gods (unchanging celestial intelligences), through Illustrious Heroes (elevated human souls of great virtue), down to terrestrial *daimones* (ontological intermediaries governing the sub-lunary sphere, rendered by Redfield as "Spirits demi-Gods"), and finally anchoring in human kinship bonds (parents and blood relatives). "Guarding thy faith" translates the Greek *sebou horkon*—revering the Oath. In a brotherhood bound by sworn secrecy and mathematical harmony, breaking an oath fractures the linguistic covenant that binds human society to cosmic proportion. Civic piety and filial duty are the foundational tuning of the instrument before inner purification can begin.

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In the late antique and Renaissance *prisca theologia* chain—running from Iamblichus and Hierocles through Marsilio Ficino's commentaries on Platonic theology—this opening triad is read as an operational map of theurgic simpatico. Ficino and Giovanni Pico della Mirandola viewed the Pythagorean hierarchy as the pagan prefiguration of the celestial hierarchies: Gods correspond to intelligible forms or supercelestial angels, Heroes to perfected souls who mediate grace, and *daimones* to planetary and elemental intelligences. By performing "consecrated cult" (*theourgia*) and keeping the initiatory Oath, the magus aligns his *spiritus* with each rung of the cosmic ladder. Filial piety is not mere bourgeois morality; parents are the visible, material images of the generative Gods, so dishonouring one's father severs the astral sympathetic cord connecting the practitioner to the Demiurge.

## Use when

Designing onboarding protocols, personal discipline systems, or worldbuilding a philosophical order where everyday domestic integrity is a strict prerequisite for higher knowledge.

**The Constraint-Capacity Coupling:** Human capability (*Dynamis*) lives right next door to non-negotiable constraint (*Anankē*). As long as you treat a relationship, project, or discipline as optional—something you can abandon over a "trifling grievance"—your capacity remains dormant. ## Why it matters

Select friends solely on the basis of their virtue, learn from their example, and do not sever the bond over minor faults—pushing your tolerance to the limit, because human capability is forged only under the pressure of necessity.

For the Pythagoreans, *philia* (friendship) is the social expression of numerical harmony (*harmonia*). Unlike blood relatives, who are assigned by fate, a friend is deliberately chosen based on *aretē* (excellence/virtue). Once chosen, the friend serves as an externalized mirror and corrective gyroscope ("Yield to his gentle counsels"). However, human nature is composite and prone to error; abandoning an alliance over a minor offense (*hamartados mikrēs*) destroys a high-value harmonic structure over transient noise. The qualification "If thou canst at least: for a most rigid law / Binds Power to Necessity" (*dynamis gar anankēs engythi naiei*—"Power dwells near Necessity") cashes out a core pre-Socratic mechanic: human capacity (*dynamis*) is not a static reserve. You only discover and expand what you are capable of enduring when you treat the preservation of virtue and friendship as an inescapable constraint (*anankē*).

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Within the Renaissance esoteric reception (notably Ficino's *De Amore* and the Neoplatonic commentary of Hierocles that Ficino translated into Latin), Pythagorean friendship is an alchemical vessel for soul-merging: "friends hold all things in common" because their souls are tuned to the same planetary ray. The maxim that "Power dwells near Necessity" was read by Renaissance Platonists as the secret key to magical will. *Anankē* (Necessity) is the inflexible spindle of cosmic fate governing the material world; *Dynamis* (spiritual power) is awakened only when the initiate stops looking for easy exits and voluntarily submits to the friction of another soul. By enduring a brother's minor friction without breaking the circle, the initiate transmutes leaden social constraint into sovereign spiritual potency.

## Use when

Managing co-founder disputes, long-term creative partnerships, or moments when you are tempted to scrap a valuable alliance over minor interpersonal friction.

## Why it matters

Train yourself to conquer appetite, laziness, lust, and anger. Never commit a shameful act whether watched by others or completely alone; make your own internal sense of shame and self-respect your highest judge.

The Greek text on p. 114 specifies four somatic-emotional drives the initiate must habituate himself to rule (*kratein*): the belly (*gastros*, rendered "sober"), sleep (*hypnou*, rendered "diligent"), lechery (*lagneiēs*, rendered "chaste"), and boiling rage (*thymou*, "wrath"). In Pythagorean psychology, the soul is a tripartite or bipartite harmony where the rational principle must govern the irrational impulses of the body. If these four leaks are left unsealed, the soul's energy is dissipated into matter. The climax of this section—"above all else respect thyself" (*pantōn de malista aischyneo sauton*, literally "feel shame before yourself most of all")—shifts moral enforcement from external Greek *timē* (public reputation) to an internal panopticon. Because the initiate's own rational soul is a fragment of divine proportion, acting basely in secret violates the sacred witness inside one's own breast.

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In the Hermetic-Cabalistic synthesis of the Renaissance, the four passions listed here were mapped onto the four elemental corruptions of the lower astral body: gluttony (Earth), slothful sleep (Water), lust (Air/humid heat), and wrath (Fire). Until the magus rectifies these four elemental imbalances, his *ochēma* (the subtle star-vehicle of the soul) remains heavy and earthbound. The command to "respect thyself" was read through the lens of the indwelling *daimon* or divine spark (*scintilla*). For Ficino and Agrippa, to sin in secret is to pollute the temple in which the tutelary spirit resides; true self-reverence is not egoic pride, but awe before the immortal divine image stamped upon the human *mens*.

## Use when

Building personal integrity protocols, combating private vices or corner-cutting when no one is watching, or training leaders who operate without oversight.

**The Volatility-Mortality Buffer:** Impulsive, unjust decisions almost always stem from overvaluing a temporary asset or forgetting your finite timeline. Before making a high-stakes move, run a two-second mental reset: (1) *I am on a fixed clock (mortality)*, and (2) *net worth and status are high-beta variables designed to fluctuate*. This strips away scarcity panic and restores cold calculation.

## Why it matters

Deliberate before speaking or acting, practice justice in word and deed, and keep mortality and the rapid rise-and-fall of material wealth constantly in view.

The Greek text (p. 116) commands the student to practice justice (*dikaiosynēn*) in both deed and word, and never to habituate oneself to acting *alogistōs*—without *logos* (rational calculation or proportion). Why do humans act unjustly or impulsively? Because they suffer from temporal myopia, treating material acquisition as permanent and their biological lifespan as infinite. The Pythagorean corrective pairs two structural reminders: first, death is decreed (*peprōtai*) for all by an invincible law; second, *chrēmata* (wealth/property) moves in a tidal wave—now easily gained, now easily lost. By meditating on the guaranteed termination of the body and the high volatility of external goods, the rational mind breaks its panicked attachment to status and resources, making calm, just deliberation possible.

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In the *prisca theologia* tradition, *dikaiosynē* (Justice) is the ethical manifestation of the number Four (the first square number, $2 \times 2$, returning equal for equal). To act only after reflection is to imitate the Demiurge, who contemplates the archetypal Ideas before speaking the cosmos into existence. Renaissance emblems and Neoplatonic treatises contrasted the Wheel of Fortune (*Rota Fortunae*)—which governs sub-lunary riches and honours—with the immovable center of the philosophical soul. By keeping mortality (*memento mori*) before the mind's eye, the initiate loosens the soul's glue to the corporeal shell while still incarnate, practicing the "philosophical death" praised by Plato and Ficino long before physical death arrives.

## Use when

Cooling down emotional reactivity during financial windfalls, market crashes, career setbacks, or high-pressure negotiations.

## Steal

**The Triage-and-Modify Loop:** When hit by an unchosen systemic shock, split your response into two strict phases: first, **Zero-Indignation Acceptance** (drop the "why me?" outrage immediately, as anger burns cognitive fuel); second, **Surgical Mitigation** (treat the situation like a battlefield medic—fix the 20% of the damage that is still malleable and ignore the rest).

## Why it matters

Accept your allotted share of fated misfortunes without rage, work pragmatically to heal or soften them as much as possible, and trust that the wise are spared the worst ruin.

The facing Greek (p. 116) speaks of sufferings that mortals incur through *daimoniēsi tychais* ("daimonic chances" or cosmic lotteries). The Pythagorean response is neither fatalistic passivity nor raging rebellion (*mēd' aganaktei*—"do not be indignant"). Instead, it prescribes a three-step operational mechanic:
1. **Diagnostic Realism:** "Judge them what they are" (recognize your specific *moira*, your allotted portion of the cosmic distribution).
2. **Equanimous Endurance:** Bear the initial impact gently (*praōs phere*) without adding psychological tantrums to physical pain.
3. **Active Remediation:** "Strive, as much as thou art able, to modify the traits" (Greek *iasthai*—to heal or remedy like a physician).
Finally, the text offers a structural guarantee: fate does not deal a massive share of true evil to the *agathoi* (the good/wise), because a harmonized soul does not experience external blows as internal ruin.

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] Ficino and Pico della Mirandola devoted massive energy to reconciling astral fate (*fatum* / *daimoniē tychē*) with human providence. In their reading of this Pythagorean doctrine, the lower *daimones* administer the mechanical blows of planetary fate upon the physical body and fortune. The uninitiated person, living entirely in the passions, is crushed by these astral transits. The Sage, however, has elevated his centre of gravity into the intellectual sphere (*Mens*), above the reach of sub-lunary *Moira*. He uses natural magic, medicine, and philosophical music to "modify the traits" (*iasthai*—remedy the planetary influxes), while knowing that no astral configuration can penetrate the citadel of a purified soul.

## Use when

Handling black-swan events, chronic illness, sudden regulatory or market shifts, or any crisis caused by forces outside your control.

**Tactical Epistemic Retreat:** Falsehood has a massive, passionate fanbase. State your assessment prudently, then step off the tracks, protect your reserves, and wait for reality to bankrupt the error.

## Why it matters

Falsehood attracts just as many passionate partisans as truth. The wise person judges discourses cautiously, and when collective delusion wins the day, steps back silently and waits it out.

In the Greek original (p. 116), the poet observes that many *logoi* (discourses, arguments, public opinions)—both base (*deiloi*) and noble (*esthloi*)—strike against human ears like projectiles. The initiate must neither be stunned/intimidated by them nor allow himself to be bullied out of his position. When *pseudos* (falsehood/Error) is spoken and captures the crowd, the Greek instructs: *praōs eik'* ("yield gently" or step aside with mildness), which d'Olivet and Redfield expand into the striking tactical image: "If Error triumph, he departs and waits." Historically, the early Pythagorean communities in Magna Graecia faced violent populist uprisings (such as the Cylonian revolt in Croton). The system recognizes that truth cannot be forced into an unready, impassioned mob; when collective mania peaks, frontal debate is suicide. Tactical retreat preserves the seed of *logos* until the fever breaks.

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] This verse became the foundational justification for the **Discipline of the Arcane** (*disciplina arcani*) across the esoteric tradition. From the ancient Pythagorean *akousmatikoi* silence through the Renaissance academies of Florence, initiates held that sacred truth has a dual nature: unveiled to the wise, it illuminates; thrown before the profane "lovers of Error," it provokes frenzy and desecration. When the outer world falls into a cycle of iron-age delusion, the adept does not martyr himself in public polemics; he withdraws into the sanctuary, encodes the teaching in symbol and cipher, and waits for the cosmic season to turn.

## Use when

Navigating corporate groupthink, ideological mob panics, speculative market bubbles, or futile public arguments where data no longer matters.

To "think always for thyself" and "freely choose" is to exercise the divine prerogative of *liberum arbitrium* (free will). When the initiate closes his eyes and ears to the siren song of the multitude ("the example of others"), he withdraws from the realm of *doxa* (opinion/phantasy) into *noēsis* (intellectual vision), seeing future effects coiled inside present causes just as Providence does.

## Why it matters

Never attempt or fake competence in things you have not learned; instead, submit to systematic instruction over time, which unlocks the most pleasant life.

[INTERPRETATION: PRISCA THEOLOGIA / ESOTERIC] In the initiatory reception of the *prisca theologia*, this rule governs the transition from the profane world into the lesser mysteries. "Time and patience" (*la constance, au temps* in d'Olivet's French, p. 119) refer to the slow, Saturnian maturation required for the soul's intellectual eye to adjust to divine light. Only those who confess their ignorance and endure the multi-year silence of the vestibule are granted the keys to the sanctuary.

Auditing your daily energy management, diet, sleep schedule, or recovering from burnout caused by extreme routines.
