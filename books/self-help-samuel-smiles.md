## Steal

"He began by breaking three dozen new earthen pots, the pieces of which he covered with different materials which he had compounded, and then took them to a neighbouring glass-furnace to be baked."

## Why it matters

Palissy could not afford to fire whole pieces for every guess at the white enamel. Shards let him test many compounds in one firing, and the hotter glass furnace gave him "a glimmer of hope."

## Use when

Each full test of an idea is expensive and you are testing one variable at a time.

## Steal

"he noticed that a certain earth containing silica, which was black before calcination, became white after exposure to the heat of a furnace."

## Why it matters

Wedgwood studied chemistry in his spare hours and caught one small change in color. Mixing silica into the local red clay gave him the white body behind English earthenware.

## Use when

You keep seeing a small anomaly in your materials and haven't asked what it would let you make.

"He first studied the art of making the Buckingham or pillow-lace by hand, with the object of effecting the same motions by mechanical means."

## Why it matters

Heathcoat mastered the motions himself before trying to mechanize them. His master called him "undaunted by failures and mistakes," and the result was the bobbin-net machine.

## Use when

You are automating a process you have never done by hand.

## Steal

"he found himself almost unconsciously watching his daughters coming their long hair and drawing it out at full length between their fingers."

"if he could successfully imitate in a machine the process of combing out the longest hair and forcing back the short by reversing the action of the comb, it might serve to extricate him from his difficulty."

## Why it matters

Heilmann had a professionally built model and still couldn't make it work. The answer came from an ordinary motion at home, which he copied into the combing machine. ("coming" is the Gutenberg text's misprint for "combing".)

## Use when

You've been stuck on a mechanism for months and have only looked at other machines.

## Steal

"he saw a tiny spider's net suspended across his path. The idea immediately occurred to him, that a bridge of iron ropes or chains might be constructed in like manner"

"Sir Isambert Brunel took his first lessons in forming the Thames Tunnel from the tiny shipworm"

## Why it matters

Three engineers, three borrowed designs: Brown's suspension bridge from a web, Watt's jointed river pipe from a lobster shell, Brunel's tunneling shield from a boring worm. Each was already studying the problem when he saw the model.

## Use when

You are designing a structure and haven't asked what in nature already solves it.

## Steal

"'I have retouched this part—polished that—softened this feature—brought out that muscle—given some expression to this lip, and more energy to that limb.' 'But these are trifles,' remarked the visitor. 'It may be so,' replied the sculptor, 'but recollect that trifles make perfection, and perfection is no trifle.'"

## Why it matters

The visitor saw no change; Michelangelo listed six. Smiles pairs it with Poussin's answer on his reputation: "Because I have neglected nothing."

## Use when

Someone asks why the last 5 per cent is taking so long.

## Steal

"'I keep the subject continually before me, and wait till the first dawnings open slowly by little and little into a full and clear light.'"

## Why it matters

Asked how he made his discoveries, Newton said "By always thinking unto them." His rest was switching subjects, which kept the main problem in view without forcing it.

## Use when

A hard problem needs sustained attention and you keep starting fresh on it each week.

## Steal

"a pair of Norway rats had taken possession of the whole, and reared a young family among the gnawed bits of paper"

"I felt pleased that I might now make better drawings than before; and, ere a period not exceeding three years had elapsed, my portfolio was again filled."

"He had no draft, and was compelled to rake up from his memory facts, ideas, and expressions, which had been long since dismissed."

## Why it matters

Audubon lost two hundred original drawings. In his own account he was flattened for days, then treated the redo as a chance to improve, and refilled the portfolio in under three years. Smiles pairs it with Carlyle, whose lent manuscript of the first French Revolution volume was burned as waste paper and rewritten from memory.

## Use when

Months of work are gone and you can't face starting again.

Willpower alone failed. Buffon paid for each success, and Joseph earned his pay by ignoring protests and threats, once with a basin of ice water. The precommitment worked where resolutions hadn't.

## Steal

"Lord Bacon left behind him many manuscripts entitled 'Sudden thoughts set down for use.'"

"These note-books afterwards proved, like Richter's 'quarries,' the great storehouse from which he drew his illustrations."

## Why it matters

Bacon, Eldon, Pye Smith and John Hunter all kept running notes, and the habit gave them stock to draw on later. Pye Smith's biographer called him "always at work, always in advance, always accumulating."

## Use when

Good ideas show up when you aren't working and are gone by the time you are.
