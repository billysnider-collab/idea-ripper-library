The Triangle Offense: Using Social Movement Theory to Analyze Russia's Gray Zone Strategy

Alexander V. Simmons · Thesis · 1 rips

Masters thesis — National Defense University (Joint Advanced Warfighting School), 2017. Full text: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_AD1032497

Social Movement Theory's tripartite structure — ideological framing, resource mobilization, political opportunity — gives senior strategists a working model for analyzing gray-zone adversaries: applied to Russia and Ukraine, it identifies the adversary's existing sources of tension, likely vulnerabilities in a target region, and viable countermeasures.

Why it matters: The gray zone is named in the thesis as one of the greatest challenges facing the United States; this gives analysts a compact, systematic lens for spotting where an adversary draws strength and where a target region is soft, before the shooting starts.

To counter an adversary's gray-zone playbook, borrow a model from another field — social-movement analysis makes murky influence campaigns legible as framing, funding, and opportunity.
