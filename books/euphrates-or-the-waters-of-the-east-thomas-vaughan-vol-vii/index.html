Card #c321Synthesis

Element means a state of matter

The word "Element" as used by the learned at the period when "Euphrates" was penned, meant rather a "state" of matter, such as the hot, cold, moist and dry natures; or the solid, liquid, gaseous or ethereal conditions; or the stationary, slow, quick or instantaneous processes of change.

— In our words · Source: Euphrates, or the Waters of the East (Thomas Vaughan (Vol. VII))

[Use when]

When a modern reader imports chemistry into early modern matter-talk.

[Why it matters]

False friend alert — "element" here is phase/process, not periodic-table atom.

Card #c322Synthesis

Seek the first mixture of elements — fresh

This Admonition; if thou would'st know Nature, take heed of Antimonie and the common metalls; seek onely that very first mixture of elements, which Nature makes in the great world; seek it I say, whilst it is fresh and new, and having found it, conceal it.

— In our words · Source: Euphrates, or the Waters of the East (Thomas Vaughan (Vol. VII))

[Use when]

When a hunt is distracted by famous reagents instead of the upstream mixture.

[Why it matters]

Skip the named metals; chase Nature's first mix while fresh; then shut up.

Card #c323Synthesis

Dissolvent not to be sought in metals

But to seek that Dissolvent, or the matter whereof it is made, in Metals, is not only Error but Madness.

— In our words · Source: Euphrates, or the Waters of the East (Thomas Vaughan (Vol. VII))

[Use when]

When a team searches for the unlock inside the locked object.

[Why it matters]

Looking for the solvent inside what it is meant to dissolve is category madness.
