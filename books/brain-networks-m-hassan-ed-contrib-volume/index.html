Brain Networks

M. Hassan (ed./contrib. volume) · Nonfiction · 5 rips

Neuroscience of connectivity — nodes, edges, synapses, resting networks.

BC refers to the intricate network of neural pathways that facilitate communication between different brain regions, essential for cognition, behavior, and perception.

Why it matters: Cognition is framed as inter-region traffic, not a single GPU in the skull.

When someone still talks about "the brain center for X" as a lone island.

Here, two important concepts in the network connectivity of the brain are nodes and edges.

Why it matters: Graph language is not metaphor — it is the measurement frame.

When translating brain talk into network science without mysticism.

The network of neurons builds the foundation of the entire nervous system but there is no specific physical connection between two neurons.

Why it matters: Network without continuous material link — junction, not cable.

When "wired" brains need the synaptic gap restored.

The entire neuronal network is connected by special junctions called "Synapses" and they can be both electrical and chemical, passing the signals throughout the body.

Why it matters: Two transmission modes in one junction type — speed and modulation share an address.

When a system needs both fast sync and slow chemistry at the same interface.

The DMN is a network of brain regions that is more active at rest than during task performance [20].

Why it matters: Default is not idle — rest has its own active coalition.

When "doing nothing" is treated as neural silence.
