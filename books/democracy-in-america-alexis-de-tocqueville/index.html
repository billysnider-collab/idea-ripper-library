Democracy in America

by Alexis de Tocqueville

9 cards

Democratic character — Tocqueville's 1835-40 study of equality's effects: associations, the tyranny of the majority, and soft despotism.

Card #c164Synthesis

Equality, the providential fact

The gradual development of the equality of conditions is therefore a providential fact, and it possesses all the characteristics of a divine decree: it is universal, it is durable, it constantly eludes all human interference, and all events as well as all men contribute to its progress.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When democratic trends are treated as reversible policy choices.

[Why it matters]

The book’s generative claim: equality of conditions is a divine-decree-level force no generation can check.

Card #c165Synthesis

The fundamental fact

The more I advanced in the study of American society, the more I perceived that the equality of conditions is the fundamental fact from which all others seem to be derived, and the central point at which all my observations constantly terminated.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When drowning in symptoms and you need the root.

[Why it matters]

Tocqueville’s method in one line: find the single fact from which all others derive.

Card #c166Synthesis

Association, the mother science

In democratic countries the science of association is the mother of science; the progress of all the rest depends upon the progress it has made.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When collective action fails and the proposed fix is a hero.

[Why it matters]

In democracies the art of associating is the master skill on which civilization depends.

Card #c167Synthesis

Individualism, defined

Individualism is a mature and calm feeling, which disposes each member of the community to sever himself from the mass of his fellow-creatures; and to draw apart with his family and his friends; so that, after he has thus formed a little circle of his own, he willingly leaves society at large to itself.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When withdrawal gets mislabeled as selfishness.

[Why it matters]

The distinction that runs the book: individualism is a calm error of judgment, egotism a blind passion. Tocqueville coined the word for the new disease.

Card #c168Synthesis

Association against the majority

At the present time the liberty of association is become a necessary guarantee against the tyranny of the majority.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When a majority starts closing ranks.

[Why it matters]

The institutional answer to majority tyranny: not rights on paper but the organized habit of combining.

Card #c169Synthesis

Soft despotism

Above this race of men stands an immense and tutelary power, which takes upon itself alone to secure their gratifications, and to watch over their fate. That power is absolute, minute, regular, provident, and mild. It would be like the authority of a parent, if, like that authority, its object was to prepare men for manhood; but it seeks on the contrary to keep them in perpetual childhood

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When comfort is being traded for agency.

[Why it matters]

The famous prophecy: a mild, absolute, minute power that keeps men in perpetual childhood and spares them the trouble of living.

Card #c170Synthesis

The apprenticeship of liberty

It cannot be repeated too often that nothing is more fertile in prodigies than the art of being free; but there is nothing more arduous than the apprenticeship of liberty.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When liberty looks like too much trouble.

[Why it matters]

Freedom is fertile but its apprenticeship is arduous — despotism’s temporary prosperity is the trap.

Card #c171Verbatim excerpt

The sad Americans

In America I saw the freest and most enlightened men, placed in the happiest circumstances which the world affords: it seemed to me as if a cloud habitually hung upon their brow, and I thought them serious and almost sad even in their pleasures.

— Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When success does not feel like it.

[Why it matters]

The paradox of prosperity: the freest men brooding over advantages they do not possess, feverishly pursuing welfare.

The press, last bulwark

but that servitude cannot be complete if the press is free: the press is the chiefest democratic instrument of freedom.

— In our words · Source: Democracy in America (Alexis de Tocqueville)

[Use when]

When press freedom is treated as one liberty among many.

[Why it matters]

Parliaments and popular sovereignty can coexist with servitude; a free press cannot.
