## Steal

A bullshit job is defined not by whether it's useful to the employer, but by whether the person DOING it believes it serves no purpose: 'so completely pointless, unnecessary, or pernicious that even the employee cannot justify its existence.' The worker's own honest verdict is the diagnostic, not the org chart.

## Why it matters

The master test. It locates the diagnosis in the worker's own judgment, not the employer's — which is why it catches jobs that look productive from outside but feel pointless from inside. If the person doing it cannot convince themselves there is a good reason for it, it's bullshit regardless of pay or how busy it looks. (The telling example: a German soldier moving computers two rooms down could just carry it, but the job is outsourced through three subcontractors and fifteen pages of paperwork.)

## Use when

Use to audit any job — yours or someone else's. Ask the single question: could the person doing it, if honest, justify its existence? If not, it's bullshit. Use this as the umbrella before slotting a job into the five categories below.

## Steal

A flunky job exists 'only or primarily to make someone else look or feel important.' The role's real product is status — the uselessness of the uniformed retainers IS the testimony to the boss's greatness. Modern form: the receptionist with nothing to do, the cold caller hired to make a broker look busy.

## Why it matters

Recognize it in the wild: the job has no real function, but eliminating it would make someone look LESS important. Receptionists are a 'Badge of Seriousness'; other flunkies are 'Badges of Importance.' The tell is that the work is trivial (wind a clock, fill a candy dish) or pure theater (stand by the door looking regal) — the person is there to be a prop for someone else's status.

## Use when

Use to audit your own role: is there anything I do that would still need doing if it made no one look important? Use when restructuring an org chart — a flunky is an unnecessary subordinate (the opposite pole from a taskmaster); cut the role, not the person, if the role is only status.

## Steal

A goon is someone whose job 'has an aggressive element, but, crucially, who exists only because other people employ them.' Armies, lobbyists, PR specialists, telemarketers, corporate lawyers: each side's goons exist only because the other side has goons. Remove one and you could remove them all.

## Why it matters

Recognize it in the wild: an arms race of persuasion or force. The tell is that the job is (1) aggressive/manipulative and (2) only needed because the opponent has the same thing. 'Countries need armies only because other countries have armies.' The holder often feels the job is 'essentially manipulative and aggressive' and has no social value — that self-report is the diagnostic.

## Use when

Use to audit: does this role serve customers/users, or out-goon the other side's goons? Use when a 'defensive' hire (lawyer, PR, lobbyist) is proposed — the difference between real defense and matching the other side's arms race is what determines whether it's bullshit.

## Steal

A duct taper is an employee 'whose job exists only because of a glitch or fault in the organization; who is there to solve a problem that ought not to exist.' The work is real, but it's real because something upstream is broken — often cleaning up after a sloppy or incompetent superior.

## Why it matters

Recognize it in the wild: the work is genuinely necessary but the need is artificial — a patch on a glitch, not a function. The tell is resentment: 'cleaning up after someone who makes a completely gratuitous and unnecessary mess is always irritating.' Duct tapers are usually angry because their whole job is undoing someone else's damage. Most obvious form: 'underlings whose jobs are to undo the damage done by sloppy or incompetent superiors.'

## Use when

Use to audit: is the problem I'm solving caused by a defect in the system (then fix the system) or inherent to the work (then fix the work)? A duct-taper role is a signal the real fix is upstream — don't staff the patch, fix the glitch.

## Steal

A box ticker exists 'only or primarily to allow an organization to be able to claim it is doing something that, in fact, it is not doing.' The role's product is the appearance of action — a fact-finding commission, a compliance officer, a 'leisure coordinator' who exists so the org can say it offers leisure.

## Why it matters

Recognize it in the wild: the job's deliverable is documentation, not outcome. The tell is that the work produces evidence-of-doing rather than doing — the box gets ticked, the thing the box claims to track never changes. It's the government or corporation telling the public (or itself) 'we're on it' when nobody actually is.

## Use when

Use to audit: does this role change the underlying thing, or just certify that the thing was supposedly done? Use when an org says it 'does' something (compliance, wellbeing, quality) — look for the box ticker whose output is the certificate, not the change.

## Steal

Taskmasters split into two types. Type 1: 'those whose role consists entirely of assigning work to others' — unnecessary superiors, the exact opposite of flunkies. Type 2: those 'whose primary role is to create bullshit tasks for others to do, to supervise bullshit, or even to create entirely new bullshit jobs' — the bullshit generators.

## Why it matters

Recognize it in the wild: a manager who contributes nothing but the assignment of work (Type 1), or one who manufactures busywork to justify their own existence (Type 2). The tell for Type 1 is 'if I weren't here, my underlings would be fine'; for Type 2 it's that the tasks they create have no purpose except to keep people (and themselves) looking occupied.

## Use when

Use to audit management layers: is this layer adding decisions, or just adding work? A Type 1 taskmaster can be removed (the team runs itself); a Type 2 taskmaster is actively harmful — cutting them removes not just their job but the bullshit they generate for everyone else.
