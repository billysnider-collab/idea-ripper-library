argument #276

Litmus test: what he cannot do, none of us can

The hero's failure sets the human ceiling — his defeat is our shared limit.

He is a litmus test for us all: what he cannot do, none of us can hope to, and this makes his failure to become immortal all the more poignant.

Why it matters

The hero's failure sets the human ceiling — his defeat is our shared limit.

mechanism #278

The story is the immortality

Gilgamesh quests for literal immortality and loses the plant to a snake; he returns to Uruk and the epic's last gesture points at the walls.

Through stories, the teller can achieve the next best thing to immortality: eternal life in literature.

Why it matters

Gilgamesh quests for literal immortality and loses the plant to a snake; he returns to Uruk and the epic's last gesture points at the walls. Denied eternal life, he gets eternal literature — the oldest surviving epic is about a man who fails the quest and keeps the story.

Use when: Use when the quest fails but the record outlives it.
