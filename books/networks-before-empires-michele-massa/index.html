Networks before Empires

Long-distance exchange networks in Early Bronze Age Anatolia (c. 3200–1950 BC) ran on organized systems — specialized production, exchange intermediaries, route control, and rising elites leveraging the region's metal wealth — centuries before any empire managed them.

— In our words · Source: Networks before Empires (Michele Massa)

[Use when]

Infrastructure precedes institutions — exchange networks with intermediaries, route control, and standards emerged before the states that later claimed to run them.

[Why it matters]

Trade networks don't wait for empires — complex exchange systems with middlemen and route control predate state power by millennia, a pattern that repeats in every shadow economy since.
