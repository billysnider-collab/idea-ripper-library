mechanism #343

Secret shape of time: repeating lines

History rhymes structurally, not coincidentally — the pattern is the argument for a shape to time.

These (and other) parallels between the story of Julius Caesar and the story of an Irish conspirator induce Ryan to imagine some secret shape of time, a pattern of repeating lines.

Why it matters

History rhymes structurally, not coincidentally — the pattern is the argument for a shape to time.

Use when: Use when two distant stories rhyme hard enough to imply a pattern, not coincidence.

mechanism #344

Pity trained on novels spills onto the self

Empathy practice has a rebound effect: trained feeling for fictional sufferers becomes self-pity.

From learning to pity the misfortunes of the heroes of our novels, we wind up feeling too much pity for our own.

Why it matters

Empathy practice has a rebound effect: trained feeling for fictional sufferers becomes self-pity.

Use when: Use when reading empathy becomes self-pity by transfer.

concept #345

Unreal in life; not even a ghost in death

A life of performed unreality cannot even produce a convincing ghost — the afterlife inherits the deficit.

In life, Ashe was afflicted with unreality, as so many Englishmen are; in death, he is not even the ghost he was in life.

Why it matters

A life of performed unreality cannot even produce a convincing ghost — the afterlife inherits the deficit.

Use when: Use when an English understatement of self needs a colder afterlife version.

contradiction #347

Nostalgia for the unwritten book; fear the specter stays

Abandoned work leaves compound interest: grief for what was not written plus haunting by what was started.

Paradoxically, I felt both a nostalgie for the book I should have written, and now never would, and a fear that the guest, the specter, would never abandon me.

Why it matters

Abandoned work leaves compound interest: grief for what was not written plus haunting by what was started.

Use when: Use when abandoning a project leaves both grief and haunting.
