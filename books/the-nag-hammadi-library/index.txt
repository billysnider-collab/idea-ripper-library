6:T51b,Vodolazkin opens Laurus by refusing the premise of a single self: the hero 'had four names,' and the parts of a life 'have little in common with one another' — 'so little that it can seem as though they were lived by different people.' That is the definition the whole novel runs on. You are not one continuous person; you are a succession of selves, and the name is the fiction that pretends otherwise. Seen this way, changing who you are is not a betrayal of identity — it is the normal shape of identity.

Ripped from the novel (translated from the Russian by the cutter): «В разное время у него было четыре имени. В этом можно усматривать преимущество, поскольку жизнь человека неоднородна. In this one can see an advantage, since a human life is not homogeneous. It sometimes happens that its parts have little in common with one another.
