## Steal

Unpredictable weather scarcely affected life, and the forces of nature could be held in check, so the gods were usually compliant and democratic, taking their decisions by discussion in an assembly.

## Why it matters

Theology tracks ecology — stable nature yields committee gods.

Use when divine politics need an environmental premise.

## Steal

The story of the Flood was one of the most popular tales of ancient times, and is found in several ancient languages, reworked to suit different areas and cultures so that different settings and details are found in each version.

## Why it matters

Popularity produces variants — the core travels; the costume changes.

## Use when

Use when someone insists there is one "original" flood narrative.

## Steal

Sages who were sent before the Flood by the god Ea to bring civilization to mankind, and authors after the Flood were honoured with sage-like status.

## Why it matters

Culture is a pre-disaster gift; later authors inherit sage status by echo.

## Use when

Use when a tradition claims civilizing knowledge arrived as mission, not evolution.

## Steal

A later version is not necessarily fuller than an earlier one: for example, the latest version of Ishtar's Descent from Nineveh is slightly briefer than the earlier version from Assur, and less than half the length of the Sumerian story, which is even earlier.

## Why it matters

Transmission can compress — newer is not richer by default.

## Use when

Use when "latest edition" is assumed to be the most complete.
