Dungeon Crawler Carl

Matt Dinniman · Science Fiction · 6 rips

Earth turned into a televised dungeon crawl run by alien corporations.

While we love and admire your creativity, any unintentional exploits will be patched, so if you find something that has an unintended feature, don’t get too comfortable using it.

Why it matters: Creativity is applauded until it breaks the economy of the game; the house always rewrites the rules mid-run.

Use when a platform celebrates creators while reserving the right to kill their edge.

They’re a corporate system government, and they’re the ones who currently have an entire fleet parked outside of the Borant system, ready to collect on the debt.

Why it matters: Sovereignty is a balance sheet — the fleet arrives not for justice but for collection.

Use when geopolitics talk needs the debt-enforcement frame.

You will never find a pulse rifle or automated power armor in any sort of box in the world dungeon, but it’s possible to get one from a patron.

Why it matters: Power items are gated by patronage, not grind: the economy separates RNG candy from sponsored lethality.

Use when designing (or critiquing) progression systems that hide pay-to-win as lore.

This is a rendition of a Kua-Tin, the dominant species of the Borant System and principal owners of the Borant Corporation.

Why it matters: The 'alien race' is also a shareholder class — species identity and corporate ownership collapse into one logo.

Use when a monster manual needs the boardroom behind the species.

Featured crawlers always gained billions of views and millions of followers right out the gate.

Why it matters: Visibility is allocated, not earned — featured status is the real first loot drop.

Use when attention economies pretend merit while running a featured queue.

“This whole translation system is quite elegant, I must admit.”

Why it matters: The interface is the control surface: polish replaces bars, and nobody riots against good UX.

Use when: a polished interface is the trap — elegance as soft control.
