Balance of Risk in the South China Sea

China's risk-acceptant moves in the South China Sea follow balance-of-risk logic: after the 2009 UNCLOS CLCS deadline pressure, Beijing anchored its reference point on the nine-dashed line claim, and leaders facing potential losses to that reference point choose risky strategies rather than accept the loss.

— In our words · Source: Balance of Risk in the South China Sea (Adam M. McCann)

[Use when]

Find the reference point — once you know what a player treats as the status quo, you can predict when they'll gamble to defend it.

[Why it matters]

It predicts when Beijing escalates — any internationally forced pressure on the nine-dashed line puts China in the 'domain of losses', where the theory says risk-acceptant behavior follows. That makes the framework a live forecasting tool for SCS crises.
