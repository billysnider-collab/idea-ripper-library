An Analysis of the Evolution of Government Patent Policy in Research and Development Contracts

Decades of post-WWII debate over who owns inventions from federally funded R&D produced no uniform government patent policy; the 1980 patent and trademark amendments were a compromise between the "title" school (government keeps ownership) and the "license" school (contractors keep it, government takes a license), after executive, legislative, and judicial efforts all failed to unify the rules.

— In our words · Source: An Analysis of the Evolution of Government Patent Policy in Research and Development Contracts (Patrick James Flanagan)

[Use when]

When no one owns the rules, a workable compromise beats another decade of debate — the 1980 amendments ended 35 years of stalemate by splitting the difference.

[Why it matters]

The 1980 compromise (the Bayh-Dole era) is the legal bedrock under today's fights over who owns AI, semiconductor, and biotech breakthroughs paid for with public money.
