Be a good son, just brother, spouse tender and good father (4) Choose for thy friend, the friend of virtue; Yield to his gentle counsels, profit by his life, And for a trifling grievance never leave him (5); If thou canst at least: for a most rigid law Binds Power to Necessity (6). — *The Golden Verses of Pythagoras*, Purification, trans. Nayan Louise Redfield (1917), pp. 113, 115. **Surface Gloss:** Select friends solely on the basis of their virtue, learn from their example, and do not sever the bond over minor faults—pushing your tolerance to the limit, because human capability is forged only under the pressure of necessity. **The Constraint-Capacity Coupling:** Human capability (*Dynamis*) lives right next door to non-negotiable constraint (*Anankē*). As long as you treat a relationship, project, or discipline as optional—something you can abandon over a "trifling grievance"—your capacity remains dormant. Lock the exit door by treating the commitment as Necessity, and the power required to solve the friction will materialize.

— In our words · Source: The Golden Verses of Pythagoras

[Use when]

Managing co-founder disputes, long-term creative partnerships, or moments when you are tempted to scrap a valuable alliance over minor interpersonal friction. ---

Card #c1566Synthesis

“1. “That which is happy and imperishable, neither has trouble itself, nor does it cause it to anything; so that it is not subject to the feelings of either anger or gratitude; for these feelings only exist in what is weak. [...] 2. “Death is nothing to us; for that which is dissolved is devoid of sensation, and that which is devoid of sensation is nothing to us. 3. “The limit of the greatness of the pleasures is the removal of everything which can give pain. And where pleasure is, as long as it lasts, that which gives pain, or that which feels pain, or both of them, are absent. 4. “Pain does not abide continuously in the flesh, but in its extremity it is present only a very short time. That pain which only just exceeds the pleasure in the flesh, does not last many days. But long diseases have in them more that is pleasant than painful to the flesh.” — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book X, Sec. XXXI, Maxims 1–4, trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Perfected beings neither feel anger nor grant favors; death is non-experience; maximum pleasure is simply the complete absence of pain; and bodily pain is either brief in its extremity or mild in its duration. The **Zero-State Ceiling**: most human misery comes from treating satisfaction as an open-ended vertical ladder when it is actually a binary pressure-valve. Once structural deficit (pain, hunger, acute threat) is removed, you are already at 100% baseline capacity. Everything beyond that zero-deficit mark is merely horizontal flavor-swapping ("diversification"), not an increase in well-being. Stop paying exponential effort costs for horizontal variety under the delusion that it raises your vertical ceiling.

— In our words · Source: Lives of Eminent Philosophers (Diogenes Laertius)

[Use when]

Auditing a feature roadmap, personal lifestyle creep, or an optimization loop where escalating expenditure is only buying novelty rather than solving an actual deficit. ---

: PERFECTION Let not sleep e'er close thy tired eyes Without thou ask thyself: What have I omitted and what done ? (22). Abstain thou if 'tis evil; persevere if good (23). Nayan Louise Redfield (1917), p. 119. **Surface Gloss:** Never go to sleep before reviewing every action of your day in sequence: asking where you transgressed, what you accomplished, and what duty you left undone, rebuking yourself for errors and rejoicing in the good. **The Pre-Sleep Triple Replay:** Never let your brain compile the day's code into long-term memory while dirty. Before sleep, replay the day chronologically from morning to night and run the three-part query: (1) *Where did I cross a line?* (2) *What did I actually ship?* (3) *What required duty did I drop?* Feel the sting of the errors, savour the wins, and wake up recalibrated.

— In our words · Source: The Golden Verses of Pythagoras

[Use when]

Building a high-performance evening shutdown routine, improving episodic memory, or closing the gap between your stated values and daily execution. ---

Card #c1569Synthesis

“33. “Natural justice is a covenant of what is suitable, leading men to avoid injuring one another, and being injured. [...] 35. “Justice has no independent existence; it results from mutual contracts, and establishes itself wherever there is a mutual engagement to guard against doing or sustaining mutual injury. 36. “Injustice is not intrinsically bad; it has this character only because there is joined with it a fear of not escaping those who are appointed to punish actions marked with that character. 37. “It is not possible for a man who secretly does anything in contravention of the agreement which men have made with one another, to guard against doing, or sustaining mutual injury, to believe that he shall always escape notice, even if he have escaped notice already ten thousand times; for, till his death, it is uncertain whether he will not be detected. [...] 41. “[...] when in consequence of new circumstances, a thing which has been pronounced just does not any longer appear to agree with utility, the thing which was just, inasmuch as it was useful to the social relations and intercourse of mankind, ceases to be just the moment when it ceases to be useful.” — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book X, Sec. XXXI, Maxims 33, 35–37, 41, trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Justice is not an eternal entity; it is a local non-aggression treaty. Breaking it in secret is foolish only because lifelong fear of exposure ruins peace of mind, and any law stops being just the exact moment it stops being useful. Two linked mechanisms: 1. **The Sunset Clause of Rules (Maxim 41):** Never defend a legacy policy, process, or norm by claiming it was "always wrong" or "always right." Acknowledge that it *was* valid when it served mutual utility, and declare it dead the exact second environmental shifts erase that utility. 2. **The Gyges Paranoia Tax (Maxim 37):** Never take a covert shortcut whose value depends on permanent non-detection. Even a 99.9% chance of staying hidden charges an ongoing cognitive tax ("will today be the day?") that costs more than the illicit gain.

— In our words · Source: Lives of Eminent Philosophers (Diogenes Laertius)

[Use when]

Retiring sacred-cow institutional rules that have outlived their environment, or evaluating the hidden psychological carrying cost of a grey-area operational shortcut. ---

Card #c1568Synthesis

“30. “Of the desires, some are natural and necessary, some natural, but not necessary, and some are neither natural nor necessary, but owe their existence to vain opinions.” (Epicurus thinks that those are natural and necessary which put an end to pains, as drink when one is thirsty; and that those are natural but not necessary which only diversify pleasure, but do not remove pain, such as expensive food; and that these are neither natural nor necessary, which are such as crowns, or the erection of statues.) 31. “Those desires which do not lead to pain, if they are not satisfied, are not necessary. It is easy to impose silence on them when they appear difficult to gratify, or likely to produce injury. 32. “When the natural desires, the failing to satisfy which is, nevertheless, not painful, are violent and obstinate, it is a proof that there is an admixture of vain opinion in them; for then energy does not arise from their own nature, but from the vain opinions of men.” — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book X, Sec. XXXI, Maxims 30–32, trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Desires split into three classes: natural-necessary (ending pain), natural-unnecessary (merely varying pleasure), and unnatural-unnecessary (status symbols). When a non-painful natural desire feels violent and obsessive, its heat comes from corrupted social opinion, not nature. The **Obsession Diagnostic**: how do you tell whether an intense craving (for a specific hire, a luxury acquisition, a romantic target, or a market win) is a real structural need or an ego-hallucination? Run the two-step Epicurean test: First, if I walk away right now, does it cause actual structural damage ("pain"), or just a missed variation? Second, if it causes no structural damage yet my craving feels "violent and obstinate," the energy driving me is 100% synthetic narrative ("vain opinion") — not the thing itself.

— In our words · Source: Lives of Eminent Philosophers (Diogenes Laertius)

[Use when]

De-escalating negotiation fever, buyer's mania, or personal fixation when an optional target starts feeling like a life-or-death necessity. ---

Card #c1565Synthesis

And we must consider that some of the passions are natural, and some empty; and of the natural ones some are necessary, and some merely natural. And of the necessary ones some are necessary to happiness, and others, with regard to the exemption of the body, from trouble; and others with respect to living itself; for a correct theory, with regard to these things, can refer all choice and avoidance to the health of the body and the freedom from disquietude of the soul. Since this is the end of living happily; for it is for the sake of this that we do everything, wishing to avoid grief and fear; and when once this is the case, with respect to us, then the storm of the soul is, as I may say, put an end to... When, therefore, we say that pleasure is a chief good, we are not speaking of the pleasures of the debauched man, or those which lie in sensual enjoyment, as some think who are ignorant, and who do not entertain our opinions, or else interpret them perversely; but we mean the freedom of the body from pain, and of the soul from confusion. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book X, Section XXVII (Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus*), trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Writing to Menoeceus, Epicurus classifies desires into natural-necessary, merely natural, and empty, defining supreme pleasure not as sensual indulgence, but as the zero-state where bodily pain and mental turmoil are completely removed. **The Katastematic Ceiling & Desire Audit:** Run every goal, purchase, or ambition through Epicurus's three-bucket filter: 1. Does failing to get this cause actual physical or existential pain? (*Natural + Necessary* — secure immediately at lowest cost). 2. Does it merely vary the flavor of an already pain-free state without raising the ceiling? (*Natural + Non-Necessary* — enjoy when free, drop instantly when expensive). 3. Is its intensity driven entirely by status-narrative and "vain opinion"? (*Empty* — kill on sight). Remember the ceiling law: once friction and want are at zero, you are already at maximum altitude; expensive additions only change the wallpaper.

— In our words · Source: Lives of Eminent Philosophers (Diogenes Laertius)

[Use when]

Use when auditing your personal burn rate, stripping scope creep from a product roadmap, or breaking the illusion that the next tier of luxury or status will increase your baseline well-being. ---

Card #c1564Synthesis

We cannot act in the same way with respect to the heavenly phænomena: these productions may depend upon several different causes, and we may give many different explanations on this subject, equally agreeing with the impressions of the senses. Besides, it is not here a question about reasoning on new principles, and of laying down, _à priori_, rules for the interpretation of nature; the only guides for us to follow are the appearances themselves; for that which we have in view is not a set of systems and vain opinions, but much rather a life exempt from every kind of disquietude. ... The eclipses of the sun and moon may depend either on the fact that these stars extinguish themselves, a phænomenon which we often see produced under our eyes, or on the fact of other bodies, the earth, the heaven, or something else of the same kind interposing, between them and us. ... As to those who on this point admit only one explanation as possible, they put themselves in opposition to facts, and lose sight of the bounds set to human knowledge. — Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book X, Section XXV (Epicurus, *Letter to Pythocles*), trans. C. D. Yonge (1853). **Surface Gloss:** Writing to Pythocles, Epicurus rules that while fundamental physics has single necessary truths, distant sky-events must be explained by multiple simultaneous hypotheses drawn from everyday analogies; insisting on a single cause where we cannot get close enough to verify is vain dogmatism that breeds anxiety. **The Anti-Mythology Portfolio (Pleonachos Tropos):** When an opaque, distant event occurs (a competitor’s sudden pivot, a market crash, a senior executive's silence) where you cannot get close-range verification, the human brain defaults to *monachos tropos*: locking onto a single dramatic explanation (usually attributing godlike intent or malice to the actor) and panicking over it. Break the spell by forcing yourself to list **four distinct, mundane, mechanical explanations** drawn from everyday analogies that all fit the visible facts, and hold all four open at equal weight. The moment you refuse to crown a single story, the superstitious anxiety evaporates.

— In our words · Source: Lives of Eminent Philosophers (Diogenes Laertius)

[Use when]

Use when you or your organization are spiraling into paranoia over an unverifiable external event, a competitor's opaque move, or an ambiguous signal from a distant decision-maker. ---

Card #c1434SynthesisThesis

Treating words as opaque symbols is the wrong assumption for morphologically complex languages. Encoding morphology into three model types — a Bayesian compound-decomposition n-gram model, a distributed model learning vector representations of morphemes, and an unsupervised model that discovers even discontiguous (Semitic-style) morphemes — improves model performance and beats the data sparsity that morphological processes create, with benefits for machine translation.

— In our words · Source: Probabilistic Modelling of Morphologically Rich Languages (Jan A. Botha)

[Use when]

Don't model the surface — learn the substructure; the regularities that fix sparsity live inside the unit everyone treats as atomic.

Card #c1412SynthesisThesis

In metagenomic read classification, spaced seeds (k-mers with wildcard positions) beat traditional contiguous k-mers — but with an explicit operating envelope: the advantage holds below ~0.9 sequence identity; for very high-quality alignments (identity ~0.95+) contiguous k-mers with hit-number scoring win. Jaccard-index scoring amplifies the spaced-seed advantage for small-weight seeds.

— In our words · Source: Spaced seeds improve k-mer-based metagenomic classification (Karel Brinda)

[Use when]

Every method has an operating envelope — spaced seeds win at low identity, contiguous k-mers at high identity; the honest answer is a switch, not a champion.
